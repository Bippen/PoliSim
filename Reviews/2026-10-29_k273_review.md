## Hunt answers

1. **Harness faithfulness.** The harness shares `Prepare`, `Evaluate`, `Payoff`, `SupportTerm` and `JointAlike`. It **duplicates** Form's defection loop, `WouldHold` (private in the runtime) and the BestOwn rebuild. The IDENTICAL test checks only the top government's cabinet, support and score, and only at the built fraction. That is the one point where the BaseScore-shift trick is not applied. It could pass while Form's Viable list, ordering or Outcome differed (L1). My base-vs-branch dump closes the gap for this change.
2. **BaseScore vs Score.** `BaseScore` is read only in `Prepare` (to build `Score`) and in `Evaluate` (base + the proposal's own or derived SupportTerm). Every other path goes through `Prepare`/`Evaluate`/`Form`: the formateur and `FormationProposal.Answer` (FormationProposal.cs:102, 133, 241), the speaker order and `DraftProposal` (SimulationManager.cs:4783, 4842-4845), `GovernmentFormation` (GovernmentFormation.cs:536), and the Sejm, German and Italian paths and the AI's in-run elections (all through `Form`). No caller outside CoalitionFormation reads `BaseScore`, `Score` or `BestOwn`, except the F7 line and the harness. `ConfidenceProcedure` reads `DefectionMargin` only. Nothing is stale.
3. **Loop.** None. `SupportersOf` (CoalitionFormation.cs:813-858) reads lines, compatibility, Banzhaf power and the masks, never a score. `Score` (489) is a pure function of the cabinet's own derived supporters. `BestOwn` (503-514) is built after `Score` and read only by `Evaluate`. The comment at 452-456 is accurate.
4. **Determinism and saves.** Pure arithmetic, no RNG, no dictionary order. For a derived cabinet, `Evaluate`'s score (570) is the same expression on the same operands as `Prepare`'s (489), so it is bit-identical. `FormationSweepDiagnostic`'s `back.Score.Equals(...)` relies on this. No formation score, Chamber or GovernmentOption is saved (grep), so there is no format bump. A loaded game forms its next government under the new score.
5. **The prereg.** The grid's exclusion of 0 rests on the ruling's words: "counts partly", against K267's "support not scored". The exclusion of 1 rests on "at a fraction of a member's weight" and "partly". Both are fair. The midpoint rule is acceptable as a convention, but its stated reason is weaker than it reads (L2). The disclosure that the rules followed the reproduction is honest. The rules are the obvious ones and do not look fitted.
6. **F7.** Honest. The condition (`f7`, PolishDeclarationsDiagnostic.cs:336) is unchanged and lifts itself when the game's cabinet equals KO+TD+NL. The printed figures switch to `Score` and are right: KO+TD+NL has no supporter, so its score is independent of f, while KO+TD's rises with f. At the played count the harness gives 65.666 against 68.154, matching the prereg's expected line. Structurally, raising f can only widen the gap, so "no fraction lifts Poland" is true on these chambers. M1 shows it is also slightly less likely to lift in play.
7. **Pins.** `FormationSweepDiagnostic`'s digest **will move**. Every row with a supporter prints `score {R}` (FormationSweepDiagnostic.cs:135), and the branch does not re-pin (M2). Its API assertion (44-45) is **not** vacuous. It now also proves that a proposal naming exactly the derived supporters scores bit-identically to the formation, which is stronger than before. No other Editor check asserts a formation score. CoalitionHarness:160, Formation2026Diagnostic:109 and PolishDeclarationsDiagnostic:235 only print one.
8. **CRLF, .csproj, comments.** The index is LF for all five files. Working copies: the Assets files are CRLF under `core.autocrlf=true` and the Tools files are LF, which matches the repository (every tracked file is i/lf). `K273SupportSweep.csproj` is matched by `.gitignore:39:*.csproj` and force-added. ## Findings

**H1 (High): in play, the support term seats a Polish minority on its two arch-rivals' support, which is not plausible. It moves the century: Poland's debt is +12 % at t100, with trade ripples to Sweden and the USA.**
- Where: CoalitionFormation.cs:303-304 (`SupportTerm`), 489, 570. The enabling rules are older: SupportersOf condition 2 (CoalitionFormation.cs:846-847) and the defection rounds (352-363).
- Seed 777's 2055 election installs "TD led by TD with PiS+KO" where the base formed TD+KO+NL. Three things combine:
  1. **The term is unbounded.** Supporters count by all their seats, not by the seats the cabinet needs. TD (117) needs 114 more. PiS alone gives 218, and KO's 91 is pure surplus, yet both add: 0.09 × 309/460 × 100 = **+6.05 points**. TD on PiS+KO goes from 60.964 to 67.009, past KO+TD+NL (64.310, which has no supporter and does not move). The largest party propping up a small one from outside is exactly the case the term rewards most.
  2. **PiS and KO can share support.** Every PiS–KO line in the 2055 reading is one-way (`PiS-KO(1w)(bs)`, `KO-PiS(1w)(bs)`). By K-1, a one-way line "refuses the other party's CABINET, never its company among the supporters", so condition 2 never separates them.
  3. **The defection rounds are abandoned in Poland.** KO does better in KO+TD+NL than as TD's supporter, and TD does better alone (payoff = its full cohesion) than in any coalition. So every viable option has a defector, `kept.Count == 0`, and the rounds stop. All six options stand, and the score alone decides. The base's 2051 "Konf led by Konf with PiS" (an 11-seat cabinet) is the same pre-existing pathology. K273 hands it more weight.
- Plausibility: none. PiS and KO refuse each other's cabinets and are each other's main opponent. Under Art. 154 ust. 2 both would have to vote confidence in a TD prime minister with no portfolio for either. No Polish government has formed this way.
- Failure scenario: any AI Polish chamber where a small centre party sits between PiS and KO (the TD-117 shape seed 777 produces by the 2050s) installs this government. Its book then drives Poland's debt path and trade for decades.
- The prereg's acceptance could not catch it: it measures four chambers of record, not in-run chambers. Its "no government of record or seed moves" bar passed.
- A narrower form exists, but choosing it is Elias's ruling, not a re-fit: count supporters only up to the seats the cabinet needs for a majority. Checked by hand on this chamber: TD on PiS+KO becomes about 63.19, below KO+TD on NL at about 64.42 and KO+TD+NL at 64.310, so KO+TD on NL forms. Sweden 2022 is untouched (SD's 73 are needed in full: M+KD+L 103 + 73 = 176 against a majority of 175).

**H2 (High): a record chamber's government moves in-engine, which by the prereg's own last clause holds K273 for return.**
- Where: FormationSweepDiagnostic rows "Sweden | 2022 dated 2026-01-30 with/without rules | negative True".
- Without rules, the 2022 chamber under the 2026-01-30 declarations formed the government of record (M+KD+L on SD) and now forms S on MP.
- With rules, the game's actual mid-term reading for a Swedish formation between 30 Jan 2026 and the election goes from S on MP to **M alone on KD's support**. Neither is the record. The new one has KD supporting from outside for no portfolio while every option has a defector. That is H1's mechanism again: a one-party cabinet's full cohesion plus a discounted supporter beats sitting together.
- The prereg tested Sweden only under `ElectionVintage.Sweden2022`'s declarations, never on the dated timeline the game reads mid-term. So its acceptance passed while the sweep moved.
- The prereg says: "If any government of record moves in-engine, K273 is held and returned, not re-fitted." The 'without rules' row is the record government on the record's seats.
- Failure scenario: a Swedish game whose government falls between 30 Jan and Sep 2026 re-forms as a lone Moderate cabinet on KD's support.

**M1 (Medium): K273 at 0.30 moves Polish governments near the seed under Poland's own rule, away from the record. The "no government moves" evidence covers exact chambers only.**

**L1 (Low): the harness proves less than its IDENTICAL line suggests.**
- Where: Program.cs:25-61, 339-343, 232-236.
- The defection loop, `WouldHold` and the BestOwn rebuild are copies. IDENTICAL compares only the top government at the built f.

## Verdict

The code does what the build says. It has one score routine for ranking, investiture and hold-out, no feedback loop, determinism intact and no save effect. The reported harness numbers reproduce, and on the exact chambers of record and seed the governments match the base commit's own code.

The rule as built at 0.30 still fails in play:
- **H1:** an unbounded support term, in a chamber whose defection rounds are abandoned, seats TD on PiS+KO and moves Poland's century.
- **H2:** in-engine, the 2022 record chamber's dated 2026-01-30 rows change government, the record's M+KD+L on SD among them, and the game's mid-term reading gives a lone Moderate cabinet on KD's support.

By the prereg's own clause, K273 is held and returned to Elias, not re-fitted and not re-pinned. The question for him: should support count only up to the seats a cabinet needs (H1's sketch), and is a cabinet's one-party cohesion meant to beat sitting together?
