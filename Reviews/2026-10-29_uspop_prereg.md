# K299 (K290's follow-up) - the pass rule, registered before any measurement of K299's tree (2026-10-29)

Drafted by the implementer of `wave/uspop` and committed ALONE on that branch before any code of the item is committed, and before any run of K299's tree (in or out of the engine). K290 (docs/RULINGS_2026-10-23.md; COMPLETED §950) registered K276's labour-force line (c) for USA 2040 as a miss (-3.43 %) and filed "the US population projection updated to CBO's 2025 demographics".

**What the build is** (for the rule's reading). CBO, *The Demographic Outlook: 2025 to 2055* (January 2025, publication 60875), its data file as CBO reposted it on 2 May 2025 (`ElectionsData/usa/raw/uspop_cbo_demographics/`, the Internet Archive's capture of cbo.gov's own file; cbo.gov refuses with a JavaScript challenge). That file publishes the Social Security area population in **four age groups** (Figure 2: 24 or younger, 25 to 54, 55 to 64, 65 or older, 1975-2055) and the civilian noninstitutionalized population 16+ in four groups (Figure 10) - **no population by single year of age or five-year band**. `Tools/uspop_prep.pl` generates `UsDemographicOutlook` from it (Figure 2's total asserted equal to the LTBO March 2025 population row at every shared year; Figure 10's total asserted equal to the LTBO's labour force over its participation rate within 0.05 % of the index). The model's anchored pyramid (`CohortDemographics.Projection`) keeps the Census Bureau's 2023 projection for each band's shape inside its CBO group and scales each group from age 25 to CBO's group in that year (band = Census band x CBO group / Census group); the D-19 rebasing onto the seeded pyramid is unchanged. **The group 24 or younger is NOT scaled** (DECLARED): CBO does not split it between the children born after its base year (set by CBO's fertility) and the young adults alive at it (set by its mortality and immigration), and one factor over 0-24 would charge a shortfall of births to people already born. Past 2055 each group's factor holds at its 2055 value, so the Census path's band-by-band change carries CBO's 2055 pyramid on.

**Disclosed before this rule: what was computed from the sources, not the model.** To choose how the group 24 or younger is treated, the target paths were computed from the generated tables alone (no run of the model, in or out of the engine): one factor over all four groups would move the anchor's 15+ index at 2055 DOWN (its 15-24 bands carry CBO's fertility shortfall), the treatment above moves it up at all three years. The choice rests on the allocation argument above, which holds whatever either figure is. The same arithmetic shows the within-group reweighting the rebasing makes (the seed's band composition against Census 2023's at 2024): at most 0.75 % on the 65+ group's index (2055), under 0.1 % on the other groups - the reason (d1)'s band is 1 %.

**Also measured before this rule, on CBO's two files alone** (the K290 attribution re-read; information for the record, not part of the rule): "the population CBO's rate applies to" that K290's split uses (CBO's potential labour force index over its actual participation rate index: x1.0986 at 2040 on 2025) is not CBO's population. CBO's civilian noninstitutionalized 16+ (Figure 10, equal to the LTBO's actual labour force over its actual rate) grows x1.0880; the difference (+0.99 %) is CBO's potential labour force growing faster than its actual one (2026-2040), a rate concept the LTBO file has no potential rate for. And Figure 10's civilian 25+ steps up 1.10 % against the Social Security area's 25+ between 2025 and 2026 (BLS's level reconciled to CBO's projection), then tracks it (-0.16 % by 2040). So of K290's -3.34 % population part, about 1 point is the potential-against-actual term, about 0.9 a concept step in the civilian series, and what is left is the model's population against CBO's demographics and the model's year alignment. The rule below is not changed by this.

## Amendment r2 - after the review (Reviews/2026-10-29_uspop_review.md, M1, M2, M3, L4), registered before any measurement of the amended tree (2026-10-29)

The text above is the prereg as committed alone in 86bee07c, before the first run of K299's tree. The tree it governed was measured (out of the engine: USA (c) 2030 -1.80 %, 2040 -2.69 %, 2055 -2.89 %; every (d) line inside its band) and reviewed READY on conditions. The coordinator directed that the review's M1 and M2 be fixed on this branch rather than filed. This amendment is committed ALONE, before any code of the amended tree is committed and before any run of it. It changes no band and no stated year.

**Disclosed: what was computed before this amendment, from the sources and not from the amended tree.** The review computed the rebased target out of the engine from the generated tables: the cohort split below moves (c)'s USA labour-force index by -0.00, -0.00 and -0.37 point at 2030, 2040 and 2055 on (c)'s old year key (2055 to about -3.26 %), and (c)'s year key moves the 15+ index by about +0.25, +0.56 and +0.64 point under K299. Those figures were in hand when this amendment was written. The two changes below rest on the arguments given, which hold whatever either figure is: M1's is the prereg's own allocation argument carried to the people it covers, and M2's is that one diagnostic cannot mean two things by a year.

**r2-1 (M1) - the group 24 or younger is split by cohort.** The prereg left the whole group on the Census path because one factor over 0-24 would charge a shortfall of births to people already born. That holds for the people alive in CBO's base year and not for those born after it, whose numbers CBO's fertility (and its mortality and immigration since their birth) sets. So, in year y: a band's share alive in the base year is the share of its five ages that are at least (y - 2025) old (uniform inside the band, DECLARED, as (d2)'s fifth of 15-19); that part keeps the Census path, shape and level. The part born after the base year takes CBO's Figure 2 group less the alive part, where CBO's group is put on the Census path's base-year level (the Census group in 2025 x CBO's group in y / CBO's group in 2025), so the two publishers' levels never mix; each born-after band is scaled by that one factor. The base year is 2025, the year CBO's projection opens on and (c)'s and (d)'s base. Through 2025 the group is the Census path; from 2050 every band of it was born after the base, so the group is CBO's whole. **Past 2055, consistently with the groups from 25:** the born-after factor holds at its 2055 value, so the Census path's band-by-band change carries CBO's 2055 group on. The groups from 25 are unchanged (one factor a group, held past 2055).

**r2-2 (M2) - (c) is keyed by the pyramid's own year.** (c) keyed a no-policy record by the floored midpoint of its span, (d) by the substrate year its pyramid stands at (NoPolicyCentury.Year.SubstrateYear). For a run that opens in the first half of its year (the USA's) the first trails the second by one, so (c)'s "2040 on 2025" read the pyramid's 2041 on 2026. From this amendment (c) reads every record by its substrate year, for all six countries, with its rule, years, base rule (the first year the source covers) and +/-3 % band unchanged. **Expected, DECLARED before measurement:** the EU five's runs open in the second half of their years, where the two keys agree, so their (c) lines do not move; if any EU line moves, the item reports it as this alignment, with its figure, and does not re-band it.

**r2-3 - (d), re-formed for r2-1.** (d1) asserts all four Figure 2 groups (the one below 25 now too) and the sum from 25 within the prereg's +/-1.0 %, at 2030, 2040 and 2055 by the pyramid's year on 2025. (d2) unchanged (+/-1.5 %). New, information only (review L4): the whole population against CBO's total (Figure 2).

**r2-4 (M3) - a by-age table.** The Internet Archive's capture of the publication page of 2026-08-25 (read at 18:53Z) links, under "Economic Data", CBO's "Demographic Projections" (`/recurring-data/57059`; the 2025-02-05 capture linked the same series as `/publication/57059`, which the first fetch log missed), and its "Corrections and Updates" says: "The file of demographic projections was also reposted with corrected values and additional information." That file is now being sought through the Archive. If a CBO population table by single year or five-year age for the January 2025 vintage is fetched byte-exact before the amended code is committed, a further amendment, committed alone, replaces r2-1 with that table; otherwise r2-1 stands. The attempt is recorded in the fetch log either way.

**r2-5 - unchanged, and the stopping rule restated.** K276's (c) rule, years and band; (d)'s bands; the other countries unchanged (the EU five's lines byte-identical); LongRunAnchors, DebtPathHold, CohortAgingStep, CohortSubstrate and GeneratedCatalog as in point 4. **If any USA (c) line lies outside +/-3 % on the amended tree, that is a stopping result: it is reported with its population and rate split, the band is not moved and nothing is registered.** K290's registry stays empty.
