Independent adversarial review. Read-only: no file in the project or its worktrees edited, Unity not run. Every finding below was checked against the code at the cited line.

The mechanism is sound. The AI appointment draws only the keyed generator. The ECB lean is exactly zero until the first appointment. The hold is re-checked on every day step and released by the verb. ## M

### M1 - The impact ledger's "Monetary" except-world keeps the old head forever. The seeded shadow seats the AI's pick instead.
- **Where:** `Assets/Scripts/Simulation/SimulationManager.cs:3517` (`PlayerAppointsCentralBankHead`) and `:3533-3555` (`AppointCentralBankHeadsWhoseTermsEnded`); `Assets/Scripts/Simulation/PolicyImpactLedger.cs:125` (`CentralBankHead -> "Monetary"`) and `:188` (fork built with `realSim.PlayerCountryId`); `Assets/Scripts/Persistence/SaveGameService.cs:292` (the fork's sim gets `PlayerCountryId`); `Assets/Scripts/Simulation/ShadowBaseline.cs:70-73` (the seeded shadow sets no player).
- **Scenario:** a Swedish game reaches the Riksbank term's end on day T, and the hold rises. The player appoints, so `AppointCentralBankHead` pulls a `CentralBankHead` lever. That is the Monetary family's first touch, so `BeforeLever` forks the except-world, which excludes the lever. In that fork, `PlayerCountryId == Sweden` and the player governs, so `PlayerAppointsCentralBankHead` is true. `AppointCentralBankHeadsWhoseTermsEnded` therefore skips Sweden every day from T on. The two counterfactuals now disagree:
  - the "without your policies" graph compares against the AI's governor;
  - the ledger's Monetary row compares against a governor who is never replaced, which contradicts AF-7's own rule that every head is replaced on its statute's date.
- This was the same before AF-7, when the except-world also kept the chair forever, so it is not a regression. AF-7 is what makes the inconsistency visible: the counterfactual now violates the statute rows the feature installs.
- **Fix:** in a world that excludes the head's lever, let the AI's keyed appointment run at the term's end, so the counterfactual is "the AI's pick" in both counterfactuals. One way is a per-sim flag `HoldsCentralBankForPlayer` that is false on a fork whose excluded family is Monetary. Alternatively, rule that the holdover-forever reading is intended, and state it on `PolicyImpactLedger`'s Monetary row.

### M2 - The average minister is not neutral. Its competence bias is the pool's mean, and every pool mean is positive. The diagnostic's "the path the chairs do not move" holds only for efficiency.
- **Where:** `CabinetSystem.cs:558-571` (`AverageMinister`: bias = mean of the pool); `CabinetSystem.cs:684-686` (`GetCompetenceBias` reads any seated minister, with no player gate); the readers are `MacroSystem.cs:765` (Health), `:1028` (Education) and `:1585` (Interior); `AverageCabinetDiagnostic.cs:18-19` (doc claim).

- **Why the check misses it:** `AverageCabinetDiagnostic` asserts "the neutral point - the path the chairs do not move" but measures only `HealthFamily` targets, which read efficiency. The competence channel is never checked.
- **Fix:** either seat the average minister at `CompetenceBias = 0f` (neutral, as efficiency ×1 is), or keep the pool mean, put the choice to Elias as a reading of "at the average", and correct the diagnostic's doc. Either way, add a check that compares the three competence readers with and without the average cabinet.

### M4 - The residual pass can pay a heavy post to a micro-party. No pin covers this.
