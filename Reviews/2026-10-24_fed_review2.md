## Findings

### H-A: the `FedNominationPending != null` clause changes the played game; a departed president's nominee is seated after the oath
`SimulationManager.cs:3935-3936`, together with the removed `if (fed) { country.FedNominationPending = null; }`; the contradicted comment is at `SimulationManager.cs:3962`.

H-1 needed only `FedAwaitsPlayersPick`. Every counterfactual that copies the played world's role gate is held by it. The added `country.FedNominationPending != null` acts only where that gate is CLOSED, which means the player has left the presidency with a nomination before the Senate. That is the played world's own case, not a fork's.

**Before:** the incoming (AI) president's pick was seated on the day the gate closed, and the nomination lapsed. That reading was ruled reasonable by the first review. It is also stated in `ConsentsToAiNominee`'s summary: "the oath: a player-president who leaves office inside a holdover hands the nomination to the AI that day - the second review's L5".

**Now:**
- The Vice Chair (lean 0) acts until `VoteOn`, and the incoming president can do nothing.
- On `VoteOn`, `UsFedConsent.Vote(country, day, pending.NominatingParty)` counts the DEPARTED president's party as the "own" caucus, under the new president's name (`Count.President` is the president of the day, `PresidentsParty` the old party).
- Under the crossing rule no Senate refuses, so the defeated administration's nominee is always seated, for four years.

**This is reachable in an ordinary game.** From the USA's start (2024-03-12), the seeded term ends 2028-03-12. A player-president who nominates on each hold walks the chain to term ends of 2032-06-09, 2036-09-06 and 2040-12-04. The 2040 nomination's vote falls on **2041-03-03**, six weeks after the 20 January 2041 oath. If the player loses the November 2040 election, the played world now seats the player's nominee on 3 March 2041, where the built rule seated the incoming president's pick on 20 January. That is four years of a different rate bias, on the money path, in the played world.

There are also two wording gaps:
- The Docket keeps "Before the Senate · <name>" for a player who no longer appoints.
- In real practice a pending nomination is returned at the end of a Congress (3 January), so the new reading is not the record's either.

**Fix:** drop the `country.FedNominationPending != null ||` term from the condition and restore the lapse (`if (fed) { country.FedNominationPending = null; }` where the AI seats). A fork first touched inside the window is still held by `FedAwaitsPlayersPick` wherever the played world holds, so section 5 passes unchanged. Played world and fork then follow one rule when the gate closes: both lapse. Add a probe: lodge, cross an oath to an AI president inside the window, and hold that the AI's pick is seated that day and the nomination cleared. If instead the new reading is wanted, it is Elias's to rule, and the `:3962` comment, the class note and the Docket's wording must change with it.

### L-A: a persistent null count retries, and logs an error, every day, forever, and locks the player out
`SimulationManager.cs:3922-3927`.

The vote is held again on every day `>= VoteOn`, and each failure is a `Debug.LogError`. Every counterfactual's errors pass into the played log (`ShadowBaseline.OnOwnGenerator`). While the nomination stays pending:
- the hold never rises (`CentralBankHeadAwaitsPlayer` needs no pending nomination);
- the verb refuses ("A NOMINATION IS ALREADY BEFORE THE SENATE");
- the AI may not seat.

So if no president or Senate is found for good, the seat is frozen with the Vice Chair acting, and the log carries hundreds of errors a turn per world. This is reachable only through a defect: `SenateFor` falls back to the record's Senate, and `PresidentOn` falls back to the government. The old build's null was bounded (one record). The project's way elsewhere (`ConsentsToAiNominee`) logs only on the term day, 3 January and 20 January.

**Fix:** log on the vote day and on those days only, and say what the player can do. If the reading holds, consider letting the player withdraw.

### L-B: the AI's pick "the morning after" misses a turn close that falls on the term day
`ShadowBaseline.cs:308-317` with `AdvanceTurn` (`:194-202`); `CurrencySystem.cs:50-52`.

In a world that answers the wait (the head family's, or the loaded no-policy world at `GameController.cs:949`, which seats the player), a term day that is a turn's LAST day goes through `_sim.AdvanceTurn`, and the rate is applied with the Vice Chair's lean. The AI's pick is seated only at the next day's start. The built AI rule, and the old code for a world that existed before T, seated it in day T's `AdvanceDay`, ahead of the turn close. So that one turn's rate differs.

**Reachability:** on the chains walked here the term days fall at 1, 90, 180, 271 and 361 days into their turn, never on the last day. A chain shifted by a load or a fork can land there with odds of about 1 in 365 per term. The section 5 window is planted mid-turn, so it cannot see this.

**Fix:** document it as DECLARED, or answer the Fed's wait inside the same day after `DoScheduled`, still dated T.
