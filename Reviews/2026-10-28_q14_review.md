## Findings

### H-1 (High): the floor can sit ABOVE the natural rate, and then the model is incoherent

France's seeded NAIRU is 7.5 against a floor of 7.358 (margin 0.14); Germany 3.3 against 2.95 (0.35); the USA 4.0 against 3.633 (0.37). The law catalogue cuts the natural rate by 0.3 to 1.2 points per act (LawCatalog.cs:1913-2012: Benefit Consolidation -1.2, Benefit Duration -1.0, In-Work Tax Credit -0.5, EPL -0.4, Wage Bargaining -0.3, ...), and the FT-8 composition shift lowers it as the labour force ages. A single -0.3 act puts France's effective NAIRU below its floor; a -0.4 act does it for Germany and the USA.

Failure scenario: France passes the Unemployment Benefit Consolidation Act. NAIRU 7.5 -> 6.3. Okun's reversion now pulls toward 6.3, but the clamp pins U at 7.358 for ever. The Phillips slack is permanently 6.3 ln(7.358/6.3) = +0.98, so inflation is permanently disinflationary; with the anchoring step the fixed point is E = T - 0.45 s, pi = T - 0.75 s, i.e. inflation about 0.73 pt below target indefinitely. The Taylor rule's (linear) gap term reads n - U = -1.06 for ever and holds rates low; the resulting above-potential growth cannot move unemployment (clamped), so the economy runs a standing positive output push while disinflating. The reform's unemployment payoff is exactly zero, and the effects panel will say so. None of this is K138's stated intent ("floors at the lowest rate since 1990"); K138 does not say what happens when the natural rate itself is legislated below the floor. The diagnostic does not test this case.

Fix (needs a ruling or a stated choice): e.g. make every natural-rate reader see max(EffectiveNaturalUnemploymentRate, floor) (one natural rate, the s399 rule), or floor the natural rate parameter at the country's floor, or ask Elias. Then add a diagnostic case: a NAIRU cut below the floor leaves the slack, the Taylor gap and the reversion at 0 with U at the floor.

### M-1 (Medium): the supply-shock excess is booked even when the floor absorbs the move - a phantom rise follows

ApplySupplyShockToUnemployment writes `SupplyUnemploymentExcess = excessAfter` unconditionally, then clamps U. When U is at the floor and participation falls cyclically, the excess goes negative but U does not move. Failure scenario: France at its floor 7.358, participation 56, a one-point cyclical fall: impact = -1 x 92.6 / 56 = -1.65, excess -1.65, U unchanged. Next boundary the excess decays to -0.99 and U rises by +0.66 with nothing behind it; meanwhile Okun's reversion reference (U - excess = 9.0, above the NAIRU) pushes U down all year. The pre-q14 clamp at 0 had the same defect but never bound; the floor binds routinely (see H-1, M-2). Fix: book the realized move, `state.SupplyUnemploymentExcess = excessBefore + (Unew - Uold)`. The diagnostic's (b) supply check sets the excess to 0 and checks only U, so it does not see this.

### M-2 (Medium): France's seed is below its floor, and France can barely overheat

France is seeded at 7.3 (WorldFactory.cs:169), below its floor 7.358 and below every French annual observation since 1990 in the stored CSV (2024 reads 7.45). The first Okun step lifts it +0.058 on day 1: in the mf14 trajectory that jump is a seed inconsistency, not the ruling's effect. Separately, with the NAIRU at 7.5, France can sit at most 0.142 below its natural rate: the largest overheating print is 0.3 x 7.5 x ln(7.5/7.358) = +0.043 pt above expectations. Record both as intended in the trajectory note, or reconcile the seed to the series (7.45, OECD 2024) before the family is cut.

### L-1 (Low): a phantom class name in the generator and the generated file

`Tools/unemployment_floor_prep.pl` (lines 6 and 67) and `UnemploymentFloorData.cs` line 6 say "UnemploymentFloorDiagnostic re-reads the CSV"; the class is `UnemploymentFloorPhillipsDiagnostic`. It is not backticked, so CommentClaimCheck will not catch it. Fix in the Perl and regenerate.

### L-2 (Low): the cross-read uses the other convex form's simulation

The PhillipsSlack comment checks the log form against the memo's section-5 simulation (30 bp linear vs "less than 40 bps" convex). As far as pdftotext renders it ("convex (slack = ,/ ,) DCCE estimates"), that simulation used the ratio form ugap/u, not ln(u/uNRU). The bound is still a fair sanity range, but the comment should say the simulated form is the memo's other convex specification.

### L-3 (Low): Sweden's floor is a pre-redefinition 1990 figure

Sweden's 1.717 (1990) predates the 2005 LFS redefinition and sits far below anything the modern series reaches (8-9). It never binds in play, so Sweden effectively keeps the old 0 floor. K138 read literally; noted for the record.

### L-4 (Low): legal no-cut indexation rules are not modelled under deflation

Pension lines index by the price-level ratio, so deflation cuts them nominally. US Social Security has no negative COLA (42 U.S.C. 415(i)), German pensions have the Rentengarantie (SGB VI 68a). Only bites in a deflation year; a follow-on, not a blocker.

### L-5 (Low): the Okun ledger seam reports unclamped terms

`OkunLedger` subscribers receive the three terms before the clamp; when the floor binds the terms no longer sum to the move, so a probe attributing unemployment (OkunAsymmetryProbe, the ledger twins) misreads floor days. Worth a note in the seam's summary.

## Summary

The data, the generator, the clamp sites, the log form, the deflation readers and the tags are all correct. H-1 is a modelling gap the ruling opens and the branch does not handle: legislating the natural rate below the floor (one -0.3 act for France) leaves a permanent slack, below-target inflation and a dead reform. M-1 is a bookkeeping defect that the floor turns from latent to live. Both should be fixed (H-1 after a ruling) before the mf14 family is recorded.

## Verdict: NOT READY
