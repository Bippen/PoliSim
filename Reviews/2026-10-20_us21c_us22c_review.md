## Findings (ranked)

### M1: When an answer ends a lapse at once, the economy runs one more lapsed day after funding has returned
`SimulationManager.cs:2184-2185` (`AdvanceUsFundingDay` sets `LapseFlowDay = CurrentDate + 1` while lapsed), `SimulationManager.cs:2080-2090` (`FundedByLaw` never clears it), and `SimulationManager.cs:1809` (`ActOnDocketAnswers`, which runs after the day's tick).

**Scenario.** A lapse is running and the player holds the presidency. This happens when the player's party withheld the resolution and then took the presidency, so the withhold lapses and the release resolution meets his docket on the period rule. It also happens when a held appropriation act is still on his docket. On day D, after D's tick, he signs.
1. `FundedByLaw` runs on D and resets `LapseSince`.
2. `LapseFlowDay` is still D+1, because D's funding tick armed it.
3. On D+1, `AdvanceDay` runs the economy first. `UsLapse.Lapsed(country, D+1)` is true, so `OutlayToday` stops another day's discretionary outlays and `ApplyOutput` counts one more lapsed day (`LapseOutputDays++`). That extra day scales the whole four-quarter CBO profile by (n+1)/n.
4. D+1's funding tick then disarms the flag, and the back pay lands a day late on D+2.

This breaks the timing contract in `UsLapse` ("the lapse's flows run from the day after it opens through the day funding returns, one day fewer than its counted days"). Before this branch the same answer was acted on in D+1's budget tick, after D+1's economy, so the counts agreed. `UsDocketDiagnostic` (c) checks "no money moved" only outside a lapse, so it does not catch this.

**Fix.** In `FundedByLaw`, set `country.LapseFlowDay = System.DateTime.MinValue` alongside `LapseSince`. On the normal tick path this is bit-identical: the flag already equals today and has been consumed, and the funding tick would set MinValue anyway. Add a W2-style check: sign a docketed lapse resolution between ticks, then assert `LapseOutputDays == LapseDays - 1` and that the next day's outlay is the back pay.

### M2: A docket deadline gives the player one day fewer than it says, and `DocketLawDay` is wrong for the horizon-release case
`SimulationManager.cs:1673` (`if (CurrentDate.Date < docket.Deadline) return null;`, which signs AT the deadline's step), `SimulationManager.cs:1753-1759` (`DocketLastDay` and `DocketLawDay` both return `Deadline`), and `SimulationManager.cs:2065`.

The period rule gives `LastDay` = the last day the player can answer (after that day's tick) and `LawDay = LastDay + 1` = the step at which the bill becomes law. The deadline rule breaks that pairing:
- **The eve's resolution.** It is presented on E = H-1 with `Deadline = H`. The docket (`DocketItem.LastDay`) says "answered by H", but H's budget tick signs it before the player ever sees day H. The only answerable moment is the evening of E. The diagnostic's (k) asserts `lastDay8 == lawDay8 == horizon8`, so it locks the inconsistency in.
- **The release on the horizon.** It is presented on H with `Deadline = H` and signed at H+1's step. `DocketLawDay` (shown by `GameController.AddBill` and `DeskLawDay`) reports H, but the law day is H+1.

J7 says "with the horizon as its deadline. Unanswered by then, it is signed". Read naturally, the player can still answer on the horizon.

**Fix.** Sign when `CurrentDate.Date > docket.Deadline` (the first step after the deadline), keep `DocketLastDay = Deadline`, and make `DocketLawDay = Deadline + 1`. This cannot open a lapse: on H+1 the budget tick (`AdvanceBudgetBillDay`) runs before `AdvanceUsFundingDay`, `FundedByLaw` moves `AppropriatedThrough` to the next 30 Sep, and the lapse test `CurrentDate > AppropriatedThrough` then fails. The horizon-release path already works this way. Update (k) to expect law and funding on H+1, with no lapse and no `LapseFlowDay` armed.

### L2: Veto ceremonies from an answer are queued a day late
`ActOnDocketAnswers` writes the override divisions (`Congress.RecordVeto`) outside the day loop. `GameController.QueueNewlyResolvedDivisions` (`GameController.cs:2246`) runs only after `AdvanceCountryDayTick` (`:781`), so the signing ceremony for an answer's override first appears after the NEXT day has run, dated the day before. Nothing in play calls the verbs yet (US-23 owns the surface), so this is latent.
**Fix.** When US-23 wires the verbs, call `QueueNewlyResolvedDivisions()` right after a successful `SignDocketBill` or `VetoDocketBill`.

### L3: Under J7, a player-president's veto cannot produce a lapse (worth putting to Elias)
J7 says "a lapse needs the player's veto or a withhold". In this build:
- The eve's resolution is always uncontested (an AI never withholds, and the president's party cannot withhold), so a player-president's veto of it is always overridden. `UsBudgetProcedureDiagnostic` (s9) asserts exactly that.
- Even if a veto were sustained, the funding tick on H would decide a fresh resolution ("released after the eve's fell") and docket it again, signed at H+1 if unanswered. One veto alone would still not lapse.

No code defect against the code's own declared rules, but J7's stated consequence cannot be reached.

### L4: An old-build save cut after a lapse ended loses that month's furlough mark
`FurloughCountedMonth` is additive. If an earlier-build save is cut later in the same month that a lapse covered the reference week, after the lapse has ended, it loads as MinValue. That month's published rate then omits the furloughed, where a continuous run on this build includes them. While the lapse is still running, the next funding tick re-marks the month, so the gap is narrow. Declaring it in the field's doc is enough.
