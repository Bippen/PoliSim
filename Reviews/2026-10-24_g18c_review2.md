## (2) L-2: the excess that cannot be returned

**The reading** (SeatConversion.cs:182-215): the party returns what 4 a § allows and keeps the rest. It is set aside with its seats, and the totalfördelning is taken again among the remaining nationally eligible lists, on 349 less the reserved 12 %-only seats and less the set-aside parties' seats. This repeats until no new party is set aside, which takes at most `parties + 1` passes against a guard of `4 * parties + 4`.

**Is it the most defensible reading?** Yes. The statute is less silent than the comment says:
- **5 § första stycket covers the case in its own words.** "Om ett parti vid fördelningen av de fasta valkretsmandaten enligt 3, 4 a och 4 b §§ har fått så många mandat som behövs för att det ska vara proportionellt representerat i riksdagen, ska man vid fördelningen av utjämningsmandaten bortse från partiet och de mandat det har fått." A party left over its total after 4 a and 4 b "har fått så många mandat som behövs" and more, so 5 § sets it aside together with its seats.
- **The re-division is what setting aside must mean.** Once the party and its seats are disregarded, the remaining lists' proportional shares can only be read on the seats that remain. That is the pre-2014 rule's own method: the overhang stood and the rest were re-divided.
- **RF 3 kap. 2 § fixes the chamber at 349.** That rules out the alternative of letting the chamber grow by the excess.
- The other alternative is to ignore the floor in that case. It contradicts 4 a §'s express "får dock inte".
- Returning what may be returned before setting aside follows 4 a §'s "ska ... återföras". The floor forbids only the small valkretsar.

**The re-division is correct.** Sum of totals = (349 - reserved - set-aside seats) + reserved + set-aside seats = 349. A party that already returned seats is re-tested against its new, lower total on the next pass. 12 %-only lists keep `totals = fixedWon`.

**Case (e), re-derived.**
- **The setup:** 155 valkretsar of two fixed seats each, so no return is ever allowed. 100 small valkretsar of {O 1000, A 600, B 500}, 55 large of {A 10000, B 9000}. O has 7.97 % of the vote nationally, A 48.6 % and B 43.4 %.
- **The fixed seats:** O 100, A 155, B 55.
- **The first totals:** about O 28, A 170, B 152.
- **The result:**
  - O, 72 over and unable to return, is set aside.
  - The re-division of 249 seats gives A about 131 against its 155 seats, so A is set aside too.
  - B takes the remaining 94 seats: 55 fixed and 39 adjustment.
  - Seats O 100, A 155, B 94 = 349, with 0 returned. This matches the code's assertion (SwedishRiksdagAllocationDiagnostic.cs:266).
- **The consequence:** B, with 43 % of the vote, holds 27 % of the chamber. That is what the reading does in a pathological chamber, and it can only arise where a party over 4 % wins fixed seats wholly in two-seat valkretsar. Gotland is the only one in Sweden.

**A residue, informational only:**
- The iteration re-divides after returns already made. Rounding in a re-division could in principle raise a party's total by one after it has returned seats against the older, higher-pass total.
- It would then sit one seat short and take an adjustment seat, with the chamber still at 349.
- Unreachable on anything Swedish; no change asked.
