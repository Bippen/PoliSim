## The questions asked

**(1) The algebra.** Turn form: Y' = (1+g)(rho Y + (1-rho) P) + kappa (C + I + G + NX - a_seed Y - gamma_seed P) - R P, with C + I = a Y + DI. Daily form: A = rho (1+g) + kappa (a - a_seed), B = kappa (G + NX + DI) + ((1-rho)(1+g) - kappa gamma_seed - R) P_open. Expanding the turn form gives exactly A Y + B, so the D affine slices still telescope to the turn map at constant inputs, as before. At the seed's references with R = 0 and Y = P, Y' = (1+g) P = P': the gap is zero against the period's END potential, which removes n929's standing -g/(1-rho+g) term (about -3.7 % at g = 1.5 %). Measured against P' the gap keeps rho: x' = rho x + (impulse - R)/(1+g); (c2) divides both kept shares by (1+g) and its model expectation rho + kappa (a - a_seed)/(1+g) is the same expression. g: `PotentialGrowthRate` is written only in the turn's potential-growth block (and the constructor), so it is constant across a period's 365 days and both forms read the same g; it is the derived growth over the previous period, an expectation that lags a change in labour growth by a year (gap error about the year-on-year change in potential growth over (1-rho), a few hundredths of a percent - negligible). The clamp to A <= 0.99 cannot bind (A is about 0.62). One harmless consequence: the daily fixed point B/(1-A) sits about 5 % above P_open at g = 2 %, so intra-year growth is front-loaded (annualised about 2.5 % on day 1, 1.6 % on day 365, exact over the year); this shape existed before and Okun's daily increments still sum to the turn's.

**(2) R's lifecycle.** Saved: a public field on a `[Serializable]` Country under Newtonsoft's as-is World serialization, format 65 (new in this branch; main is at 64, a v64 save is refused). Loaded: same path; `dayOfTurn` is recomputed from the dates, so a mid-period load closes R on the right day. Preview: `ClonePreviewCountry` copies it, and the turn form's own close mutates only the clone. Shadow baselines copy by save/load round trip, so R rides. Closed exactly once a period: the live, shadow, smoke and harness runs all advance through `AdvanceDay`, whose macro block calls `CloseDemandReference` when `dayOfTurn == DaysPerTurn` (the day the boundary test fires), after the day's identity, with the GDP the identity read and the period-open potential it used; `AdvanceTurn` no longer calls the turn form, so no period is closed twice. The remaining turn-form callers are the preview clone and three diagnostics on throwaway worlds. The only way to skip or double a close is an epoch change mid-run (`SetEpoch` moves `dayOfTurn` and the boundary together, so the straddled period may close early or late once) - diagnostics only. The daily close samples day 365's C and I against day 365's prior GDP where the turn form samples the period's opening, so R is equivalent to second order (kappa (a - a_seed) dY / P, about 0.04 % of potential), not exactly; `AggregationEquivalenceCheck` runs one period and does not compare R (info).

The "about 4.5 r" in the brief carries an extra (1 - lambda), which would hold only if R were moved before the identity read it; it understates the gap by 1/(1-lambda) = 1.39. See M-1.

## Findings

### M-1 (Medium, a prediction, not a code defect) - (c4) at 2 % is marginal under the new form; expect a red bar on the first run

Code-wise (c4) is right: three hard bounds plus a count, all failing. The concern is the outcome. A 2 % bound tolerates a demand-share drift of 2/6.26 = 0.32 % a year (EU) or 0.29 % (USA). Taking the brief's France figure of -0.34 % a year gives 2.1 % on the code's factor, a fail by a hair, where 4.5 r would have read 1.5 %. My own estimate: I inverted n929's gaps at years 0/1/5/10/30/100 (old form: kappa 0.5, no R, no g, with each country's potential growth from (d2)) to the implied impulse share, then pushed that path through the new form. The years-30 and 100 figures are the reliable ones: Sweden -1.8 / -0.7, Poland -2.0 / -1.1, USA -1.1 / -0.5, Germany -1.0 / -0.7, France -0.8 / -0.5, Italy +0.6 / +0.1. Year 10 reads -1.5 to -3 for Sweden, Poland and the USA, but the inversion treats early transients as steady states there, so it is unreliable. The estimate is optimistic for two reasons:
(i) In n929 the Taylor rule partly offset the drift, so the exogenous drift is larger than the observed one. Under K209 a steady Taylor stimulus is itself absorbed by R and offsets nothing in the steady state.
(ii) With the rate response the gap/R system gains a slow mode (eigenvalue about 0.85 against rho's 0.61), so an early disturbance is still visible at year 10.

Failure scenario: the next suite run fails (c4) for Sweden and/or Poland at year 30, and possibly Sweden, Poland and the USA at year 10. That is another rework round on a ruled design. Fix: none in code. Run `LongRunAnchorsDiagnostic` before the merge, and if (c4) fails, take the measured drift rates to Elias with the factor stated as 6.3 d (EU) and 7.0 d (USA), not 4.5 d. The bound is preregistered, and lambda/kappa are pinned by D-13 (b), so neither can move to pass.
