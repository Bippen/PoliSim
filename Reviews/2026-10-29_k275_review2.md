## The new EffectiveRate

**Is it right, and is it kink-free?** Yes. Write E(r) = seed + f·y(r) − y₀. Then E(r) − (seed − y₀) = f·y(r). The factor f reads no lever and no sub-row (review 1, hunt 2), so the yield the behavioural rate stands on is the uncapped yield times a constant. A lever or band move therefore changes it by exactly the ratio form's proportion, y(r′)/y(r). In points the move is f·Δy, with the same constant slope above and below the current position. E is affine in the yield, so the curve has no kink. Where the cap binds, the slope in points is f, not 1. That is the same scaling the revenue gets (seed × base × f × Δratio), so the player's move keeps its proportion in both readings.

**Is the 2e-8 proportion error float noise?** Yes. In my rerun, Poland at y40 gives 1.037224107481345 for the capped form against 1.0372241325337082 for the uncapped, a relative error of 2.4e-8. Germany at y100 gives 4.9e-9, and y1 (inside the bound) is identical to the bit.

`EffectiveRate` returns a float. At about 23.6 a float's ulp is 1.9e-6, and creep_play then subtracts seed − y₀ (about 6.2) and divides by f·y (about 17.3). One ulp therefore becomes about 1.1e-7 in the quotient. The observed 2.4e-8 is under one ulp. A real non-proportionality, such as the points cap in review 4's N6, would show errors of order 0.1 to 0.5. The diagnostic's `FloatTolerance` of 1e-5 sits about 100× above the noise and four orders below a kink, so it is a sound choice.

The additive form does not break proportionality, because the additive constant (seed − y₀) is removed before the proportion is taken. That constant is the same in both forms. In the y1 probe, with no cap, the two forms agree to all printed digits.

**Is the behavioural rate now consistent with the revenue's take?** Yes, in the sense that matters: both read the same capped yield, f·y. They are still different numbers. Poland at y100 shows a take of 34.89 % on the anchored base against a flat-equivalent rate of 23.55 % on income. That difference is the points-form against ratio-form gap, which exists at every position whenever the yield is above the seed's (seed > y₀; review 4's N6). It is not new. The pre-cap mismatch is gone: the behavioural rate no longer exceeds the statute's top rate (23.55 against 36), and the participation term (−1.58) no longer charges creep the revenue does not collect.

The behavioural rate is continuous in time at the binding year, because f = 1 exactly at the bound: Poland y24 reads 23.44 and y25 reads 23.74 (uncapped 24.04). Germany y63 reads 37.37 and y64 reads 37.46.

Both get the same f, because `CreepCapFactor` reads only `RateSeed`, which the clone copies. So the hike is f·Δy, consistent with the revenue.
- q37's tax term (`TaxLevelPoints` on either side of each write: ParliamentSystem.cs:593-595, 619-621, 747-753; SimulationManager.cs:7999) books before-and-after deltas within one call. f changes only at a boundary (the statute memo's key), so a write never sees f move under it, and no spurious impulse arises from the cap's drift.

## The new §6 assertion: is it non-vacuous?

Partly.

- **Poland's top-rate check discriminates.** The uncapped rate passes 36 % from about year 63 (36.32 at y63, 40.62 at y100). Under 2b5ea836's `EffectiveRate` that check would therefore fail. The `bound == 0` guard stops a Poland run that never reaches the cap from passing silently.
- **The "inside the bound, uncapped to the bit" check discriminates** against a cap that acts where it should not.
- **Germany's top-rate check is vacuous.** Germany's uncapped rate peaks at 42.39 against a top rate of 45, so it passes with or without the factor.
- **The proportion probes cannot detect a wrong factor.** Any rate-independent factor passes them, including none at all.

So §6 never pins the factor itself (L-n1).

## New findings

There are no High or Medium findings.

**L-n1. §6 does not pin the behavioural factor to the revenue's.** Every §6 check passes for any rate-independent factor that keeps E under the top rate. That includes an over-cap such as f², or E pinned at the seed rate. The consistency M1 asked for is that the behavioural yield is the revenue's yield times the same f. The record already holds the pieces, so it would cost nothing to assert, in every binding year:

(E − seed + y₀) / (E_uncapped − seed + y₀) ≈ IncomeTaxRatio / IncomeTaxRatioUncapped, within `FloatTolerance`.

The two sides are the same f. The failure scenario: a later edit squares the factor in `EffectiveRate`, and §6 stays green.
