## 1. The wait factor (HealthFamily.cs 248-249)

`waitFactor = (1 / perAgeCostRatio)^w x (1 / effectivenessRatio)^0.5`, with `w = WaitSpendingElasticity = 1 / (1.069 - (-0.149)) = 1 / 1.218 = 0.8210`. Units: `perAgeCostRatio` is (real health spending / the age-cost index) over the same quantity captured at `Seed` (`SpendPerAgeCostSeed = SpendPerAgeCost(country)`, line 165), so it is dimensionless and exactly 1 on the seed world. It is the same ratio treatable mortality already reads, which is what the ruling asks ("as coverage and mortality do"). Sign: more spending gives a ratio above 1, so the factor falls below 1 and the waits shorten; `eS - eD > 0`, so the exponent is positive and the equilibrium is stable. Correct.

Edge cases. With zero or negative spending the ratio is floored at 0.01 (shared with mortality), so the factor is at most 100^0.821 = 43.8. `AdvanceYear` then clamps the target at `MaxWaitDays` = 1000: the result is bounded and does not run away. NaN in the price level propagates. Unity's `Mathf.Max(0.01f, NaN)` returns NaN, so the floor does not catch it. That was already true of mortality and coverage and is not new. At the seed the spending term is `1^0.821 = 1`, so the factor reproduces the old effectiveness-only value exactly, and diagnostic (a) asserts that.

Other readers. I grepped `WaitFactor`, `TargetsFor`, `WaitCataractDays` and `WaitKneeDays` across `Assets`. The new term multiplies the factor by a positive constant at fixed spending, so the readers are safe. `AverageCabinetDiagnostic` 107-110 and `EffectivenessDiagnostic` 114/126/131 compare factors at the same `spending`, so their orderings and their 1e-5 equalities at the seed's spending hold unchanged. `HealthFamilyDiagnostic`'s starved run asserts longer waits, and that now holds more strongly. The UI readers (`PeopleV35` 154, `PoliticsV35` 681, `PeopleSlips` 281-283) only print. Nothing in the model reads the waits, so there is no feedback into the macro path, and the byte-identity checks (the lever ledger, `Phase5NoFeedbackDiagnostic`) compare paths that all carry the new term alike.

## 2. The source

`sha256sum` of the PDF is `2b885df3...d6d323`, the same as `SHA256SUMS.txt`. The headers file also matches its digest. The server's `Content-MD5` `5sV0b/+q13WL88YhbbwWeQ==` equals the base64 MD5 of the bytes (`openssl md5 -binary | base64`). The text extracts cleanly with `pdftotext -layout`. In Table 2 (demand), pooled cross-section 2011-14, the IV column reads "Waiting time (log) -0.149* (0.086)". In Table 3 (supply), the same column reads "1.069*** (0.354)", with a first-stage F of 14.04. Table 3's note confirms that the instrument set "includes proportion of population over 60 years old". The conclusions (p. 18) give the demand range "-0.15 and -0.24" and England's "-0.2" (Martin and Smith). Both equations are log utilisation on log waiting time (section 4, equation (3)), so the coefficients are elasticities, and the paper assumes equilibrium (demand equals supply). Under `q = A w^eD` and `q = K w^eS`, `ln w = (ln A - ln K) / (eS - eD)`. The derivation is sound, and the code's comments say what the paper says. The column choice is internally consistent (one column for both). Note that it gives the smallest response the table admits: the first-difference IV pair gives 1 / (0.627 + 0.238) = 1.16 and the 2013-14 pair gives 1 / (0.718 + 0.128) = 1.18 (see L3). The fetch log bills the OECD cross-country paper that could not be reached and does not use the CMAJ relay. That is the right call.

## 3. The diagnostic

It follows the sibling idiom: `CheckExit.ArmLogFold()`, a static `_ok` and `_sb` reset at the top of `Run`, a `HOSPITALWAITS: PASS` or `FAILED` line, and `CheckExit.Finish(_ok ? 0 : 1)`. Every world is built inside `SimulationManager.EpochScope()` after `SimulationRandom.Seed`, and every played run destroys its GameObject in a `finally`. Nothing leaks between runs except `NoPolicyCentury`'s per-player cache. That cache is intended, and the new fields are filled in the same `Advance` that fills the cached run, so a cached century always carries them. Missing waits, a missing country or a short century each fail rather than pass.

Would it fail while the statement is false? Yes, decisively through (a). On the base's formula the factor at +/-20 % spending equals the factor at the seed, so the direction test (`moved < atSeed`) fails for all three countries. (a)'s expected value reads the same constant the code uses (line 93). That is the statement being tested, not a tautology about the source, but it does not check that the constant is the paper's figure (the provenance block does that). (b)'s step check (line 154) recomputes the factor through `TargetsFor` itself. So it proves that `AdvanceYear` uses `TargetsFor` at the state it read, not that the formula is right. (b)'s gap check is the played-path proof, but it is thinner than it looks (L1). (c) cannot see the drift M1 describes.

## Findings

**High:** none.

**M1 (Medium) - Outside the instrument's case, the waits fall with real wage growth at no policy, and nothing measures or declares it.** `HealthFamily.cs` 248-249 and the DERIVED premise at 89-92; the mechanism is `SimulationManager.cs` 8324-8350. Wherever the player's party does not lead (`aiBudget = !PlayerGoverns(country)`), `IndexSpendingLines` grows the health line by prices x the age-cost driver x the real wage index (RF-2, §384, "the service lines ... are staff costs that move with wages - Baumol's finding"). `HealthSpendingReal` deflates by the CPI price level, not by wages, so real spending per age-cost unit climbs with the real wage index every year. The waits then shorten by (wage growth)^0.821 with no policy at all. Failure scenario: the player takes Sweden as S, in opposition at the 2022 start, or plays Germany while Sweden, Italy and Poland run AI books. At about 1.5 % a year of real wage growth, the ratio is about 1.16 after ten years (waits about 11 % shorter) and about 4.4 after a century (waits at about 30 % of their seed). Better outcomes come purely from the wage bill, which the codebase itself names as cost, not capacity. That contradicts the premise "capacity moves one for one with real health spending". Diagnostic (c) cannot see this. `NoPolicyCentury` runs the player with no party, and `PlayerGoverns` returns true for that case (3674-3676), so the wage term is 1 and the ratio stays flat. Treatable mortality already carries the same drift through the same ratio, so the change does follow the ruling's "as mortality does". It should still be DECLARED beside `WaitSpendingElasticity`. Better, (c) should also run one AI-book or opposition case and print the ten-year ratio, so the drift is measured, not discovered. Deflating by a wage index for the capacity term is the principled fix, but that is a ruling (it would move mortality too), not this item's.

**L1 (Low) - (b)'s gap is satisfied mostly by the term (a) already proves, and its margin over the base formula is thin.** `HospitalWaitsDiagnostic.cs` 35 and 110-115. `MinGapDays` = 0.5 d, against an expected gap of about 3 d. Sweden's cataract: the target is 60.2 x (1 - 1.1^-0.821) = 4.5 d below the seed, and 1 - 0.7^3 of that is 3.0 d after three years. Year one's raise also lifts the effectiveness flow (allocated / requested above 1 for that year only), so the base formula already moves the waits. With a portfolio ratio near 1.1 the base leaves the cataract wait about 0.42 d shorter after three years: 60 x 0.047 x 0.3 x 0.7^2. The knee wait, 1.0 d, would already clear 0.5. The check fails on the base only because the cataract leg falls short by tenths of a day. Failure scenario: an edit that cuts the spending elasticity to a fifth of its value still passes (b), and only (a) catches it. Fix: compare against the same run with `WaitSpendingElasticity` forced to 0, or require the gap to be at least half the closed-form expectation (seed x (1 - ratio^-w) x (1 - 0.7^3)).

**L2 (Low) - In a raise's first year, the same money enters the waits twice.** `HealthFamily.cs` 249. The equilibrium elasticity already is the waits' full response to capacity. The P5-C7 effectiveness term multiplies on top, and in year one it reads the same raise through allocated / requested. Mortality has the identical structure (`QualityEfficiencyElasticity` beside `QualitySpendingElasticity`), so this is consistent with the ruling, but the comment "kept as the multiplier it was" should say that the year-one flow overlaps the spending term.

**L3 (Low) - The column choice should be stated as a choice.** `HealthFamily.cs` 81-88. The pooled cross-section IV pair gives w = 0.82. The paper's first-difference IV pair, which it treats as robustness, gives 1.16. The paper's own demand equation finds the over-60 share's effect on utilisation insignificant (0.021, s.e. 0.035). That is weak support for "demand moves one for one with the age-cost index", which is already marked `[AUTHORED-DRAFT]`. One DECLARED sentence naming the alternative pair and its w would close this.

**L4 (Low) - Not registered and not run.** `HospitalWaitsDiagnostic` appears in no `CheckSuite.cs` entry, and there is no `HOSPITALWAITS` log under `PoliSim-captures`. The commit does not say it was run, and the arithmetic in L1 shows the (b) margins are untested. The q32 precedent leaves registration to the integrator, but a PASS line from one run is owed before merge.

**L5 (Low) - The step check assumes nothing after the families step changes its inputs.** `HospitalWaitsDiagnostic.cs` 154. It recomputes the factor after `AdvanceTurn`. Between `HealthFamily.AdvanceYear` (`SimulationManager.cs` 6801) and the end of the turn, I found no write to the price level, the health lines or the age-cost driver. Any later boundary step that touches them (a year's event, a re-formed cabinet in a case where a party is seated) would make this check fail even though the model is right. That is a loud false negative, not a silent pass, so it is Low.

**L6 (Low, documentation) - SOCIETY_STATS §6 keeps a stale clause.** Line 191 still says "until C7 lands, spending per head against its seed stands in for the ratio" (from before this change). The appended q28 sentence is accurate, and so are the §1, §3 and §7 rows: the formula, 1 / (1.069 + 0.149), the SOURCED and `[AUTHORED-DRAFT]` split, and no figure copied. The comment at `HealthFamilyDiagnostic.cs` 94 ("the waits, which read it [effectiveness], lengthen") is now half the story. Neither is wrong enough to block.

## Summary

The change does what the ruling says, the way mortality does it. The elasticities are in the paper at the stated tables and columns, and they mean what the code says. The equilibrium derivation is right. The digest, the MD5 and the bytes agree. The seed reproduces the old factor exactly, the guards bound the extremes, and no other reader changes behaviour. The diagnostic fails on the base through (a). Its played-path gap is thin (L1), and its idle path cannot see the one behaviour this change introduces in real play: waits that fall with the wage bill under an AI book (M1). None of this is a correctness bug in the item. M1 is a modelling consequence that mortality already has, and it should be DECLARED and measured. L4's run is owed before merge.
