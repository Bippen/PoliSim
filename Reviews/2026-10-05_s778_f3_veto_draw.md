### 1. Live forks key the boundary tax act on a different date, so the 'every world draws alike' claim fails in play

- **Lens:** draw-gate - **reviewer:** minor - **skeptic:** minor
- **Where:** Assets/Scripts/Simulation/SimulationManager.cs:1454

**The scenario.** Setup: the player governs Poland, a partner holds Finance, and the player touches a dial family, say Sectors, in turn N.

What happens at boundary B:
- GameController.Update first calls AdvanceDay (GameController.cs:773), then AdvanceTurn() (:791). That calls _impactLedger.AdvanceTurn (:6112), which forks the Sectors except-world from the state at B (PolicyImpactLedger.cs:142).
- The new fork immediately runs ShadowBaseline.AdvanceTurn (ShadowBaseline.cs:138-139). That advances DaysPerTurn more days before resolving.
- So the fork's PartnerTaxAct -> PolishStatuteActs -> PresidentialVetoGate keys Draw on CurrentDate = B+DaysPerTurn. The played world keyed the same act on B.
- The post-load 'no-policy' shadow is a fork made at the save's mid-turn date D (GameController.cs:911). It keys its boundary act on D+DaysPerTurn.

Result:
- For an identically titled act at risk at rate r, the two draws are independent and disagree with probability 2r(1-r).
- The ledger then shows a tax-rate contribution under a family that never touched taxes.
- PresidentialVeto.cs:79-83 and SimulationManager.cs:1442-1444 assert agreement 'in every world the game holds'. That holds only where the worlds' clocks agree, which is PolicyImpactLedgerDiagnostic's order, not GameController's. (The seed shadow never reaches the gate: it has no PlayerCountryId, so no PartnerTaxAct and no day ticks.)

**The fix proposed.** Key the boundary act on the boundary it closes. Pass PartnerTaxAct's `boundary` (TurnBoundary(CurrentTurn+1)) through PolishStatuteActs into the gate as the draw's date, defaulting to CurrentDate for the day-tick paths.
- In the played world CurrentDate == boundary at that moment, so no played outcome changes.
- Every world at the same turn then shares the key.
- Also state the premise in both docs (the worlds agree only where they hold the act on the same keyed date). The root skew is the next finding.

**The skeptic's evidence.** I could not refute it. I traced the failing path in play, and every line below was read in the staged tree. SimulationManager.cs is staged only. ShadowBaseline.cs, PolicyImpactLedger.cs and FinancePartner.cs are unchanged.

1. The played world reaches the boundary first. GameController.cs:773 `bool turnBoundaryCrossed = _simulationManager.AdvanceDay();` sets CurrentDate to B = TurnBoundary(N+1) while CurrentTurn is still N. Then :791 calls `AdvanceTurn();`.
2. The ledger forks after the played days. Inside GameController.AdvanceTurn, :6112 `_impactLedger?.AdvanceTurn(...)` runs before :6118 `_simulationManager.AdvanceTurn(decisions)`. On first touch, PolicyImpactLedger.cs:142 does `new ShadowBaseline(realSim, realWorld, _playerCountryId)`, and :147 calls `world.AdvanceTurn(Strip(...))` right away.
3. The fork copies the boundary date and the player. SaveGameService.cs:98-99 copies CurrentTurn N and CurrentDate B. :243 sets `sim.PlayerCountryId`. AiFinanceMinistryEnabled is an instance field that defaults to true (SimulationManager.cs:707).
4. The fork then advances a full turn of days. ShadowBaseline.cs:138-139 runs `for (int d = 0; d < SimulationManager.DaysPerTurn; d++) { _sim.AdvanceDay(); } _sim.AdvanceTurn(...)`, with DaysPerTurn = 365 (SimulationManager.cs:216). So the fork resolves turn N at B+365, while its ComingBoundaryDate is still B.
5. Both worlds reach the gate, keyed on different dates. SimulationManager.cs:4809-4815 calls `PartnerTaxAct(country, decision, partnerWrote, ComingBoundaryDate)` for the player's Poland. That goes through PolishStatuteActs (:1307-1308) to the gate. Staged :1454 is `Draw(SimulationRandom.MasterSeed, country.Id, CurrentDate, passage.Title)`. The played world keys on B and the fork keys on B+365. The master seed is the same in both, so an identically titled act draws independently.
6. The diagnostic uses the other order. PolicyImpactLedgerDiagnostic.cs:166-170 runs the ledger first, then the real days, then the real AdvanceTurn. That order is in sync; GameController's is not.
7. The post-load shadow is also offset. GameController.cs:911 forks it at the save's date D. It carries PlayerCountryId, so it can reach PartnerTaxAct. It keys on D+365, which differs from B unless the save was cut on a boundary.
8. The seed shadow is not affected. It has no PlayerCountryId (ShadowBaseline.cs:53-70) and runs no day ticks, so it never reaches the gate.

The docs:
- Staged SimulationManager.cs:1443 makes an unconditional claim: "so every world the game holds draws alike". In GameController's order that is false.
- PresidentialVeto.cs:79-81 is conditional ("the same act on the same day") and literally true. But its reason, "a fork that skips the day ticks would draw apart", assumes the forks hold the act on the played world's day, and in play they do not.

Re-grade, kept at minor:
- No played outcome changes and no random stream moves: Draw is a pure hash and reads only MasterSeed.
- The effect is limited to the counterfactual worlds: the impact ledger and a post-load shadow line.
- The root skew is older than this change. GameController advances the days before PolicyImpactLedger.AdvanceTurn, against that method's "call BEFORE the real game advances" contract. That skew already offsets those worlds by a year of dynamics, which outweighs this term.
- Under that skew the fork's act often has a different title anyway. FinancePartner.cs:192-194 and 269-276 size the step from NominalGdp and the tax bases, and both drift over the extra year. So the date key is one of several reasons the draws differ.
- The path itself is rare. The partner must tighten, the lines' clamps must leave a shortfall, the act must pass, and the President's backing party must not vote for it.
- What this change does owe is a stated premise on a money-path doc. The claim convention requires it.

**The skeptic's corrected fix.** 1. Owed by this change: state the premise in both docs, at SimulationManager.PresidentialVetoGate and PresidentialVeto.Draw. The worlds draw alike only where they hold the same act, with the same title, on the same day. In play, two worlds resolve their boundary later than the played world, so their boundary tax act may draw apart:
   - the impact ledger's except-worlds, a turn later. They fork at the boundary after the played days, then ShadowBaseline.AdvanceTurn runs DaysPerTurn more.
   - a shadow forked from a mid-turn load, later by the save's day of the turn.
   The played world and its streams are untouched. This is a DECLARED premise.

2. Optional hardening: pass PartnerTaxAct's `boundary` (ComingBoundaryDate) through PolishStatuteActs to the gate as the draw's date, with CurrentDate as the default for the day-tick paths.
   - In the played world CurrentDate == ComingBoundaryDate at AdvanceTurn, so no played outcome changes. Planted checks are unaffected.
   - This aligns only identically titled acts. The fork's partner step is sized from a year-later NominalGdp and tax base, and Decide still reads the president on CurrentDate.

3. The real cure is the older clock skew (the reviewer's separate finding): GameController's order against PolicyImpactLedger.AdvanceTurn's contract, plus the post-load fork's offset. Once the worlds' clocks agree, keying on CurrentDate already agrees and needs no change.

### 2. PRE-EXISTING (outside the staged diff): live impact-ledger forks and the post-load shadow resolve each turn a turn of days late

- **Lens:** draw-gate - **reviewer:** defect - **skeptic:** defect
- **Where:** Assets/Scripts/UI/GameController.cs:6112

**The scenario.** Update runs the turn's DaysPerTurn AdvanceDay calls (GameController.cs:773) before AdvanceTurn() (:791). AdvanceTurn's first act is _impactLedger.AdvanceTurn (:6112).
- A first-touched family is forked from the boundary-day state (PolicyImpactLedger.cs:142) and advanced at once (:147).
- ShadowBaseline.AdvanceTurn then runs another DaysPerTurn days (ShadowBaseline.cs:138) before resolving turn N.
- So every except-world carries an extra year of daily dynamics: MacroSystem's daily step, the cohort substrate's ApplyDaily, publications. It resolves turn k at TurnBoundary(k+2) while the played world resolves it at TurnBoundary(k+1).
- Every contribution real - except(f) mixes in that drift.

Why the existing proof misses it: PolicyImpactLedgerDiagnostic advances the ledger BEFORE the day loop (lines 166-169, 219-221, 283-288). Its 'the lazy fork is exact' identity never runs in GameController's order.

The post-load shadow (GameController.cs:911) has the same shape, offset by the save's day-of-turn.

**The fix proposed.** Fork and advance the ledger at the turn's start, as the diagnostic does. Or let a fork taken at the boundary resolve without re-running the days it already holds. Then add a PolicyImpactLedgerDiagnostic case in GameController's own order (days, then ledger, then real resolution). The same applies to the shadow re-forked on load. Worth its own item; F3 inherits it through the date key.

**The skeptic's evidence.** I could not refute it. The failing path is in the code, it was there before this change, and the staged diff leaves it as it was. Its live reach is narrower than "every except-world".

The order in live play
- In GameController.cs, line 773 calls `bool turnBoundaryCrossed = _simulationManager.AdvanceDay();`. Lines 789-792 then call `if (turnBoundaryCrossed) { AdvanceTurn(); }`. This is the only call to AdvanceTurn(). AdvanceDay returns true once the 365th day has run (SimulationManager.cs:482, `daysSinceEpoch % DaysPerTurn == 0`).
- AdvanceTurn's first step is `_impactLedger?.AdvanceTurn(...)` at :6112. The played world's `_simulationManager.AdvanceTurn(decisions)` comes after it, at :6118. So the ledger runs after the turn's days, not at the turn's start. The comment at :6108-6111 says otherwise.
- PolicyImpactLedger.cs:142 forks the world from that boundary-day state, and :147 advances it at once.
- RestoreSaveState sets `CurrentDate = currentDate;` (SimulationManager.cs:4593), so the fork keeps the boundary day's date.
- ShadowBaseline.cs:138-139 then runs `for (int d = 0; d < SimulationManager.DaysPerTurn; d++) { _sim.AdvanceDay(); }` and then `_sim.AdvanceTurn(...)`.
- AdvanceDay has no guard. The daily fiscal accrual is not capped: `period.PlannedGovernmentSpending * FiscalFlowPerDayFraction` at SimulationManager.cs:6860. So the fork runs a second year of the old period's spending, GDP growth and price compounding before its boundary.
- Result: the fork's CurrentTurn matches the played world's, but its CurrentDate is 365 days later. GameController.Statistics.cs:550 prints contribution = real - except, so that drift goes straight onto the page.

Why the proofs pass
- PolicyImpactLedgerDiagnostic advances the ledger before the day loop (lines 166-169, 219-222, 283-288).
- UiScreenshotDriver.cs:5870-5873 uses the same order.
- No test runs in GameController's order.
- The order has been like this since the ledger arrived in cf18f273: AdvanceDay at :848, `AdvanceTurn();` at :866, the ledger at :4948. HEAD cba1bd04 has the same lines at 791, 6112 and 6126.

The post-load shadow
- GameController.cs:911 forks the shadow at the save's day d. Autosaves (:801 and :1411) can fall on any day.
- The shadow then runs 365 days from day d on every turn, so its boundaries fall d days after the played world's.

Live reach, which is narrower than the finding says
- BuildPlayerDecision (GameController.cs:6277-6300) sets only InterestRateChange.
- Its dial is drawn only when `CurrentFedChair == null` (:3971, :4006, :4031). WorldFactory.cs:962, 992 and 997 give the USA, Sweden and Poland a central-bank head, so the dial exists only for Germany, France and Italy. EurozoneRateSystem.cs:105 reads it there.
- So the ledger forks in live play only for a Eurozone player who sets a rate push. In those games the Statistics page shows wrong MONETARY and interaction lines.
- In a Polish game the ledger never forks. The F3 gate is reached only through the post-load shadow. RestoreInto restores PlayerCountryId, which lets the shadow reach PartnerTaxAct, then PolishStatuteActs, then PresidentialVetoGate.
- There the staged Draw keys on `date.ToString("yyyy-MM-dd")` (staged PresidentialVeto.cs). The shadow's date is d days off, so its draw differs from the played world's. The gate's new comment, "every world the game holds draws alike", holds only once the clocks agree.
- No random stream moves: the draw is a pure hash.

Grade: a real defect, but pre-existing. It should be filed as its own item and does not block F3.

**The skeptic's corrected fix.** Moving the fork to the turn's start, as the finding proposes, does not work in GameController. The decision is built at the boundary, and the drafts can change during the turn.

Instead, make ShadowBaseline.AdvanceTurn advance to the coming boundary date rather than a fixed 365 days:
`while (_sim.CurrentDate < SimulationManager.TurnBoundary(_sim.CurrentTurn + 1)) { _sim.AdvanceDay(); } _sim.AdvanceTurn(decisions ?? _noDecisions);`

That runs:
- 0 days for a fork taken on the boundary day (live play's first touch);
- 365 - d days for the post-load fork;
- 365 days for the shadow built from the seed, and for the diagnostic's fork taken at the turn's start.

Both the diagnostic's order and GameController's order then stay exact.

Then add two cases to PolicyImpactLedgerDiagnostic:
- one in GameController's order (the days, then ledger.AdvanceTurn, then the played world's boundary). It should check that the fork matches the same world run from the seed, and that the fork's CurrentDate equals the played world's after every boundary;
- one where the shadow is forked mid-turn after a load.

File this as its own item, not part of F3. After the fix, F3's line "every world the game holds draws alike" is true for the post-load shadow.
