**Why this exists.** K251 asked for the fiscal reaction function in play (`GetFiscalReactionMultiplier`, slope 1.5, moving revenue about 0.6-0.7 points of GDP per point of debt above the seed with no rate moved) to be retired or re-sourced. Retiring it exposed two defects it had been masking from opposite sides. The item therefore grew to three parts, built and reviewed together on `wave/k251`:

1. **The FRF retired where the AI finance ministry governs.** It stays in the player-governed book, where it delays the driver-only lines' runaway (K269 (a)).
2. **The ministry's EU rule given the Pact's stop.** Before, the rule drove debt to zero: it never released past cuts, and below 60 % it released only to a balanced budget. Now:
   - above 60 % it asks the debt safeguard's pace (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263 Art. 7), and releases a faster fall at half the gap a year, capped at 1 % of GDP;
   - below 60 % the TSCG's 1 % structural deficit is the resting point;
   - the tax ratchet is symmetric: a release lowers the rates the rule raised before it gives back lines, and rates rise only while the rule is consolidating.
3. **K271, the revenue drift at unchanged rates.** This is the income tax's bracket creep: thresholds indexed to prices (nominal in Poland) while incomes grow with real wages, with the yield ratio climbing to 1.8-5.6 over a century. Now:
   - an AI government re-indexes its thresholds to incomes;
   - the player's book keeps the statute, and the USA keeps its CPI indexing;
   - Sweden's earned income credit price base amount follows the threshold scale (it stood nominal);
   - the ministry's own release is left out of the net-expenditure claw-back;
   - K271's top-marginal yield cap is withdrawn (K272).
