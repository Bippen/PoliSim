## The questions, answered

**(1) Once per country per day, never twice.** Confirmed. `ApplyApprovalDay` has one play caller: `SimulationManager.AdvanceDay`, inside the per-country loop, after the Phase 5 macro block and before `History.Append`. `AdvanceTurn` no longer reverts. The boundary formula carries only the tax impulse and the spending change. The shadow baseline and every impact-ledger fork step days through the real `SimulationManager.AdvanceDay` (`ShadowBaseline.RunDaysThrough`), and forks are built by a save round trip. `_daysRun`/`DaysIntoTurn` keep a fork made inside a turn, or on the boundary day, from re-running a day, so nothing duplicates the step. Every `AdvanceTurn` caller in `Assets/` also advances days; the only exception, `PolicyImpactLedger`, delegates to `ShadowBaseline`. The preview runs 365 steps on its clone. The clone has its own fresh ledger, value copies of `TaxTermStock` and `ApprovalEconomyStock`, and its own `TaxTermNet`, so nothing leaks into the real country.

**(2) Ledger and attribution.** Each daily term is computed once, applied and added once to the accruing ledger. `ClampLoss` takes each day's (after - before) - step, which is the clamp where one binds and the float rounding where none does. The ATTRIB sum identity is therefore exact by construction up to accumulator rounding. That rounding is roughly 1e-5 random-walk over 365 adds, against a tolerance of 1e-3. The boundary twin now reads only `BoundarySpendingEffect`, which `ApplyApprovalRating` also uses. `ApprovalAfterFormula` now means "the last step's approval, held at a bound once a clamped step left it there". That can only disarm the drift detector, never fire it falsely (N-4). No path I found records a term twice or writes approval without recording.

**(3) TaxTerm.** The term is not double-faded. The reversion reads base = approval + T. `TaxTerm.AdvanceDay` moves approval and T together, so the base is unchanged, and T returns only through its own fading. When the daily step clamps, `TaxTerm.AfterShock` carries the clamp into T exactly as every clamped writer does. `BookFormula` now reads approval live, right after the formula, and the formula's step from `TaxHikePenalty + SpendingEffect`. That is required, since `ApprovalAfterFormula` no longer means "after the formula", and it is correct on all three call paths (boundary, preview, `TaxTermDiagnostic`).

**(4) The economy stock.** `ClampShare` keeps the same semantics with explicit inputs: x = the base before the step plus the step, approval's own clamp shared where approval met a bound and the base did not. Retiring `BaseBeforeFormula` also removes its ulp read-back error. One declared change: the boundary's one-off step no longer gives the stock a clamp share. The floor and ceiling invariants still hold, because the stock does not move at the boundary.

**(5) Growth units.** Correct. `annualizedDailyGrowth` is the day's increment over period-open GDP x 100 x 365, an annual percentage point, the unit the authored 0.3 per point of annual gap is written in. Over a year the daily terms sum to 365 x 0.0023066 / 0.05 x 0.3 x gap, about 16.8 x 0.3 x gap, against an equilibrium of 20 x 0.3 x gap. Nothing tests the call site's units, though (N-5).

**(6) PreviewParity.** Dropping the assertions is not fully justified (N-1).

**(7) Save/load.** Every piece of per-day state is serialized: `EconomyState.ApprovalRating`, `TaxTermStock`, `ApprovalEconomyStock`, and `ApprovalLedgerAccruing` with its summed fields and `ApprovalAfterFormula` (Newtonsoft, public fields). Nothing transient was added, so a mid-year save loses nothing. A mid-year save from before this branch loads with `ApprovalAfterFormula = 0`, which only disarms the drift check for that period (N-4).

**(8) Performance.** The step is about 10 terms plus a short welfare-list walk, with no allocation (the lambda captures nothing). Across six countries x 365 days x the fork worlds it is a small fraction of `AdvanceDay`. Each preview adds 365 calls. The Desk computes two previews per refresh, and `EstimateBudgetBill` pays for two years of approval steps whose result it never reads. That is sub-millisecond: informational only.

**(9) Diagnostics.** (a) pins the daily persistence. (b1) checks figures independently of the scale. (b2) runs 8000 days live per country. A missing scale makes every country settle at a bound, so the "Sweden must be measured" clause fails it. The 5e-3 bar is above the float freeze band of about 1.6e-3 x 2. (c1) asserts every day for 400 days, and (c2) the half-life to within one day. (e) asserts exactly zero daily terms booked at two boundaries, with ATTRIB folded into failure. ApprovalVoteDiagnostic (6b)/(6e) check the stock and the reversion on every country-day; (8) re-sizes the clamp cases on one daily step. All of them can fail when false.

**(10) Policy Web and checks.** The readouts are arithmetically right (paid leave 1 point per week; drug policy 0.4 points per dial point; welfare sensitivity x (generosity - seed) / 0.05). The new player strings carry no MetaTextCheck pattern. `ConstantProvenanceCheck` enumerates `static readonly`, and `ApprovalReversionSpeed` and `SustainedApprovalTermScale` keep their DERIVED remarks. Static initializer order is textual and correct. Every new backticked `Type.Member` exists, and no live comment still names `AdvanceEconomyStock` or `BaseBeforeFormula`.

## New findings

### N-1 (Medium): PreviewParity no longer guards the list-held inputs, and its PASS line overstates what it asserted
Welfare, PaidLeave, DrugPolicy and Gini are now printed rather than asserted. The commit says THE CLONE AUDIT covers their inputs. It does not cover all of them: the audit marks value fields and primitive arrays, but tests a reference field only for "the country carries it, the clone carries null". `Country.BaselineWelfarePrograms` and `WelfarePrograms` are lists, and `BaselineWelfarePrograms` initialises to `new List<WelfareProgram>()`. **Failure scenario:** a later edit to `ClonePreviewCountry` drops the `BaselineWelfarePrograms` copy. The clone carries an empty, non-null list, so the clone audit passes. The preview's welfare term then reads the whole portfolio as a deviation from an empty seed: UBI at 100 % alone previews +3 x scale a day, a +60-point standing effect. Before this round the WelfareEffect assertion caught exactly this class (the BaselineGini class, three recorded appearances). The assertion is also recoverable. The four inputs are constant over the clone's 365-day loop: the preview runs no Gini update, and a no-op decision moves no dial or generosity. So assert `prev.X == DaysPerTurn x scale x term(real country at the boundary)` for each of the four, within float accumulation tolerance. Separately, the final line still prints "PARITY: 7 of 7 asserted terms match" where two are asserted.

### N-2 (Medium): the preview's year projects every approval term but the tax term's fading, so a draft tax move's approval cost reads about 2.3x too large
`PreviewTurnOnClone` now books the impulse into the clone's T and then runs 365 daily steps without `TaxTerm.AdvanceDay` ("the preview runs no tax-term fading, as before"). The base excludes T, so nothing in the loop returns any of it. **Failure scenario:** a draft VAT rise with impulse I. The Desk's "Approval" effect (`withDraft.ApprovalChange - preview.ApprovalChange`, GameController) shows -I a year on, plus the year's reversion of the rest. The game itself hands back 1 - 0.81^4 = 57 % of T over the same year, so at the year's end the true cost is -0.43 I. Before this round the preview reported the boundary step itself, and -I was the right figure for that instant. Projecting a year made the horizon a year for every term but T. The fix is one call: `TaxTerm.AdvanceDay(previewCountry, date)` before `ApplyApprovalDay` in the loop. Its fiscal-year reset nulls only the clone's own `TaxTermNet` reference, and its fading records into the clone's ledger. Related: `ClonePreviewCountry`'s comment "the preview closes no period, so it stands" on `ApprovalEconomyStock` is now false, because the clone's stock moves.

### N-3 (Low): LeverLivenessCheck's Loyalty case may lose its pressure before the roll (a correction to the first review)
`PutUnderPressure` plants approval at 30, the floor less 10, right after `AdvanceTurn`. `TryRollCabinetEvents` reads `UnderPressure` at the next boundary. Approval now reverts for 365 days in between, so at the roll it stands at E - 0.4305 x (E - 30). That is above 40 wherever the country's equilibrium E exceeds about 47.6. The same was true at 1676de7d, because the boundary formula ran before the roll, and the first review wrongly called this check unaffected. **Failure scenario:** in every country E > 47.6, no roll sees pressure, Loyalty 0 and 100 fingerprint the same, and the check logs DEAD ATTRIBUTE. That failure is loud, not silent. The remedy is to plant on the turn's last day, before `AdvanceTurn`.

### N-4 (Low): the drift detector's teeth thin out
`ApprovalAfterFormula` opens at the period's opening approval and then holds at a bound for the rest of the period. So a period that opens at 0 or 100, or that has any clamped day, disarms the twin-drift check for the whole period, the boundary twin included. A mid-year save from before this branch loads with `ApprovalAfterFormula = 0` (it was never set on an accruing ledger), which disarms that period too. Neither case can raise a false alarm. Separately, `ClampLoss` now carries 365 days of approval rounding (at most half an ulp a day, 3.8e-6 above 64). That exceeds the 1e-3 tolerance only if approval freezes near its equilibrium, with the step persistently under half an ulp and of one sign. In play, the growth input's daily quantisation noise (about 4e-5 a day) prevents that.

## What is right
The daily step is a clean single source: it computes, applies and records once, so there is no twin to drift. The constants return to their authored literals, with one `SustainedApprovalTermScale` applied at the step. The reversion, the tax term's fading and the economy stock share one daily persistence, built from `TaxTerm.DailyPersistence` by reference. The clamp is carried into T by the same `AfterShock` every writer uses. The diagnostics were rebuilt to measure the claims that matter, every day rather than at boundaries, and each one fails when its claim is false.

What blocks the merge is N-2, a player-facing approval figure this round made wrong by about 2.3x for tax moves, and N-1, a removed guard whose PASS line still claims seven assertions.
