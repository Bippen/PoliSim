## Verdict

**Not ready to land as is. There is one HIGH finding and several MEDIUM ones.**

The parts that are sound:
- The sourced coefficients are quoted correctly, and the page numbers are right.
- The bound holds by construction.
- Absorption preserves the sum.
- The Shapley ledger is exact.
- The no-campaign and empty-tally paths are byte-identical.

The HIGH is the AI re-scaling. The turnout half of `CampaignAi.Points` was converted to share points with a formula that grows with the absolute electorate. In Germany and the US the AI now ranks actions almost entirely on a turnout channel that the live count does not read (the plan's own §5(1)). That is the "dead scale" risk §5(6) names.

## HIGH

### H1. The AI's turnout term swamps persuasion and grows with the size of the electorate
**Where:** CampaignAi.cs:834-835 (`Points`) and :739-741 (the doors branch); CampaignPersuasion.cs:197-201 (`TurnoutPointsAsSharePp`).

**What happens.**
- Persuasion is now valued as `CACE·q·(1−e^(−E/N))`, which is roughly `CACE·q·E/N` and so depends on the share of the electorate reached.
- Turnout is valued as `enthusiasm/60 000` "turnout points". `EnthusiasmPerTurnoutPoint` is an absolute count of people. Those points are then converted at `100·s(1−s)·0.15/50` per point, so they stay linear in the absolute exposure E.
- The ratio between the two terms therefore grows linearly with N.
- Before this change both terms were linear in absolute pressure, so the ratio did not depend on N. This is a regression introduced by the re-unit.

**Figures.** These are hand-computed from the spec table at the smallest outlay: FlatCredibility 0.6, q = 1, s = 0.2, EnthusiasmValue = 1, and N = valid votes as `LiveCampaignSetup` stages it. The ratio is enthusiasm points over persuasion points.

| electorate | TV | Digital | Social | Interview |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweden, N 6.5M | 1.8× | 2.3× | 5.1× | 6.6× |
| Germany, N 46.5M | 12.7× | 16.6× | 36.4× | 47.4× |
| US, N 155M | 42× | 55× | 121× | 158× |

- One US interview is worth +2.23 share points to the AI against a sourced persuasion effect of +0.014.
- That US interview carries 46.5 "turnout points". The attribute is clamped to 0-100 (`TurnoutModel.Multiplier`), so a first-order reading at that size is meaningless.

**Why it matters.**
- `ToTurnoutBonus` has no caller anywhere in Assets.
- `GotvModel.PartyTurnout` takes one shared enthusiasm scalar.
- The plan's §5(1) says enthusiasm and mobilisation "reach no live count".
- So outside Sweden, every AI personality now chooses on a value the world pays at 0. The doors branch has the same scale problem through `eligible/N`, which is less severe because doors are an absolute count of knocks.

**Fix.** Pick one:
- (a) Value turnout at 0 in `Points` and in the doors branch until 5b. That is honest, because the world counts 0.
- (b) Make the turnout term relative to the electorate (for example `turnoutPoints` from `enthusiasm/(N·k)`), clamp it to the attribute range, and re-measure `CampaignAiHarness` on the German and US stagings, not only on Sweden 2022.

In either case, add a diagnostic check that persuasion's share of the AI's expected value for a TV ad and an interview is within a stated band across Sweden, Germany and the US.

## MEDIUM

### M1. When every party campaigns equally, share moves from large parties to small ones in multi-party systems
**Where:** CampaignPersuasion.Apply, CampaignPersuasion.cs:150-170, together with an effect that is the same absolute number of points for every party.

**What happens.**
- Party p ends with `e_p − s_p·Σ_{q≠p} e_q/(1−s_q)`.
- With two parties this cancels exactly, which is the KB18 setting.
- With eight parties it does not. I gave each Swedish 2022 party 0.40 pp, which is about the impersonal plus earned saturation every AI reaches. Result: S −0.56 pp, SD −0.27, M −0.22; V and C +0.18 each, KD +0.22, MP +0.23, L +0.25. The sum stays exactly 1.
- That is a structural drift toward fragmentation, as large as anything a player can do. It lands in every idle game, which bears on pass criterion (e).

**Fix.** Scale each party's effect by the logit slope, `e_p = CACE·q·c·s_p(1−s_p)/0.25`. That leaves a 50/50 two-candidate race unchanged, which is the sourced case. Under the current `Apply`, equal effort then cancels exactly: `s_p(1−s_p) − s_p·Σ_{q≠p}s_q = 0`.

The alternative is to keep the form, declare the drift and measure it against criterion (e). Either way it is Elias's reading to rule on. Today it is neither declared nor measured.

### M2. Coverage can exceed the share of the electorate a party can reach at all
**Where:** `CampaignPressure.ShareEffects` and `BoundsPp` (CampaignActions.cs, the new `ShareEffects`/`BoundsPp`), which pass `setup.NationalAudience` as N.

**What happens.**
- A national act's audience is multiplied by `StandingShare(p)` (CampaignRun.cs:842).
- Local acts concentrate on a single region.
- Coverage is still computed as `1−e^(−E/N)` over the whole country.
- Example: a party standing in 15 % of the electorate (CSU in Bavaria, SSW) that piles up about one national-N of exposure over 56 days gets coverage 0.63. It can reach at most 0.15, so its effect is about 4× too large.
- The (4)(b) bound uses the same coverage, so the bound check cannot catch this.

**Fix.** Use `N_p = StandingShare(p)·N` and `c = (N_p/N)·(1−e^(−E/N_p))`. Ideally also keep a per-region tally for the personal class. At minimum, cap c at the party's reachable share.

### M3. The AI values every contact as if it were the first; the run saturates
**Where:** CampaignAi.cs `Points`. Its own comment says "first-order, the party's earlier contacts not netted".

**What happens.**
- Daily social posts alone in Sweden reach an E/N of about 1.7 over the campaign. The marginal coverage of the last post is then e^(−1.7) ≈ 18 % of the first.
- All impersonal contacts share one class coverage, so TV, digital and social posts saturate together.
- The AI therefore keeps paying for the classes it has already saturated and undervalues the personal class, whose coverage is still low.

**Fix.** The party knows its own actions. Carry its per-class own exposure on `AiView` and value `ClassEffectPp` at E+e minus the value at E. Then re-measure the personality bars.

### M4. Old saves replay differently, with no version gate
**Where:** PreCampaignRun.Apply (offices now refused, TV buys now charged), the model change itself, and `RestoreCampaign` (SimulationManager.cs:5617-5665).

**What happens.**
- A run-up queue saved before this change replays as follows:
  - A queued OFFICE is silently refused ("REFUSED …" in the digest). The planned office vanishes, and the chest comes back larger.
  - Each queued TV BUY now costs 500 000. A later queued poll or hire can then be refused for lack of money.
- A save made mid-campaign replays through the new AI and the new vote model, so the restored world differs from the saved one. `AssertReplayLandedOn` logs an error.
- `PlayerCampaignRecord` has no version field.

**Fix.** Pick one:
- Add a record version that refuses or migrates a pre-5a campaign record, telling the player so.
- Or grandfather a pre-5a queue: skip a queued office with its price refunded, and leave an old TV buy uncharged.

State the chosen behaviour in the commit and the docs.

### M5. The new diagnostic is weak on the claims that matter
**Where:** Assets/Editor/CampaignPersuasionDiagnostic.cs.

**What happens.**
- (b) "inside the bound" cannot fail. `ClassEffectPp ≤ UpperOf·c` term by term, and `BoundsPp` adds up the same coverages. The same is true of the copies in CampaignAiHarness 4e and UsCampaignGeographyCheck.
- (c) rebuilds the final shares with the very functions the run used. It tests the plumbing only. The "compatibility values above 100" figure is printed, not asserted.
- (a) asserts only `|moved| > 0`. A move of 1e-9 passes. The defect being fixed (GA-5-1) was "within 0.1 point of idle", so this check does not test for its return.
- There is no US run and no regional-party run, so M2 goes unseen.
- Nothing reports how often the `min(CACE·q, upper)` cap binds. That cap is the new "clamp", and the "never saturating at the clamp" claim cannot be checked without it.
- There is no idle-drift or equal-effort check, so M1 goes unseen.
- There is no check on the AI's valuation magnitudes, so H1 goes unseen.

**Fix.**
- Assert (a) against a floor and a ceiling, for example 0.05 pp ≤ moved ≤ bound.
- Add a Germany run with CSU, and a US run.
- Print and bound the share of class-effects where the cap binds.
- Add an equal-effort check: give every party the same tally and expect a net move of about 0, or declare the drift.
- Add the H1 ratio check.

**The bound.** Each class effect is at least 0 and at most `UpperOf·c` (quality is at least 0 and the persuasion multipliers are positive). So |own − against| ≤ own + against ≤ `BoundsPp`. No party can exceed its bound.

**Shares stay in [0,1] and `Apply` preserves the sum.**
- For a normalised input, Σ of the subtractions = `e·(1−s_p)/(1−s_p)`.
- Absorption uses the original shares, so the result does not depend on party order.
- The negative clamp and renormalisation act only as a guard.
- Tiny N gives c → 1, so the effect tends to `CACE·q`. N ≤ 0 gives 0. Huge N gives c ≈ E/N. Nothing produces NaN.

**Where:** `Scaled` (CampaignPersuasion.cs:184), read with `Coverage(…, reach)` (:123-127).

**What happens.**
- Within its reach a party's logit slope is s_loc(1−s_loc), where s_loc = s_nat/r. The code uses s_nat(1−s_nat).
- CSU, 2025 vintage: about 6 % national and about 37 % in Bayern, with r about 0.16. The code's factor is 0.23 where the in-region factor is 0.93, so CSU's effect is about 4× too small.
- SSW: about 0.2 % national and about 3 % in Schleswig-Holstein. Its factor is about 15× too small.
- CDU, standing outside Bayern (about 19 % national, about 22 % where it stands): 0.62 against 0.69, so it is barely affected.

**Fix.** Scale by `s_loc(1−s_loc)/0.25` with `s_loc = clamp01(s_p / r_p)`. Exact equal-effort cancellation then holds only among parties with the same reach, which is the honest statement for a federal ballot. At minimum, declare it beside M2's note.

### N3. The run pools quality per class, so the AI's marginal value is not the run's, and the run is non-monotone in contacts
**Where:** `ShareEffects` (CampaignActions.cs:402-424) uses the class's MEAN quality, Σq/ΣE, times the coverage of ΣE; `Points` (CampaignAi.cs:828-832).

**The arithmetic.** Adding a contact (e, q_new) to a class that already holds (E, q̄) changes the run's effect by, to first order:

  PC(q̄)·ΔCov + CACE·(e/(E+e))·(q_new − q̄)/norm·Cov(E+e)

- The AI values only the first term, PC(q_new)·ΔCov.
- At saturation ΔCov tends to 0, but the second term does not. It is negative for any contact below the class mean.

**Example.** Sweden, impersonal class: daily posts at q̄ = 1 bring E/N to about 1.7 (Cov 0.82). Add a further post at q = 0.5 with e/N of about 0.053, so e/(E+e) is about 0.03:
- the coverage term is 0.28 × e^(−1.7) × 0.053 ≈ +0.0027 pp;
- the quality term is −0.28 × 0.03 × 0.5 × 0.82 ≈ −0.0034 pp;
- so the run's net change is about −0.0007 pp.

The AI scores the same post at +0.14 × 0.0097 ≈ +0.0014 pp: positive, while it lowers its party's share. A high-quality television ad late in the campaign is undervalued by the same term, in the other direction.

**Why it matters.** The new doc comment ("the run's own arithmetic at the MARGIN", CampaignAi.cs:816) overstates what the code does. The non-monotonicity is a property of 5a's form, which neither review flagged before.

**Fix.**
- Either value Δ of the run's own class effect: carry Σquality per class next to `OwnExposureByClass`, and difference `ClassEffectPp` at (E+e, Q+qe) minus its value at (E, Q).
- Or change the run so that a new contact adds q_new·ΔCov to a stock, with no retroactive re-averaging.

The second is the better model ("persuadable once" by whichever contact reached them first) and makes the run monotone. Either way, re-measure the personality bars; the commit records no bar run.

### M-r1. Residual M5: the independent bound is loose, and the staged M2 check is a tautology
**Where:** CampaignPersuasionDiagnostic.cs:177-180 and :201-228.

**What happens.**
- `AllowedMovesPp` uses the UNSCALED bounds, which are built on the upper limits. The slack against a real move is about (upper/CACE) × 1/(4s(1−s)): roughly 3× for S and up to about 25× for L's earned class.
- So the check fails only on gross errors. It would not catch the M1 scaling being dropped, or the CACE being replaced by its upper limit.
- The (M2) staged check, `bound ≤ 2·Σ upper·r`, holds by construction, because `BoundPp = upper × Coverage(…, r)` and `Coverage ≤ r`. It tests nothing new.
- The (a) "sourced expectation" is printed but not asserted.

**Fix.**
- Compute `allowed` with the scaled bound: `B_q·4s_q(1−s_q)` in both terms.
- Assert (a)'s move within a stated factor of its sourced expectation.
- Replace the reach check with a hand-computed CSU coverage from its staged exposure.
