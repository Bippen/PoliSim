## Findings

### Medium

**M1. The response's reference is the in-game statute, but the Ageing Report's path assumes that Sweden's and Italy's indexation goes on past the dates the statute has published.** The statute's own steps are therefore counted twice exactly where the item says they no longer are. The participation path and the pension line also read two different laws.
- **Where:** `Assets/Scripts/Data/PensionParticipationResponse.cs:75` (`ReferenceAge(id, year) => PensionAgeStatute.AgeInForce(id, year)`) and `Assets/Scripts/Data/ParticipationCohortPath.cs:79` (`Eu`).
- **The two laws differ.** `PensionAgeStatute` carries Sweden flat at 67 after 2032 and Italy flat at 67 y 3 m after 2028 (the `Carried` state). The AR 2024 baseline projects under the age linked to life expectancy. The annex's own Table II.1.92 is titled "Public pensions … – Retirement age linked to increases in life expectancy", and II.1.93 "Constant retirement age scenario" is the alternative to it.
- **What the AR path carries.** Italy's 55-64 band rises +16.5 pts and its 65-74 band +23.2 pts by 2070; Sweden's 65-74 rises +12.1 pts (the prep tool's own summary).
- **Failure scenario.** In 2045 a player passes a bill setting Sweden's age to 68.
  - The response measures from the carried 67 and adds +0.256 pts to the 15+ rate (headless (b2) Sweden 2045).
  - But the AR path for 2045 already contains the participation of the riktålder rising past 67. The bill's year is paid for twice.
  - The same holds for any Italian bill after 2028.
- **The two laws in the no-policy run.**
  - The labour force carries 55-74-year-olds working as if the age were around 69 to 70.
  - The pension line's headcount (`SpendingDrivers.StatutoryPensionAge`) counts everyone at or above 67 y 3 m as a pensioner.
  - Italy's (d2) 2052-61 closure (0.83 → 1.09) rests partly on this.
- **Status.** The implementer raises this as an ASK (K278), not as a fix. It is a real defect against K276's own statement ("the statute's own path is not counted twice").
- **Options:**
  - read the AR's projected statutory age for these two countries as the reference;
  - or extend the indexed statutes' paths to match the AR's assumption;
  - or rule that the carried figure stands and accept the double count by name.

**M2. ParticipationCohortDiagnostic (c) never reads the EU five's actual labour force.** It reads a construct built from the same rates that (a) checks, and it leaves out the 65-74 band where most of the change is.
- **Where:** `Assets/Editor/ParticipationCohortDiagnostic.cs:302` (`model = lf2064[y] / lf2064[baseYear]`) and `NoPolicyCentury.LabourForce2064At`.
- **What it compares.** The EU check is Σ(band × `RatesAt`) over 20-64 on the pyramid, against II.1.26. It does not use `PotentialOutput.LabourForce` or the state's rate, which are what the model's labour input and wage base read.
  - The prereg admits the rates "match the sources by construction". What is left is the pyramid against EUROPOP2023.
  - The USA's (c) uses the state's 15+ labour force, and it is the only line that fails.
- **Failure scenario.** Suppose `MacroSystem` regressed to anchor on the seed rates, or the state's rate stopped following the anchor (a lever term, a clamp). The EU (c) would still pass, because it never touches the state.
- **What the run shows.** On the state's 15+ index the EU five depart from the II.1.26 index far more than 3 %:
  - Italy 2050: 0.9571 against 0.9068 (+5.5 %);
  - Italy 2070: 0.9147 against 0.8566 (+6.8 %);
  - Poland 2070: 0.6669 against 0.6982 (−4.5 %).
  
  Part of that is the perimeter (15+ against 20-64). But the 65-74 band, where Italy's AR change is +23 pts, is exactly what the 20-64 perimeter leaves out.
- **Better holds.** The ruling names II.1.15, employment 15-74, which K210 already reads. II.1.28 (participation 20-74) is also on disk. Either would hold the model's own labour input on a comparable perimeter.
- **Prereg.** This is what the prereg fixed, so it is not a breach of the prereg. It is a check that passes while "the labour force against the sources" could be false.

### Low

**L1. The composition natural rate still weights by the seed's participation.**
- **Where:** `Assets/Scripts/Data/UnemploymentRateByAgeTable.cs:54`.
- **What happens.** FT-8 (`MacroSystem.cs:579-588`) books the structural rate's change as "trend": it moves neither unemployment nor the excess, and the comment says "the natural rate reads that drift instead, as a composition effect". Since K276 the trend includes the AR/CBO rate path. `CompositionRate` uses `ParticipationRateTable.For(id)`, the seed's rates. So the rate path's change in the mix of the labour force (more 55-74-year-olds, whose unemployment rates differ) reaches neither unemployment nor the natural rate.
- **Scale.** Small, but undeclared.

**L2. Old saves carry the old meaning of two fields, and nothing records it.**
- **Where:** `Country.PensionParticipationApplied` and `StructuralParticipationAtLastBoundary`, read by `MacroSystem.cs:1055-1062` and `:584-588`.
- **Failure scenario.** A save from before K276 is loaded in 2032. Germany (67 from 2031) or the USA (67 from 2027) holds a positive `PensionParticipationApplied` from the statute's step.
  - On load the response is now 0, so `SyncPensionParticipation` takes that response off the state's rate at once.
  - The next boundary books that drop inside deltaP as cyclical, while deltaTrend jumps to the year's projected rates.
  - The result is a one-time fall in unemployment on the first boundary after the load.
- **What is missing.** No format bump and no migration note. Say so in the record, or seed the two references on load.

**L3. The prereg is not in git.** It exists only in the implementer's scratchpad.
