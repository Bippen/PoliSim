## M

### M-1 - The fading and approval's reversion both give back the same lost points, so a tax rise ends up raising approval for about a decade
- **Where:** `MacroSystem.cs:2336`, `reversion = ApprovalReversionSpeed * (NeutralApprovalRating - state.ApprovalRating)`, recorded the same way at :2397, together with `TaxTerm.AdvanceDay` (TaxTerm.cs:130-140).
- **The intended path holds day by day.** I walked a +2-point VAT rise at a boundary (s 1.5, impulse 3):

  | Day | T | Approval change | Recorded as |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | D | 3 | −3 (in the formula) | `TaxHikePenalty` −3 |
  | D+1 | 2.993079 | +0.006921 | `TaxTermFading` |
  | D+2 | 2.986173 | +0.006906 | `TaxTermFading` |
  | D+3 | | +0.006890 | `TaxTermFading` |

  - So approval = base − T, and the ledger's identity holds: the fading is a post-clamp actual in `TermSum`, and `ClampLoss` is measured against `FormulaTermSum` only.
- **The problem is the boundary reversion.** It pulls toward 50 the approval that already carries −T. The term "base" therefore also recovers r·T at each boundary, on top of the hand-back of all of T.
  - For a boundary impulse I, T at the next boundaries is 0.43 I, 0.185 I, and so on (Σ ≈ 0.75 I). The reversion adds about 0.05 × 0.75 I ≈ 0.04 I of approval that was never lost.
  - That overshoot then decays at the reversion's 5 % a year (half-life about 13.5 years).
  - Because both mechanisms recover the shock, the long-run integral of a rise's approval effect is about zero: the early penalty (about 1.2·I point-years) is repaid by a later bonus of the same area.
- **Failure:** GA-2-12's restoring bill (23 effective points, I = 34.5) leaves approval about 1.3 points ABOVE the no-move run from year 2 to about year 15. A cut (mirror −17) leaves it about 0.65 below. Once q15 reads approval into the vote, an old rise helps the incumbent at the next election. That is the wrong sign, and it is not declared anywhere: TaxTerm's summary says only "its reversion untouched".
- **Fix (pick one, and state it at the constant):**
  - (a) Revert the base, not the reading: `reversion = r * (50 - (approval + country.TaxTermStock))`, mirrored in `RecordApprovalAttribution`, where the twin audit will catch a one-sided edit. This changes the reversion's form, not its speed, so Q3's ruling ("the speed stays out of A-LOOP") is arguably untouched.
  - (b) Ask Elias to rule the overshoot acceptable until MF-13 rebuilds the reversion, and declare it in TaxTerm's ⚠ Declared readings.

### M-2 - The clamp creates and destroys approval: an impulse is booked into T in full while approval clamps, and T is later handed back in full
- **Where:** `TaxTerm.Book` (:104-109) is called before, and independently of, three clamped writes:
  - `ParliamentSystem.cs:636`: `Clamp(approval - taxTermImpulse, 0, 100)`;
  - `TaxTerm.ChargeLevyMove` :117;
  - the formula's whole-delta clamp, `MacroSystem.cs:2338`.
  The fading is clamped too (:138), while T still shrinks.
- **The sign walk.** T > 0 is a penalty, and its fading is positive (approval up). T < 0 is a cut's mirror, and its fading is negative.
  - **Floor:** approval 0.5, a 10-point rise by bill (impulse 15). Approval goes to 0 (−0.5 actual), T = 15. Over the next year the fading hands back about +8.5, and +15 in the end. A government at the floor gains about +14.5 against the run with no rise. Raising taxes at the floor is rewarded.
  - **Ceiling:** approval 99.5, a 4-point cut (mirror −3). Approval goes to 100 (+0.5), T = −3, and the fading takes back 3. Net −2.5 against no move.
  - **Fading at a bound:** at 100 with T > 0, or at 0 with T < 0, T decays and approval does not move. That is silent forgiveness.
- **What holds:** approval never leaves [0, 100] and never goes NaN; the ledger stays exact, because it records post-clamp actuals. The drift is real, though, and the plan's pass list does not cover it. TaxTerm's summary declares the reading ("so T keeps the year's accounting"), but not its consequence, which is an exploit at the floor. The formula's clamp has bound in play before: ATTRIB carries a `formulaClamped` branch for it.
- **Fix:** book the post-clamp actual where the clamp binds:
  - the bill and levy paths: `T += approvalBefore - approvalAfter` when the clamped result sits at 0 or 100;
  - the formula path: book `impulse - max(0, share of ClampLoss)`, or simply book nothing beyond the actual when `ApprovalAfterFormula` is at a bound.

  The netting of a cut and its restore then holds everywhere except at a bound, which is the honest place for it to fail. The alternative is to declare the floor exploit in the summary and put it on Elias's list.

### M-3 - Moves no one booked (the carbon statute's step, the income line's indexation and bracket creep) enter the next net, and around the kink they misprice a booked move, both ways
- **Where:** `TaxTerm.Impulse` (:96-100) reads `Charge(to − opening) − Charge(from − opening)`. The `from` point already contains every unbooked move since the opening:
  - `CarbonRateStatute.AdvanceYear`, which writes `line.Rate` at the boundary before `ApplyTaxRateChanges`;
  - `TaxSchedule.EffectiveRate`, which moves with `PriceLevelAtLastIndex`, `RealWageIndexAtLastIndex` and the pyramid at each boundary index.
- **The effect.** The charge is convex: m·s left of the kink, s right of it. A drift d that moves the line across or toward the kink changes a later booked move's price by up to (1−m)·s·|d| = 0.75·|d|. This is not only a gift:
  - **Stealth charge:** cut 5, an up-drift of +0.2 at the boundary, then a restore of 5. The restore costs C(0.2) − C(−4.8) = 3.9 against the cut's 3.75, so 0.15 is lost. The plan's "a cut and its restore in one fiscal year: |Δ| ≤ 0.01" fails whenever a boundary falls between them. Sweden's real start puts the boundary about 17 days after 1 January, inside the budget season.
  - **Stealth gift:** after an unbooked +x step, a cut of x earns s·x instead of m·s·x.
  - In Germany, BEHG overwrites a passed figure at the 2026 boundary (`AdvanceYear` :136) with no booking. That restore is free.
- **Magnitude:** small in practice (tenths of a point for indexation, a point or two for a BEHG or Swedish-index step in dial per cent). It is declared in TaxTerm's summary, but the player cannot see it coming: the hover (PolicyWebRenderer :1334, :1347) talks of "the fiscal year's opening" and the opening is shown nowhere.
- **Fix:** either book unbooked moves into the opening, so that drift is not a government move (`opening[type] += Δ` at `CarbonRateStatute.AdvanceYear` and at the boundary index), which keeps the net equal to the government's own moves, or keep the reading and add one line to the declared readings with the bound 0.75·|d| and the failing pass criterion.
