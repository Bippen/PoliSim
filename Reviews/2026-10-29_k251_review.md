## High

**H1. The EU rule's fixed point is zero debt: no release above 60 %, release to a headline 0 below it.** `AiFinanceMinistry.cs:90-93` - above the 60 % reference `EuRequiredAdjustmentPercentOfGdp` returns `Max(safeguardShortfall, resilience)`, never negative: when the ratio falls faster than the safeguard's pace the rule owes nothing and gives nothing back, and the earlier cuts and rate rises are levels the indexation never returns (the class's own doc, line 28). Below 60 (`:84-88`, `EuReleaseTargetBalancePercent = 0`, `:52`) a surplus is released only to a headline balance of 0 and the band [-1, 0] is held. With a balance averaging zero the ratio decays at the nominal growth rate forever. Measured (branch, all-AI, frf_play): France falls 127.5 (y10) -> 94.9 (y20) -> 55.8 (y30), 3-5 points a year against a safeguard asking 1, on a primary surplus of 3.9-5.7 % of GDP held for twenty years (y10-y30) while the ministry writes nothing; Italy 131.6 -> 109.8 -> 80.2 (2.2-3 a year); then below 60 France 55.8 -> 7.8 (y100), Germany 43.0 -> 10.1, Sweden 21.7 -> 12.8, balances oscillating around 0. The Stability and Growth Pact and Regulation 2024/1263 set a ceiling path (net expenditure, the 1 / 0.5-point safeguards, the 3 % reference); they do not demand a 5 % primary surplus for twenty years, which is outside the historical record of sustained surpluses, and they do not drive debt to zero. TSCG Art. 3(1)(b)/(d) defines "balanced" as the country-specific MTO, with a structural deficit of up to 1 % allowed where debt is significantly below 60 % - releasing to the -1 % limit gives a fixed point of roughly 1 / 0.035 = 25-30 %, not 0. In the base the reaction masked this from the other side (cutting revenue as the ratio fell below its seed); retired, the rule is the whole reaction and it is mis-specified. Failure scenario: any game played past year 30 shows France, Germany and Italy at AAA with near-zero debt and primary surpluses the player is told are "the treaty". Fix, two lines in the rule then re-measure: above 60, when the fall exceeds the pace, release the excess fall back toward the pace (or let the lines grow to the net-expenditure benchmark); below 60, release toward `-EuLowDebtDeficitLimitPercent` rather than 0 (or hold any balance between -3 and 0). Until then the item's premise - "the rule is the government's reaction, sourced in statute" (SimulationManager.cs:695-697) - does not hold.

**H2. Retired, a revenue drift with no rate moved by anyone runs unopposed in the AI world.** Branch, all-AI, revenue as % of GDP year 1 -> 100: Sweden 42.4 -> 55.8, Germany 40.2 -> 55.8, France 44.6 -> 56.3, Poland 36.7 -> 73.3. The drift is underlying (the idle Sweden player book with the reaction off: 42.4 -> 56.0), and the ministry adds a ratchet on top: `ApplyEuRule` calls `RaiseHouseholdRates` (`AiFinanceMinistry.cs:174`, `:214-227`) whenever a line cut is clamp-limited, including below 60 % when the balance falls under -1 (`:87`), and the release side (`RestoreLinesUniformly`, `:198-211`) only restores lines, never lowers a rate. (I could not print the rates from frf_play to split the two; Poland's 73 % suggests the ratchet is real.) Failure scenario: the player, governing Sweden, compares with Poland at year 60-100 and sees a government taking 64-73 % of GDP. Before the change the floor-pinned reaction hid this (Poland 39.6 % at year 100 on the base). This should be filed and measured before the AI book loses its only counterweight, or the release side should lower the household rates it raised before it restores lines.
