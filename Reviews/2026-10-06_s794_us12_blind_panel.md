## The final specification

### Rationale

The base is the precedent design. R-US18's preregistered form is what C15 asks for, and its block was the most complete. Each departure from it is argued from the game's use or from what so few seats can decide.

- Step 1 compares misses cycle by cycle: a method is recommended only where it is no worse in every cycle, and a parting of the cycles stops the rule whatever the sums. This makes 'K3 never overturns K1' true by construction (an earlier draft let equal sums hide a K1 loss), and B1 says plainly that K3's one power is to break a K1 tie.
- The C6 fallback and (a)'s midterm form follow from play. US-26 rolls the game's own share forward, so a 0/1 statewide base would freeze one of 100 seats for the whole game; the fallback is held to the identity like any base, so it can never hand a seat away at zero swing unnoticed. (a) at a midterm uses the share a game actually holds and moves it by the poll's change without re-solving, for R-US18's own reason; its declared alternative (x) changes only the form, never the priors, so a stop on (x) names one thing.
- The base count is the last count in which both sides stand, and the truth is the deciding count's winner: a one-side round (Alaska 2022) carries no two-party information, and a November count Georgia's runoff overturned names the wrong holder.
- The tie cascade starts at the holder's caucus, which play holds under both methods; a base winner would cost (a) a Senate base it never otherwise needs.
- K3 joins D because the game's second Senate election is a midterm and (a)'s midterm form can be tested nowhere else, and because one cycle of 33 seats cannot separate methods a seat apart. S2 compares only across two cycles, as R-US18's reading (ii) does, because one cycle's net count is one seat of noise.
- Misses decide because the text says so. |N| breaks a cycle-by-cycle tie and guards S2, because bills pass by party blocs and the net count is what play feels. Control is printed, never a stop. A full tie is asked, because the plan gives no ground for either method.
- The truth is the race winner by caucus. Every published 119th figure agrees with it, so the opening-day roster is printed, not a reading. A winner with no side stops the rule (S7), because it breaks the two-unit premise; the catalog can now carry a sourced 'neither' so S7 can fire from data rather than turning into a red bar.
- Premise failures that only Elias can resolve (S8, S9) are registered asks, not red bars, as C15 and R-US18 hold asked to be registered; only failures code or sourcing can fix fail the bar.
- (c) is in-sample by construction, so it is a design preference, never a winner; its later-field claim is restated as f = 1.0, with the roll-forward storage under (b) put in its ask. Both hybrids are buildable options and labelled in-sample by construction.
- Each convention the record cannot choose is a blind ask with a default and an S4 reading, the first run waits on the answers or the word to run on defaults, and B7 is one question with R-US18's choice 4. R-US19 blocks only US-16, which also waits on US-8, US-6, R-US18 and US-15, so the critical path loses nothing. The ruling is conditioned on R-US15 (a) (P12).
- No choice rests on an expected 2024 outcome. This judge opened neither scratch map folder, read only the catalogs' header and comment lines, the R-US18 block, house_by_state.csv's Alaska 2022 row and house_districts.md (House data) and checked that the saved state pages name pre-2023 holders; it computed no method on any seat. A grep of senate_record.md for 'reach' incidentally displayed one aggregate roster-count line for 2026-09-06; no choice here uses it. The plan's from-memory remark about 2024's split tickets is used by no choice, and (a-record) is printed only for the stated fairness reason.

### The rule

The block goes in docs/specs/USA_STAGE_PLAN.md between <!-- R-US19 RULE BEGIN --> and <!-- R-US19 RULE END -->. Its digests: SHA-256 over the UTF-8 bytes (no BOM, LF only) of the lines strictly between the markers. The preregistered digest comes first, committed before the catalog step and any instrument output, then one per amendment naming Elias's ruling.

R-US19'S RULE - US-12's measurement decides how the game elects the Senate's seats.

THE TERMS
- A side is REP or DEM. A candidate's side: R gives REP; D gives DEM (printed labels); I gives the conference named by his senate_seats.csv holder row's caucus (R-US10 (a)), and none if he holds no seat or his caucus is N; any other party or write-in has none.
- A race's deciding count: the runoff wherever one was held under state law (Clerk's marks or a saved certified state page; else BILLED); a ranked count's last round; otherwise the general, fusion summed. Its base count: the last count of that sequence in which both sides stand, each side summed. gen_r, gen_dc, final_r, final_dc, final_r_top, final_dc_top and final_none_top are generated by Tools/us_senate_races_prep.pl, never typed.
- Cycles: K1, 2018->2024 Class I (presidential); K3, 2016->2022 Class III (midterm), read from the saved Clerk statistics before the first run; K2, 2020->2026 Class II, BILLED. D = {K1, K3}.
- A cycle's count: its target year's regular full-term races of its class (K1 33, K3 34, in the default seat set).
  - A special to another class is printed.
  - A same-seat, same-day special is never counted, with every method's call on it asserted equal to its call on the full term.
- A seat's base: its latest race of record to its state and class before the target year, the full-term race where one seat had two that year; a race of record missing from the catalog is BILLED.
- V(E) = sum votes_r / sum (votes_r + votes_d) over house_by_state.csv's 50 rows of E, never recomputed. A January runoff takes its general's year.
- x(base) = final_r / (final_r + final_dc) where both are positive. Otherwise it is the state's own-line House two-party share in the base's year (house_by_state.csv), and failing that the state's two-party presidential share of record at the latest presidential year at or before it.
- (b) = x(base) + V(T) - V(base year), unclamped.
- (a):
  - at a presidential T, the state's two-party share from R-US14 (a)'s derivation from the previous presidential election P at T's true national shares;
  - at a midterm T, a_s(P) + V(T) - V(P), with a_s(P) P's own derivation from its previous presidential election at P's true national shares.
- A call is REP above 0.5 and DEM below. At exactly 0.5: the caucus side of the seat's holder at the end of the polling day; failing that, the Senate majority with the Vice President.
- No change: each seat to its base winner's side.
- Asserted: every base of D has a share. Every base whose winner has a side has x > 0.5 exactly where that side is REP and < 0.5 exactly where it is DEM, substituted shares included; a failure is S8.
- The truth: the winner of the target race's deciding count, by side. Z: the count's seats with no truth. O: its seats whose winner has no side.
- M = the seats of the count called wrong (Z never; O for every method).
- N = the signed distance of the called REP seats from [R, R+Z+O], positive meaning REP over.
- Every comparison is exact, on integers, as written. Equal is never better.

STEP 1 - (a) against (b).
- If M(X) <= M(Y) in every cycle of D and M(X) < M(Y) in at least one, code recommends X (by M).
- If M(X) < M(Y) in one cycle of D and M(Y) < M(X) in another, the rule STOPS (S1), naming each cycle with its M, whatever the sums.
- If M(a) = M(b) in every cycle of D: X if |N(X)| <= |N(Y)| in every cycle and |N(X)| < |N(Y)| in at least one (by |N|). Otherwise TIE: the rule does not decide.
- Asserted: the rule never recommends a method with more misses in K1 than the other.

STEP 2 - control. Only where D has at least two cycles: if step 1 picked by M, the rule STOPS (S2) if the pick's |N| is strictly above the other's in every cycle of D. Where D has one cycle, S2 is printed and counts as not firing. After TIE or S1, both directions are listed there.

STEP 3 - (c) is printed, in-sample by construction. It is in no step and never recommended.

STEP 4 - the declared readings. Steps 1-2 are run again under each, one at a time:
- (i) D = {K1};
- (ii) the specials in the count (Nebraska's 2024 Class II on its 2020 base; K3's specials to other classes);
- (iii) targets with a side absent (gen_r = 0 or gen_dc = 0) out;
- (iv) bases without both sides at 1/0 by the base winner's side;
- (v) seats whose base lacks a side out;
- (vi) each side's strongest candidate alone in the base count (final_r_top, final_dc_top), in every race;
- (vii) V_dc(E) = sum final_r / sum (final_r + final_d) over house_districts.csv's 435 races replaces V(E) in (a) and (b);
- (viii) (a) by R-US14 (b), proportional;
- (ix) (a) by R-US14 (c), plain additive;
- (x) (a) at a midterm by re-derivation: pi* = pi(P) + V(T) - V(P), N(T) = ((1-o)(1-pi*), (1-o)pi*, o), then R-US14's method from (a)'s own derived q_s(P) with the weights it was derived under; asserted to return a_s(P) at V(T) = V(P).
The rule STOPS (S4) if any reading changes the outcome, naming it. S4 compares outcomes less S4, S7, S8 and S9, and, where a reading's D has one cycle, less S2.

STEP 5 - two sides. The rule STOPS (S7) if a cycle of D has O > 0, printing it under both scorings: a miss for every method alike; and O moved into Z.

STEP 6 - premises waiting on a ruling (registered, never a red bar):
- S8: a base of D (or a play base read with it) whose share sits at or across 0.5 from its winner's side, two-sided or substituted. Steps 1-5 run with the share as computed, flagged.
- S9: a race of D whose deciding count rests on no saved page. Steps 1-5 run over the cycles fully read, printed.

THE OUTCOME is step 1's verdict ((a), (b), TIE or S1) plus the stops that fire (S2, S4 with its readings, S7, S8 with its bases, S9 with its races). Its figures are not part of it.
- A run is decided only when step 1 picks a method and no stop fires.
- The rule reaches (a), (b) or an ask, never (c).
- The verdict line is generated, never typed:
  - decided: 'By R-US19's rule, code recommends (a)|(b): M and N per cycle and summed; discordant d_ab:d_ba, sign-test p = ...; the same under every declared reading; (c) is in-sample by construction', with 'within the record's noise' added where p > 0.05;
  - otherwise: 'R-US19's rule does not decide: <each stop with its figures> - asked.'
- R-US19 is asked with the full table whatever the outcome, with (c) beside it as a design preference.

THE STOPS' OPTIONS, each with 'amend the rule as ruled' besides:

- S1: (a); (b); (a) on presidential days and (b) at midterms; (b) on presidential days and (a) at midterms - each hybrid labelled 'in-sample by construction (each cycle's winner)', with the hybrid-midterm row beside it; source K0.

### The two-party basis

C4, THE CAUCUS SIDE, SUMMED.
- The DEM side is the D candidates plus each independent whose seat row caucuses with the Democrats; the REP side is defined alike. It is generated as gen_dc / gen_r (the general's ballot) and final_dc / final_r (the base count), because votes_i pools every independent.
- Reason: R-US10 (a) is RULED, and in a two-unit poll both kinds of DEM-side vote are the DEM unit's.
- 'Side absent' is always tested on this basis (gen_* for a target; no two-sided count for a base). Vermont is asserted two-sided.
- Labels only: printed only. Vermont would have no DEM side.
- Non-Republican summed: printed only. It puts no-side independents on the DEM side, which no saved page supports.
- The strongest candidate alone: reading (vi), B6, in every race with several candidates on one side.
- A nominee printed on a non-party line is sided by his printed label or caucus only; a nomination no saved page shows is not read.

C5, MAINE:
- Its one printed count is the deciding count and the base count (§793 reading 7: a majority winner, no ranked round decided it).
- Re-tabulation is not sourced and unnecessary, so it is not a reading.
- Its DEM side sums the Democrat and the caucus independent. Reading (vi) takes the strongest alone.
- The identity is asserted on it.

### Method (a)

(a) THE STRAIGHT TICKET. A seat's REP share is its state's derived two-party presidential-level share that day. No Senate vote is read; the tie cascade reads the seat's holder, never a Senate base.

INPUTS (generated catalogs, re-read by GeneratedCatalogCheck, never typed):
- president_by_state.csv: the 51 statewide rows only. DC enters the derivation as a jurisdiction, never as a seat. The ME/NE district rows are not read.
- house_by_state.csv: only for the midterm form's V (defined under (b)).
- senate_seats.csv (extended back by the catalog step, see (b)): only for the tie cascade's holder.

PRESIDENTIAL FORM (K1: P = 2020, T = 2024; in play, every presidential polling day, US-16):
1. N(T) = (D, R, O) = (sum votes_d, sum votes_r, sum votes_other) / sum votes_total over T's 51 rows. At measurement these are the TRUE 2024 national presidential shares, read by UsStateSwingCheck's own reader, never retyped. At measurement (a) reads the true value of the national measure play feeds it, as (b) does.
2. Priors p_s: P's row shares, all others pooled. Weights: P's votes_total (US-4, DECLARED).
3. q_s by R-US14's method. While R-US14 is asked, the deciding reading is R-US14 (a): RegionalVoteModel.RegionalSharesByUniformSwing, unchanged, its residual asserted < 1e-9. S4 reading (viii) is R-US14 (b), proportional (UsStateSwingCheck's (ii)); reading (ix) is R-US14 (c), plain additive (its (iii)). When R-US14 is ruled, the block is amended to the ruled method and (viii)/(ix) become printed only.
4. a_s = q_R / (q_R + q_D), stored unclamped, clamped for printing only. A zero two-party total is a data defect. A nominee floored at zero gives 0 or 1, flagged.
5. The call: REP if a_s > 0.5, DEM if a_s < 0.5. At exactly 0.5, THE TIE CASCADE (shared with (b)):
   - the caucus side of the seat's holder at the end of the polling day (at measurement: the roster on 5 Nov 2024 for K1, 8 Nov 2022 for K3, from the extended senate_seats.csv);
   - if the seat is vacant or its holder has no side, the side holding the Senate majority that day, the Vice President's vote counted.
   It is printed when it fires. Reason: play always holds the holder; a base winner would cost (a) a stored Senate base it otherwise never needs.
6. Every seat of one state on one day takes the same a_s (asserted) and the same call except where the cascade fired; those exceptions are printed.
- In play on a presidential day, a_s is the presidential count's own state shares after E1's factors (US-8): the night's map. Each Senate call is the state's two-nominee plurality. The card states that (a) cannot produce a split ticket on a presidential day.

MIDTERM FORM (K3: P = 2020, M = 2022; in play, US-27). Anchored on the poll's change, plain additive, never re-solved:
  a_s(M) = a_s(P) + [V(M) - V(P)].
- a_s(P) is the share (a) held on the last presidential day, unclamped. In play US-16 stores it with V(P), the poll's REP/(REP+DEM) before E1's factors that day. V is (b)'s V: own-line by default; V_dc under reading (vii).

### Method (c)

### The premises

**P1.** Two sides: every seat is REP or DEM, an independent counted with his conference. A winner with no side (including a sourced 'neither') fires S7 and is never assigned.

*Reason.* R-US10 (a), RULED. The chamber holds two blocs (US-15), and a third unit is US-15's and SP-6b's question.

**P2.** One poll: the game's only national input is V = REP/(REP+DEM), the own-line House vote, before E1's factors. On a presidential day the presidential count's state shares are added. There is no Senate or state poll.

*Reason.* R-US15 (a) (asked, recommended §789); facts section 2. At measurement each method reads the true value of the measure play feeds it.

**P3.** (a)'s state shares come from R-US14's method, its (a) while R-US14 is asked; the other two are readings.

*Reason.* R-US14 is asked. Its ruling cannot silently move R-US19's outcome.

**P4.** Anchored, plain additive, never re-solved. Each share is stored unclamped with its day's V, and the next election starts from it.

*Reason.* R-US18's form and R-US11 (a)/US-26: an anchored level never jumps at the first game election, and additive forms telescope exactly.

**P5.** Plurality decides in play. Runoffs and ranked rounds are not modelled; the record's deciding count names the truth and the base winner, and the base count (the last count in which both sides stand) gives the share.

*Reason.* With two sides a plurality is a majority (US-11's scope); a one-side round carries no two-party information.

**P6.** The zero-swing identity holds on every base of D whose winner has a side, substituted shares included.

*Reason.* It is asserted. A failure means (b) is not anchored there; it is the registered stop S8, never substituted silently and never a red bar.

**P7.** A caucus read in the 2023-2026 window is carried back to the same person's earlier race.

*Reason.* No saved page dates an earlier caucus. DECLARED, as §792 applies it over the window.

**P8.** No candidate effect outside (c): no incumbency, retirement or quality. (b) carries a seat's previous candidate effect forward; (a) carries none.

*Reason.* E1's principle and the one-poll game.
