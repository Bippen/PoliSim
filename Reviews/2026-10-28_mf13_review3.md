## The questions, answered

**(a) TaxTerm.AdvanceDay on the preview clone.** It touches only the clone.
- `TaxTerm` holds only readonly constants.
- `IsFiscalYearStart` reads the static, read-only `FiscalYearData`.
- `Reclamp` writes the clone's `TaxTermStock`.
- The fiscal-year reset nulls `previewCountry.TaxTermNet`. That is the clone's own dictionary, copied in `ClonePreviewCountry`, and nothing after the loop reads it: the `PolicyPreview` initializer reads only state, ledger and locals.
- `RecordTaxTermFading` goes to `EnsureAccruing(clone)`. That returns the clone's fresh ledger, which `ClonePreviewCountry` builds non-null, so it never touches the real `ApprovalLedgerAccruing`.
- `ApprovalLedgerRecorder` has no static state.
- The clone audit runs on a separately cloned memberwise copy, not on the previewed clone, so a nulled net trips nothing.

The order matches the game. The real boundary books the impulse on day D, and `AdvanceDay` runs `TaxTerm.AdvanceDay` first on days D+1..D+365, with `ApplyApprovalDay` later in each day. The preview books at `CurrentDate` and then runs fade-then-step on `CurrentDate+1..+365`. Over the year this hands back 1 - 0.81^4 = 57 % of T, as the game does. What remains different is the declared projection: the clone's state is held at the preview's resolution for the year. That is K191's accepted form.

**(b) SustainedTermsForDay / ApprovalDayTerms: is it byte-identical?** Yes.
- Each of the nine expressions is textually the old one: the same operands, the same association, and `scale` read once into a float local, as before.
- The result is stored into a float struct field and read back into float locals. On x64 SSE (the Editor, Mono x64 and IL2CPP players) every float operation and store rounds to binary32 either way, so the copy cannot change a bit.
- `step`, `economy`, `reversion` and the ledger adds are untouched and in the same order.
- `GetWelfareApprovalEffect` is called once, at the same point relative to the other reads.

The trajectory digest cannot move from this extraction.

**(c) PreviewParity's YearOf exactness.** The four inputs are constant across the clone's year. They are also constant from the real country's instant to the clone's loop.
- **Gini:** the only writer of `State.Gini` is `ApplyGini`, reached only through `ApplyGiniDaily`. The preview never calls it.
- **Dials:** `PaidFamilyLeaveWeeks` and `DrugPolicyLevel` move only on an override of 0 or more, and `PolicyDecision.None()` carries -1.
- **Generosity:** generosity moves only through `WelfareGenerosityOverrides` (empty under None; FinancePartner writes only spending lines and tax overrides) and `ParliamentSystem.ApplyBillResult`, which `PreviewTurn` does not run.
- **Inside the loop:** `TaxTerm.AdvanceDay` and `ApplyApprovalDay` write only approval, T, the economy stock and the ledger.

The clone's welfare list is a deep copy in the same order, so `WelfareSum` iterates identically. Both sides call the same `SustainedTermsForDay`, so each day's term is bit-equal to the real country's. The clone's ledger fields open at 0 and receive exactly 365 float `+=` of that value: `RecordApprovalAttribution` writes only `TaxHikePenalty`, `SpendingEffect` and `ClampLoss`. `YearOf` performs the same 365 binary32 adds from 0f. The match is exact, not merely within the 5e-4 tolerance, and it cannot flake.

The teeth are real for welfare (the N-1 scenario: an empty seed list makes the clone's term differ), paid leave and Gini. DrugPolicy has none in the default world: every country is seeded at the neutral 50, so the term is 0 on both sides whatever the clone carries. `DrugPolicyLevel` is a value field, though, and THE CLONE AUDIT already marks and reads it back. Informational only.

**(d) (e2): tolerance and teeth.** By construction the check is an identity, not an approximation. Each day `annualizedDailyGrowth` = (GDP after the block - GDP before) / `GdpAtPeriodOpen` x 100 x 365. The lapse overlay is restored before the block and re-applied after it, and the event shock sits inside the block. So the daily increments telescope to identity-level close minus open over a fixed denominator. The float subtraction of two values within a factor of two is exact (Sterbenz). The potential rate is sampled once per day after the step, on the same day it was read, and on the boundary day before `AdvanceTurn`. So implied minus realised is only float accumulation noise, on the order of 1e-5.

Against that, 0.1 + 5 % is very loose, but it still has teeth.
- A raw, unannualised call site makes implied the mean growth divided by 365. That fails wherever |realised| exceeds about 0.105, and `unitMeasured` demands at least one country-period beyond 0.5.
- A fraction (0.02 for 2 %) fails the same way. A value 100x too large fails too.
- Only a same-unit variant passes, for example a per-day-base percent, which differs at second order. That is correct.

The GDP-open capture is right:
- **Period 1:** the diagnostic reads GDP at creation, and the game seeds `GdpAtPeriodOpen` in `GetOrSeedFiscalPeriod` on day 1, before any GDP writer runs. The only writers are the two national-accounts forms, the event shock and the lapse overlay.
- **Period 2:** both read after `AdvanceTurn`, which writes no GDP (FT-9).

The one way it can fail spuriously is the US lapse overlay (R3-2).

**(e) LeverLivenessCheck re-plant.** It is the right day. After the 365th `AdvanceDay` (its approval step done), approval is set to floor - 10 and recorded as an event in the still-accruing ledger. `AdvanceTurn` then runs the boundary formula and `CloseAtBoundary`, and the cabinet roll follows. With a no-op decision the formula carries only the spending one-off, far below the 10-point margin, so `UnderPressure` holds at the roll. T is zero on this path, so the plain write (no `TaxTerm.Shock`) leaves no T to reconcile, and the ledger audit still closes. The test still works. Both Loyalty runs plant identically, so any fingerprint difference comes from the cabinet roll alone. Pressure now also stands at the first boundary, which the old placement missed, so the test is slightly stronger.

**(f) N-7.** Correct. `WelfareEffectDelta(country, p => p.Type == type ? sensitivity x generosity : 0)` sums the implemented live programmes of the type minus the implemented seeded ones. That is this type's exact share of what `GetWelfareApprovalEffect` reads, and `SustainedEquilibrium` turns it into the standing effect. A current programme that is not implemented contributes 0, as the old explicit ternary did, and several programmes of one type are now summed on both sides. `WelfareEffectDelta` and `GetWelfareApprovalSensitivity` are `internal`. That is fine: there is no asmdef, so `PoliSim.UI` compiles into the same Assembly-CSharp. The one closure allocation per readout is negligible.

## New findings

### R3-1 (Medium): with the year's fading in place, a tax rise in the budget draft previews as an approval GAIN on the Desk
The player's tax moves reach the preview only through the budget draft (`GameController.RecomputePolicyPreview`: `BuildPlayerDecision` carries the rate lever alone). The Desk's figure is `withDraft.ApprovalChange - preview.ApprovalChange`.

`PreviewTurnWithBudgetDraft` applies the draft with `ParliamentSystem.ApplyBillResult` before calling `PreviewTurnOnClone`. `ApplyBillResult` charges the draft's impulse at once (`TaxTerm.ChargeApproval`: approval -= I, T += I). `PreviewTurnOnClone` then captures `approvalBefore` after that charge, at its top. So the -I is outside the measured change on the draft side.

At bb073cd2 the two previews differed by about 0. The base (approval + T) was the same on both clones, so the reversion was identical and the cost was invisible. That figure was wrong but neutral. This round's loop now hands back 57 % of T during the year on the draft clone only.

**Failure scenario:** the player drafts a VAT rise with impulse I. The Desk shows Approval +0.57 I, a green upward arrow. In the game, a year on, approval stands 0.43 I lower than without the draft. The sign is wrong and the size is off by I. `_cachedApprovalChangeRaw`, the Statistics projection, is off by I as it was before, but now in the flattering direction. Review 2's N-2 scenario assumed the impulse rode the formula (the decision path); for the budget draft it does not.

**Fix (small):** in `PreviewTurnWithBudgetDraft`, read the clone's approval before `ApplyBillResult` and fold the bill's immediate move into the returned `ApprovalChange`. One way is to let `PreviewTurnOnClone` take the origin approval; another is to add (approval after the bill - approval before it) to the result. The draft then previews -I + 0.57 I = -0.43 I, the figure the game will reach. A PreviewParity case should pin it: a draft with a known impulse must preview -(1 - 0.81^4) x I against the standing preview, within float tolerance.

### R3-2 (Low): (e2) reads the GDP level with the US lapse overlay on, while the game's growth reads it off
The game's daily increments and `GdpAtPeriodOpen` are identity levels: `RestoreOutput` before the block, `LiftOverlay` around the period re-open. The diagnostic's `gdpOpen` and `gdpClose` read `State.GDP` with the overlay on.

**Failure scenario:** in a run where the USA is lapsed, or still in its four-quarter profile, at one boundary and not the other, realised carries the overlay's level difference and implied does not. A CBO-scaled level loss of a few tenths of a per cent is a few tenths of a point of annual growth, against a tolerance of 0.1 + 0.05 x 2 = 0.2. With Sweden as the player under seed 777 no lapse is expected, because the lapse needs the US procedure to apply. So this is deterministic and latent, not a flake. **Fix:** read `k.State.GDP + k.LapseOutputWedge` at both captures.
