## Pass 2 (dbf9a8e1)

**Method.** I read `git diff 0c669fe6..dbf9a8e1` (10 files) in full, re-read `UsVetoGate`, traced `Present` → `PresidentOn(answeredOn)` → `OverrideOf` → `RecordVeto(CurrentDate)` → `RecordLost(CurrentDate)`, checked every new UI branch with a null docket, and re-ran the gate.

**1. M1 is correct.**
- Only `AnswerDocket` writes an answer, and it refuses a player not in the presidency.
- A held bill cannot be answered later by another player-president: a change of presidency decides an unanswered bill by the incumbent's rule that day and clears the docket.
- `Present(answeredOn)` sees the player as president on the answer's day.
- Nothing is applied twice: the docket is nulled before `Present`, and the site applies and removes the bill once.
- An `AnsweredOn` of MinValue falls back to `CurrentDate`; only the unreleased 0c669fe6 build could write one.
- The override counts the recorded sides; `RecordVeto` and `RecordLost` use the step's day. A sustained veto acted on after the player has left office is therefore not the incoming government's lost day. That is defensible, but worth one DECLARED line.
- AI and non-US paths are unchanged.

**2. Every new UI branch is reached only with a docket.**
- `ResolvesIn(days, null)` gives exactly the old text, the drawdown's comma form included.
- `LawRowName`, the calendar's `AddBill`, the acts loop, the tax slip and `UsBudgetStatusText` keep the old path when the docket is null.
- Other countries' words are unchanged.

**3. PassageYear.** The new branch fires only when the docket is set, and it returns an int with no float store.

**4. Findings at dbf9a8e1 (Low only).**
