## Findings

### H1 - `UsDebtLimitLiveDiagnostic` is registered in no bar, so the item's only runtime evidence never runs
- **Where:** `Assets/Editor/CheckSuite.cs` (near :232, beside `UsMajorityReconciliationDiagnostic`). The name appears nowhere under `Assets/Editor` or `Tools/` outside its own file and three doc comments.
- **What breaks:** the diagnostic is the only thing that holds `UsDebtLimit`'s transcribed constants to their sources:
  - `SeedDebtUsd = 35960000000000.0` (`UsDebtLimit.cs:414`) is a copy of `WorldFactory`'s US debt seed;
  - `VintageSubjectToLimitUsd`, the four law figures, the headroom and the span are copied from the document's Data block.
- **Scenario:** a later commit re-seeds the US debt. `UsDebtLimitCheck` fails the cheap bar on the document's engine block. Someone rewrites the block with `WriteBridge`. `UsDebtLimit.BridgeRatio` keeps the old seed, so every reach and bind date in play is wrong and stays green. Separately, the done-when's "bars" cannot be met by a check nothing calls.
- **Fix:**
  - Enrol the diagnostic in the simulation group (it walks three years of a Sweden world and a US world).
  - Compute `SeedDebtUsd` at run time rather than transcribing it, for example from the `WorldFactory` constant itself, or from the seed debt captured on the country at creation. The claim convention says "Nobody transcribes".

### M2 - The raise's title carries a world-dependent dollar figure, so FORK-2 cannot replay the player-president's answer
- **Where:**
  - `UsDebtLimit.cs:585`: `BillTitle` appends `" to " + Usd(country.DebtLimitUsd + RaiseUsd)`.
  - `SimulationManager.cs`, `AnswerDocket` / `SamePassage` (~:1852-1872): an answer is replayed by the passage's title, day, chamber and origin.
- **Scenario (a 2024 US start, the player president):**
  1. The player touches a family before 1 January 2025.
  2. The except-world reinstates the limit at *its own* debt (`Track`, `UsDebtLimit.cs:527`). Its `DebtLimitUsd` therefore differs, so the raise's title differs on the same vote day. The reach is 2 January in every world, so the binding day matches.
  3. The player signs on the day, but no match is found in the fork. There the raise waits out the ten days and becomes law unanswered.
  4. Each of those days `AdvanceDebtLimitDay` arms the cut. US-14 measured it at about 33 % of non-interest outlays (§838), roughly $5 bn a day, so about $60 bn over the period.
  5. The ledger books that debt gap to the family the player touched.
- **No-policy world:** it is handed no answer by FORK-2's rule, so it carries the same ten-day cut against a played world where the player signed at once. This is FORK-2's DECLARED rule, but this item makes it material.
- **Fix:**
  - Make the title world-independent ("Reconciliation (the debt limit): the limit raised by $5.0 tn", or keyed by the raise's ordinal) and keep the figure on the slip.
  - Ask whether a no-policy world should sign a raise at once rather than wait out the docket.

### M3 - A lapse's deferred outlays, repaid on a day the limit binds, are cut and lost for good
- **Where:**
  - `UsLapse.cs:104-107`: the first funded day returns the whole of `LapseDeferredOutlays` and zeroes it.
  - `SimulationManager.cs:8629-8630`: that sum enters `governmentSpending`.
  - `SimulationManager.cs:8873`: it enters `CutToday`'s `nonInterest`.
- **Scenario:**
  1. A lapse runs while the measures run out.
  2. On the first funded day the cut is armed, and `CutToday` takes `nonInterest - (revenue - interest)`. That absorbs almost the whole repayment.
  3. The owed back pay is never paid after the raise. `DebtLimitCutBillions` and the slip's "CUT SO FAR" swell by it.
- **Fix:** while the limit binds, keep the repayment owed (leave `LapseDeferredOutlays` standing) and pay it from the first day a raise is law. Or exclude the repayment from the cut's base. Either way, declare the choice.

### M4 - The cut is the book only: a cut of about a third of federal outlays that moves nothing but the debt, DECLARED rather than asked

  - Meanwhile, book the cut as its own accrual line, or share it across the component accruals pro rata, so the report adds up.
