### M-2: the Gallup conversion is a sound first-order correction, but it is untested, and its invariance assumption fails exactly where events matter. For Elias. `EventSalience.cs:136`, `EventSalienceFit.cs:34`

**Is "mentions per respondent" the right correction?** Mostly, yes. A share on either instrument is the respondents naming the issue, which equals the issue's part of all mentions times the mentions a respondent gives. The bases are read consistently:
- Gallup's printed Total sums the individual codes, not the NET rows: 41 + 72 + 3 = 116, not 136, so Total is the codes' sum;
- both sums include "no opinion" / "don't know";
- the duplicate Cyprus label is excluded.

**The strongest objection.** The correction assumes an issue's part of the mentions is the same on both instruments. Two things break that:
1. **Saturation.** The relation between a top-k share and a top-1.37 share is not linear. For a minor issue the ratio of shares is near the ratio of mention counts. For a surging, dominant issue, the one most people name, it tends toward 1: whoever names it at all names it first. So for exactly the large adverse events that land the full jump, a 0.71 scale understates the US move.
2. **Granularity.** The USA's seed (`StanceModel.cs:206`, Economy 0.11) is the larger single Gallup code. Gallup splits the economy over about eleven codes, and its own "ECONOMIC PROBLEMS (NET)" row runs 30-41 %. EB's two broad items are not split that way. The converted Economy jump therefore lands on a sub-code's level while having been measured on a whole topic.

**Further mismatches.** The bases are not matched in time or population: Gallup Feb-Aug 2024 against EB 2015-2025 across the EU. The seed is a 2026 month while the base is 2024's.

**What would settle it.** An out-of-sample test is cheap. Gallup's own trend table carries the 2015 immigration rise, the 2020 coronavirus rise and the 2022 inflation rise. Fetching those columns and comparing the observed US jump with the EB jump times 0.7134 would test the reading directly. Until that is done, the DECLARED reading should say it is untested. Whether to do it is Elias's call, since K69 named the method.

### L-2: K95's comparison weighs the event's PEAK jump against the standing LEVEL, and during a ramp's flat phase that level is the first wave's. `EventSalience.cs:301` against `:309`

**Scenario.** A Natural Disaster lands on Sweden: 0.15 standing for 123 days, ramping to 0.20. During the flat phase a Cold Snap arrives. Its severity is 0.8, so its jump is 0.16, which is at least 0.15, so it LANDS:
- the shift becomes 0.27;
- the hold restarts at 339 days;
- the big event's unfinished ramp (0.05) is replaced by the small one's (0.04).

A smaller event has extended a bigger standing hold, which is what K95 forbids. The same mismatch lets a ramping class stack three times on one day in (3), where a flat class stacks twice.

**Not reachable in play today.** `TryRollEvent` fires at most once per country per turn (`SimulationManager.cs:6793`), `DaysPerTurn` is 365 (`:268`), and every hold is shorter (Health's 352 is the longest). Every hold has ended before the next event, so in play K95 only ever stops a small event adding to a larger decaying leftover. Its "never extends a hold" branch is unreachable, which Elias may want stated.

**The fix, if events ever land within a hold:** compare against what stands plus the standing ramp's unfinished rest, `ramp × min(hold, RampDays)`.

### L-3: a refit with a non-positive first wave and a later peak would land a NEGATIVE shift, or nothing. `EventSalience.cs:309, 316-317, 346`

`landed = severity × FirstLevelOf × scale`. If a refit gives FirstWaveJump ≤ 0 with a positive later peak (Security's per-step pattern could), there are two outcomes:
- landed < 0: an adverse event LOWERS salience on its day (the clamp allows down to -seed);
- landed = 0: the `== before` return skips the hold and the ramp, so the event lands nothing.

`SalienceFitCheck` checks `fit.Jump >= fit.First` (line 137), and (2r) checks `s[0] == first && first < peak`. Neither requires first > 0. A one-line guard in `SalienceFitCheck` (a generated ramping class has FirstWaveJump > 0) would close it. Unreachable on today's data: 0.063 and 0.15.
