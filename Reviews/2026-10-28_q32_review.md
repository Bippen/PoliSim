## What holds (verified)

- **The spread (1).** `GetDebtRiskPremium` and `ConvexSpreadAt` are b2 (d - d*)^2 above d* = 0.0281 / 0.0006 = 46.83, zero below, uncapped. Units are percentage points of yield, the same as the old premium: DP 141 states one point of debt raises the spread "close to 3 bps" against the benchmark's 0.0291, so its spreads are in percentage points. The minimum agrees with the paper's "above about 50%". The slope crosses the benchmark at 95.3 %, which matches "beyond 95% of GDP". The Table 1 body did not come through pdftotext, so I checked the two coefficients against the paper's own derived statements, not against the table cells. The old constants are gone. No reader is left in Assets or Tools: `DebtClampDiagnostic` mirrors the new 1000 % guard, and MODEL_REFERENCE is rewritten. The premium has one consumer, `GetDebtIssuanceRate`, scaled by `RiskPremiumSensitivity` (the USA's 0.05 is unchanged). The euro countries do not double count: the ECB zone rate is the base, the spread is added once, and nothing feeds the spread back into the zone rate.
- **The money stores (2).** Debt has exactly two writers: `StoreDebt` (the daily balance and `ApplyOneTimeBudgetImpact`) and the haircut in `SovereignCrisis.Enter`. I grepped `Mathf.Min/Max/Clamp` on GovernmentDebt, TotalAssets, Budget and reserves across Assets/Scripts. What remains: the two runaway guards (booked to `GuardAbsorbedBillions`, with a `MONEY:` LogError); the fund's zero floor on a market loss (a loss beyond holdings, which N2 accrues as landed, so no money is lost); the draw clamped to the holdings; `ApplySwfDrawdownBillEffects`'s withdrawal clamped to the holdings (booked); and a UI deliverable figure. None of them discards money. The fund refusal is subtracted once, from both the fund and the spending argument, and is booked to the country only on the daily path. The preview books to a clone, and the turn form books nothing to `Crisis`. The overflow leaves the fund once and enters revenue once, as part of `swfDraw + swfOverflow`. So refused money stays in the budget exactly once, and nothing is double booked.
- **K212 (3).** `TriggerRatingOf` only reads: `GetSettledPosition` plus `EvaluateFrom`. It writes `Crisis.TriggerRating` and never `Country.Rating`. It is re-read only on a day `ReviewIfDue` set `LastReviewDate`. For every country except the USA it equals the standing rating.
- **The haircut on the ledger (3).** `RecordEvent` adds a dated event, so the yearly audit (sum of terms + sum of events + clamp = observed change) stays closed. `AuditInvariants` adds `HaircutBillions` to the daily two-books sum.
- **The overlap with the debt limit (3).** `SovereignCrisis.CutToday` reads the non-interest outlays after `UsDebtLimit.CutToday`. Both can run on the same day without cutting the same money twice.
- **Save (4).** Version 66. Any other version is refused before deserialisation. `Country.Crisis` is a public field graph on the World, so Newtonsoft persists every field. `FiscalPeriod.AccruedSwfDraw` rides the saved `FiscalPeriods`. ## Findings

### High

**H1. The default's haircut is booked as a budget surplus of 37 % of the debt, and the economic vote rewards it.** `Enter` records the haircut with `DebtLedgerRecorder.RecordEvent`. AF-17's `FiscalOneOff.FromLedger(country.FiscalLedgerAccruing)` turns every ledger event into a one-off of `-AppliedDelta`, and the year's report counts it: `BudgetBalance = AccruedBudgetBalance + oneOffs` (SimulationManager ~6811). Failure scenario: the USA defaults at, say, 250 % of GDP. The closed year shows a one-off line "Cruces and Trebesch's mean SZ-haircut ... COUNTED IN THE YEAR'S BALANCE" of about +92 % of GDP. `History.BudgetBalanceAnnual` (the sheet's series) takes it, and so does `FiscalVote.CloseYear`. `FiscalVote.OpenRatio` adds the ledger's events to the open year too, from the haircut day on. A government that defaults therefore reads to voters as having run the largest surplus in the game. A restructuring write-off is not a budget balance; it is an other change in volume (ESA 2010). The same write-off also corrupts the next rating review: `GetSettledPosition` derives the deficit from the debt stock's change, so the haircut reads as a huge surplus and the USA's rating jumps at the first review after the default. Fix: keep the haircut on the ledger (the audit needs it), but exclude it from `FromLedger` and `OpenRatio`, for example with an event kind flag. Then either net it out of the settled-deficit reading or declare that reading in a ruling.

### Medium

**M1. A crisis re-opens on a stale rating, without a review, the moment it ends.** `AdvanceDay` ends the phase (`today > EndsOn`) and then, in the same call, tests `Phase == None && TriggerRating == CCC`. `TriggerRating` is only refreshed on review days. The USA's exclusion is 1059 days from an 1 October review, so it always ends on about 25 August, off the review calendar, and re-enters on a rating up to 11 months old. That re-entry takes a second 37 % haircut and starts a new exclusion. The same happens to a programme entered off the fiscal-year start (for example on the first-day review, as the diagnostics do). This contradicts K141's reading in the code ("the day a scheduled review leaves the rating at CCC"). The diagnostics accept it instead of testing it: "a second default may open the day the exclusion ends". Fix: require `crisis.TriggerReviewedOn == today.Date` for `Enter`, or clear `TriggerRating` when a phase ends. Then add a check that no crisis opens on a non-review day.

**M2. The default's output cost lands outside the Okun window, so unemployment never sees it.** `SovereignCrisis.AdvanceDay` cuts `State.GDP` at line 407. `gdpBeforeMacroStep` is captured about 100 lines later, around line 509. `ApplyOkunsLawDaily` and `ApplyApprovalDay` read the day's growth from that capture, so a 2.6 % output loss moves no unemployment and no realised growth. FT-9 (section 404) moved `ApplyEventShockDaily` inside that window for exactly this reason. The constant's own comment claims "an event shock's treatment (`MacroSystem.ApplyEventShockDaily`)", which the code does not do. Fix: land the daily slice next to `ApplyEventShockDaily`, after `gdpBeforeMacroStep`, and keep the trigger and the haircut where they are.

**M3. The closed-crisis cut has no line in the year's report, so the closed book's lines no longer add up.** `crisisCut` reduces `totalSpending` but is never accrued to a period field. US-24's M4 gave the debt-limit cut its own line (`AccruedDebtLimitCut`, the "Debt-limit cut" row) so that the closed book's rows add to the recorded total. In a default year, or a withheld programme year, the Budget sheet shows the gross Mandatory, Welfare and other lines against a smaller recorded balance, with no row explaining the gap. Fix: add `AccruedCrisisCut`, a report field and a row, mirroring M4.

**M4. Neither new diagnostic runs in any bar.** `MoneyIdentityDiagnostic` and `DebtPathHoldDiagnostic` are not registered in `CheckSuite`. The money identity this item exists to hold is asserted only when someone remembers to run it. The suite's own note on PartyLeadershipDiagnostic records what that costs. Fix: register MoneyIdentity in the cheap bar (it runs about 12 years of a single world), and DebtPathHold wherever the century runs are already paid for.

### Low

**L1. The programme's "equal steps" path is attributed to the Portugal MoU.** `TargetFor` and the class header say "the path the Portugal MoU of 2011 took (5.9, 4.5 and 3.0 % of GDP: the entry deficit to the Treaty's 3 % in three equal steps)". The MoU's targets from Portugal's 2010 deficit (about 9.1 %) are not equal steps: 3.2, then 1.4, then 1.5 points. The MoU sources the three years and the 3 % end point. The linear path is a model reading, and should be tagged DECLARED or CONVENTION, not presented as the MoU's.

**L2. A slip line is wrong.** "The programme's loans carry no market premium while its targets are met." `OfficialLending` holds for the whole programme, withheld years included (K214). Suggest: "The programme's loans carry no market premium."

**L3. Where the spread applies is undeclared.** The regression is euro area 2000-19, measured over the Bund. It is applied to the USA, Sweden and Poland over their own policy rates, and to Germany (the paper's benchmark, so a spread over itself of 0.08 points at 63 %). Both are defensible, but neither is declared beside `SpreadDebtCoefficient`.

**L4. Stale comments.** `SwfReturnsDiagnostic`'s header still says SWF income sits inside the multiplier, which is no longer true for the draw under K213. `MoneyIdentityDiagnostic (a)` says the slope at 95 % is "above the benchmark's 0.0291", but it is 0.0289; the crossing is at 95.3 %. The assertion itself only checks the derivative.

**L5. DebtPathHold compares offset calendar windows.** It compares the model's change from its own start year with the DSM's 2024-to-2029 and 2024-to-2035 changes. That is declared, but the DSM baseline covers the aligned window (the model's start year to 2035). On accelerating paths such as Poland's (+3.6 to +3.9 points a year), the offset biases the comparison.

**L6. A programme entered on the first-day review gets a 3 % target from year one.** At that point no year of history exists, so `EntryDeficitPercent` falls back to the reference. This affects the diagnostics' Italy run, which enters on day one.

**L7. The preview cuts whole years or not at all.** It evaluates `Unfinanced` at today and applies the result to the whole year, so a year in which the exclusion ends is previewed fully cut. It is display only; worth a stated line.

## Summary

The convex spread is right in coefficients, units and placement. The fund ceiling and the runaway guards book every unit of money. Save format 66 and the clone are complete, and K212 and K213 are implemented exactly and without side effects. Two problems block the item. H1: the haircut, booked correctly on the ledger, leaks through AF-17 into the year's balance, the annual series, the economic vote and the next rating review. M1: the crisis re-opens on a stale review, which can take a second haircut without a review. M2 and M3 are real but contained; M4 is process.

## Verdict: NOT READY
