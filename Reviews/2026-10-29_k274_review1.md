### Medium

- **M1 - the player's USA under an AI head reacts to a partial year only (`AiFinanceMinistry.cs:213`/`:220-221`, reached from `SimulationManager.cs:1442` via `:3498`).** On the bill path (K274d), `TableGovernmentBudget` calls `Decide` on the USA's budget request day (`FiscalYearData.BudgetRequestDay`: the first Monday on or after 1 February), and the reaction reads `debt(now) - DebtRatioLastReport`, where `DebtRatioLastReport` is the ratio `Observe` stored at the last TURN BOUNDARY (`SimulationManager.cs:6824` still runs on the bill path). The US game's boundaries fall on its start's anniversary (`WorldClock.cs:95`: 2024-11-05), so each year's bill answers only the November-to-February change, and the February-to-November segment is never answered by any bill - the first difference does not telescope. Failure scenario: a US game in which the player's party loses the presidency; from the second request day on, the AI government's bill tightens by about a quarter of 0.054 x the year's rise, and the sum over the years is not "the slope times the ratio's rise since the AI took office" that the class doc, MODEL_REFERENCE and K274d declare. Nothing tests this path (`UsFiscalReactionDiagnostic` (c) covers only the player-governs case). Fix options: key the bill path's memory to the last bill's ratio (a stored field - save format), or table against the ratio at the last boundary on both ends; or declare it and ask.
- **M2 - the US reaction cuts mandatory lines (Social Security included) and raises income tax, the EU rule's levers, where the task asked for "the levers an AI US government has ... as UsDebtLimit and the budget procedure allow" (`AiFinanceMinistry.cs:237-238`).** `CutLinesUniformly(..., includeMandatory: true, ...)` applies one uniform percentage to every line, the `SocialSecurity` line too, and puts what the ranges leave on the household rates. The retired US rule (`67e9ae77`, `1bff4219^`: `CutLinesUniformly(country, toTake, discNow, includeMandatory: false, ...)`, "the shortfall is not made up: the statutes are spending-side") read the US levers as discretionary-only; the project elsewhere treats US benefits as statute (MF-14's 42 U.S.C. 415(i) guarantee; `UsBudgetProcedure` routes benefit levels and rates through reconciliation / a pension act, never an appropriation). Failure scenario: at 777 the AI USA's Social Security line is cut a little every year its ratio rises from year 2 to year ~70 (the cumulative cut is ~0.054 x 50 = 2.7 % of GDP spread over all lines), a benefit cut no AI president could make by appropriation. It is DECLARED (K274c, "the levers and their order are the EU rule's own"), so it is a ruling, not a hidden defect - but the hand-back should state the conflict with the retired rule's reading and with the task's lever clause plainly, and offer the discretionary-plus-rates variant (which needs its own prereg run, since it changes the path).
- **M3 - (B) hinges on a calendar convention by 0.15 point.** The task text says "debt/GDP at year 30 within a band of CBO's 2055 figure"; the prereg chose 2056 (opening 2026 + 30, `DebtPathHoldDiagnostic`'s rule) and so the band [150.076, 200.076]. Against 2055 (171.785, band [146.785, 196.785]) both seeds PASS (146.94 and 147.69). The prereg's choice is reasoned and was fixed before the run, and the implementer did not switch after the miss - correct discipline. But the record must say that (B)'s FAIL is a 2.4-3.1 point miss under one calendar reading and a 0.15-0.9 point pass under the task's own wording, so Elias does not read it as a large miss. Separately: the prereg's "What follows a miss" names Bohn's Table III quadratic as the follow-up; its slope is STRONGER at the model's debt (0.167 at d = 1.0), which would push year 30 further below the band. The hand-back's K274e (keep 0.054, re-scope (B)) contradicts the prereg's own registered follow-up; a re-scope of the acceptance after a miss is goalpost-moving unless Elias rules it. Only a weaker column (0.028-0.037) moves toward CBO; that would be a choice by outcome and needs its own prereg, as the hand-back says.
