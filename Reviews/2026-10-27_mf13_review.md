## The questions, answered

**(1) Where approval advances.** Confirmed. `MacroSystem.ApplyApprovalRating` has exactly one play-path caller, the boundary block of `SimulationManager` (the other is `PreviewTurn` on a clone). The boundary is `daysSinceEpoch % DaysPerTurn == 0` with `DaysPerTurn = 365`, and `TaxTerm.DaysPerQuarter = DaysPerTurn / 4f = 91.25`, so a turn is exactly four quarters by definition. A once-a-turn AR step keeping 0.81^4 = 0.4305 of the gap is the right per-step persistence; the constant folds to 1 - 0.81f^4 = 0.5695. The `TaxTermStock` exclusion in the reversion base is untouched, so the tax term still returns only by its own daily fading.

**(2) Is every sustained term found?** Yes, for the formula. Its terms: growth gap, the four misery gaps, Gini gap, welfare (deviation from seed via `WelfareEffectDelta`), paid leave gap, drug policy gap: all fourteen constants are scaled. The terms left unscaled are one-offs: the tax impulse (a move's charge, booked into T); the spending change (`ApplySpendingLineChanges` measures the decision's own delta after `IndexSpendingLines`, and pinned lines skip indexation, so automatic growth never enters it); and every `TaxTerm.Shock` / `ChargeApproval` caller (events, cabinet decisions and reshuffles, foreign meetings, failed bills, enactment costs, the debt-limit and reconciliation outcomes). I found no other per-turn reader of a standing state that writes approval. The classification of the growth term is a judgement call (see L-2), and streams of one-offs are sustained in expectation (see M-2).

**(3) Readers of the constants.** The Policy Web's stat edges and welfare edges use within-group ratios, so they are unchanged. `ApprovalVote.EconomyStockPersistence`, `ClampShare` and `BaseBeforeFormula` all read `ApprovalReversionSpeed`, so they stay consistent. No AI rule compares an approval sensitivity against a threshold, and `PreviewTurn` is UI-only. The "Approval pull: X pts" lines in the Policy Web dial and welfare summaries now print a figure 11.39x larger (see L-1). `ApprovalVote`'s class comment now carries a wrong figure (see M-3).

**(4) Does the new diagnostic fail when false?** Mostly yes. `Const` fails on a missing or non-const field. (b1) pins each pre-MF-13 figure independently of the model's scale. (b2) needs Sweden measured. (a) fails if the speed reverts to 0.05. The diagnostic has three weaknesses: (i) it is not registered in `CheckSuite`, so no bar ever runs it (M-1). (ii) Its sustained list is a hand list: a fifteenth sustained term added later and left unscaled would not be caught. (iii) (c2) "half-life equals the tax term's" is measured on boundary samples only, where the two agree by construction (M-4).

**(5) Provenance and comments.** `ApprovalReversionSpeed` is DERIVED, `PreMf13ApprovalReversionSpeed` is CONVENTION, `SustainedApprovalTermScale` is DERIVED, and the scaled sensitivities keep `[AUTHORED-DRAFT]`. ConstantProvenanceCheck is satisfied. Every backticked `Type.Member` in the new comments exists (`TaxTerm.QuarterlyPersistence`, `TaxTerm.DaysPerQuarter`, `TaxTerm.DailyPersistence`, `TaxTerm.HalfLifeDays`, `SimulationManager.DaysPerTurn`, `ApprovalVote.EconomyStockPersistence`, `MacroSystem.ApprovalReversionSpeed`, `MacroSystem.ApplyApprovalRating`), and both cited paths exist. One stale claim elsewhere (M-3) and one misleading claim in the new remark (M-4) remain.

**(6) Checks elsewhere that pin approval.** `TrajectorySentinelCheck` will fail. The dump hashes every public `EconomyState` field, `ApprovalRating` among them, and every country's approval moves from turn 1 (M-5). `TaxTermDiagnostic` (6f), (6g) and (7) compare a move run against a no-move run. The base (approval + T) is identical in the two runs, so their difference is -T at any speed, and I expect them to hold. `ApprovalVoteDiagnostic` s8 is re-sized correctly: each case's step is -1.5/-2.5, -0.65/-1.65 and their mirrors at the live speed, as at 0.05, and I checked the arithmetic. (6e)'s implied-neutral test is speed-generic. `LeverLivenessCheck` re-plants pressure after every boundary, and `CabinetPressureDiagnostic` plants and checks on the same day, so neither is affected. The ATTRIB audit tolerance (1e-3) sits far above float noise on terms of size ~30.

## Findings

### M-1 (Medium): the new diagnostic never runs
`ApprovalReversionDiagnostic` is not in `CheckSuite`'s table. `ApprovalVoteDiagnostic` and `TaxTermDiagnostic` are, at lines 561-562. No guard catches an unregistered diagnostic. **Failure scenario:** a later item edits `ApprovalReversionSpeed`, or adds a sustained term at the old 0.05 unit. The cheap and simulation bars both stay green because nothing executes the check written to catch exactly that. Fix: add `("ApprovalReversionDiagnostic", ApprovalReversionDiagnostic.Run)` to the simulation group.

### M-2 (Medium, a consequence for Elias to see, not a code defect): streams of one-offs were de-facto sustained terms, and their mean contribution falls by 11x
K135 keeps every sustained term's equilibrium and lets one-offs fade faster. But some one-offs arrive as a steady random stream, and a stream has a stationary mean of E[flow per turn] / speed. The event pool: `EventChancePerTurn` 0.40 for every country every turn, uniform over 52 events whose `ApprovalEffect` averages -1.21, so E = -0.485 a turn. That shifted mean approval by about -9.7 points before (/ 0.05) and shifts it by about -0.85 now (/ 0.5695). **Failure scenario:** every country's long-run mean approval rises by about 8.8 points with no policy at all. The same applies to failed-bill costs for AI governments and to the player's ~3.65 foreign meetings a turn. Cabinet pressure (`PressureApprovalFloor`) and the vote's level reads see a different world, and the commit message's "equilibrium unchanged" does not say so. This is what K135 literally rules, but the family diff should name it per country, and Elias should see the number.

### M-3 (Medium): `ApprovalVote`'s class comment now states a wrong figure, and the consequence behind it grew 5x
`Assets/Scripts/Elections/ApprovalVote.cs` item (6) says a successor to an unpopular government gains "about b x 30 x (1 - 0.95^4) ~ 0.9 pp" for nothing it did. It also says "Q3 rules approval's persistence out of A-LOOP (MF-13)". At the new persistence the windfall is 0.16 x 30 x (1 - 0.4305^4) = 0.16 x 30 x 0.966, about 4.6 pp. **Failure scenario:** a reader, or Elias at a ruling, sizes the inherited-residue windfall at under 1 pp when the game now hands a successor nearly 5 pp of vote over one term. The comment is a DERIVED claim transcribed (CLAUDE.md's no-transcription rule) and now false. It should be re-derived or referenced, and the windfall put to Elias as MF-13's consequence.

### M-4 (Medium): "a one-off shock's half-life is the tax term's own" holds only at boundary samples
The remark on `ApprovalReversionSpeed` and the diagnostic's (c2) both claim a one-off shock halves in about 300 days, like the tax term. The tax term fades daily. Every other shock is held whole until the next boundary and then loses 57 % in one step. **Failure scenario:** a failed bill on day 364 of a turn has lost 57 % by the next morning. The same bill on day 1 is still at 100 % 363 days later. An election held on a date (PS-1) reads approval that depends on where the shock fell against the boundary. The approval chart also shows a large overnight step every new year: a gap of 20 points closes by 11.4 in one day, where it closed by 1.0 before. Sustained levers behave the same way: 57 % of a lever's equilibrium lands in one overnight jump at the next boundary. (c2) passes by construction, because it samples at boundaries. Either reword the claim ("at the boundary samples") or rule whether approval's reversion should move to the daily step the tax term uses.

### M-5 (Medium): the no-policy baseline moves and the sentinel is not re-pinned
`TrajectoryBaselineDump` hashes `EconomyState.ApprovalRating`. Every country's approval moves at turn 1, because the speed changes the first boundary's step wherever approval is not at 50. `BaselineLabel` is still `form` with the old digests. The commit message says "sentinel re-pin owed". **Failure scenario:** the simulation bar is RED on this commit, against "ONE GREEN BAR PER COMMIT". The branch cannot land until the family (proposed `mf13`) is dumped, explained per country with `Tools/traj_diff.pl`, and the label and both digests are set with a reviewed row (CLAUDE.md: "A baseline that moves needs a reviewed row for both digests").

### L-1 (Low): the Policy Web's "Approval pull: X pts" now reads 11.39x larger with no unit change
`PolicyWebRenderer` dial lines for paid leave and drug policy, and the welfare summary, print the per-turn term. Drug policy at 100 now reads +11.39 pts where it read +1.00. Neither figure is the equilibrium (+20). The number is honest as "the next boundary's step from neutral", but its label never said "a year" or "at equilibrium", and it has changed meaning in practice from a nudge to most of the effect. Consider printing figure / `ApprovalReversionSpeed` as the standing effect, or adding "a year". (The welfare summary also prints the absolute level rather than the deviation from seed the formula uses; that predates MF-13.)

### L-2 (Low): the growth term is a rate, so in level terms it behaves like a one-off
The growth gap is a growth RATE against potential. A one-time GDP level drop (a recession year) produces a one-turn gap, not a standing one. The term is classified as sustained and scaled, so a -2-point growth year now costs -6.8 approval at once, where it cost -0.6. The integral over time is the same (0.6 / 0.05 = 6.8 / 0.5695 = 12 point-turns), so nothing is lost or gained in total, only concentrated. This is defensible under K135, since the term is paid every turn the gap stands, but it is a judgement Elias may want to see. Event GDP shocks now hit approval about 11x harder in their year through this channel.

### L-3 (Low): in-term lever effects grow 3-5x, so the old equilibria now bind in play
The pre-MF-13 equilibria were 20-turn asymptotes that a four-turn term never approached. UBI from 0 to 100 % generosity: after four turns it gave +11.1 points before (3 x (1 - 0.95^4) / 0.05) and gives +58.0 now (60 x (1 - 0.4305^4)). Drug policy 50 to 100 gives +3.7 before and +19.3 now. Through the vote's 0.16, that is about +9 pp from one programme in one term. R-Q1c's standing property, "no combined ceiling on the sustained gap terms (drug policy alone +-20 eq-pts)", moves from theoretical to reachable in a single term. It is ruling-consistent, but it belongs on the play-calibration list beside K134.

### L-4 (Low): stale plan text
`docs/specs/A_LOOP_PLAN.md` s2 still reads "approval's own reversion, 5 % of the gap a turn ... effectively permanent". s9 still lists Q3 as open-for-later. The plan is a dated plan document, so this may be acceptable as history, but a pointer to MF-13 would stop a future reader treating 0.05 as live.

## What is right
The derivation is correct and tied to its sources by reference rather than literals: the speed is built from `TaxTerm.QuarterlyPersistence`, and a turn is defined through `DaysPerQuarter`. The scale is applied to all fourteen sustained constants, the formula and its ledger twin read the same constants (so ATTRIB cannot drift), the one-off constants are untouched, and `ApprovalVote`'s stock follows the same speed. The s8 clamp cases were re-sized with correct arithmetic, so they exercise the same geometry as before.
