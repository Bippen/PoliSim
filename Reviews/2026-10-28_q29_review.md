## Findings

### H-1 (High) - the generator reads Germany's Ageing Report rows from the NEXT table: Germany's life-expectancy band and potential-growth path are wrong data

`arTable` scans rows `$hr + 1 .. $hr + 40` after a table's year header and keeps the LAST row whose code matches, with no stop at the table's end. The annex's sheet 4 places tables 35 rows apart (II.1.2 at row 37, II.1.3 at 72, II.1.4 at 107, II.1.14 at 457, II.1.15 at 492), so the window runs 5 rows into the next table: BE, BG, CZ, DK and DE are overwritten by the next table's rows. Of the five, only DE is hit. Confirmed against the sheet and the committed file:

- `LifeExpectancyLow[Germany]` (meant: men, II.1.2, 79.0 in 2022) holds 83.8 - the WOMEN's row (row 79).
- `LifeExpectancyHigh[Germany]` (meant: women, 83.8) holds 18.3 - II.1.4, life expectancy at 65, MEN (row 114).
- `PotentialGrowth[Germany]` (meant: II.1.14, 0.514 in 2022) holds 0.0626 ... down to -0.64 - II.1.15, EMPLOYMENT growth (row 499).

Failure scenario: (d2) holds Germany's no-policy potential growth to Germany's employment growth, and (d3) holds its life-expectancy gain to a band between women's e0 gain and men's e65 gain. The brief says the (d) bands "are expected to fail for DE/IT/PL"; for DE at least part of that failure is this parser, not the model, and a pass would be just as meaningless. The header's promise ("a pattern that does not match ... stops the script") does not cover a pattern that matches twice. No runtime path reads these three arrays (the diagnostic alone does), so play is not affected - but a sourced table carries wrong numbers under a SOURCED banner, and an item's acceptance reading rests on it.

Fix: stop the scan at the first match per code, or bound it to the rows before the next `Table II.` title; die on a second match; add plausibility asserts (e0 in 60..95, men < women, potential growth in -3..6). Re-generate, then re-read DE's (d) results before calling them expected.

### M-1 (Medium) - the long-run demand multiplier rises about 70 % and now rests on a CONVENTION

Algebra checked: the turn form is `Y' = A Y + B` with `A = rho + kappa (a - a_seed)` and `B = kappa (G + NX + DI) + (1 - rho - kappa gamma_seed) P`; the daily form uses exactly that A and B in the affine power slice, so D daily steps compose to the turn map at constant inputs (the daily clamp on A to [0, 0.99] cannot bind: it would need a - a_seed < -1.3). The identity is the same C (with the disposable-income delta) + I + G (k-scaled, deflated) + NX in both forms, and the seed references remove the seed's levels once, so there is no double count. Note that the new form with zero references does NOT reduce to the old one (it gives `sY + (1-s)P + (1-s)Y_id`); the old form survives only behind the explicit `GapSeeded` branch, which is correct for a v64-shaped world and the AI countries.

The steady-state multiplier of a sustained demand impulse (G, NX, DI) is `kappa / (1 - rho)`: 0.5 / 0.3519 = **1.42 for the USA**, 0.5 / 0.3911 = **1.28 for the EU five**. The old form's was `(1-s) / (1 - (1-s) a)` = 0.5 / 0.6 = **0.83** at a seed propensity near 0.8. A sustained 1-point rate rise moves a by -0.006 (0.6 x 0.5 + 0.2 x 1.5, over 100) and output by -0.85 % (was -0.50 %). Against the literature: Ramey (2019) puts government-purchase multipliers at 0.6-1.0; 1.3-1.5 appears only under slack or at the lower bound (Auerbach-Gorodnichenko). 1.28-1.42 before the Taylor rule's feedback is at the high end - defensible, but kappa is `OutputGapReversionSpeed`'s complement, still tagged CONVENTION ("the rate at which a gap closes"), and it is now the impact coefficient of all demand. For the euro members the ECB answers the zone, not the member, so a national G change leaves a lasting national gap of about 1.28 x the change. Fix: tag kappa as the behavioural figure it now is (or source it), state the multiplier where the form is declared, and put the number to Elias.

### M-2 (Medium) - no check bounds the no-policy gap, and the new form amplifies any drift in G/P or NX/P

At the seed references the gap closes at rho. But the bracket reads G + NX against `gamma_seed x P`, and over a century G (lines indexed by prices and drivers) and NX (TradeSystem) need not track potential. A drift in (G + NX)/P of d leaves a gap of about 1.42 d (USA), where the old form gave 0.83 d. Example: G/P sliding from 0.20 to 0.15 over 30 years would hold a USA gap near -7 %. `LongRunAnchorsDiagnostic` (c) only PRINTS the no-policy gap at years 1, 5, 10, 30 and 100 ("info"), and (d1)'s 0.5 pp band on mean growth would not see a gap that opens at 0.25 pp a year. Fix: add a declared bound on the no-policy gap at years 10, 30 and 100 (for example |gap| < 2 %) as a real check, so the GA-7-3 premise ("a lasting gap no seed could close") is tested over the horizon and not only at the seed.

### M-3 (Medium) - the treatable excess is added flat at every age 0-74: about twice the e0 effect of the real age profile, and clamped at young ages

Units are right: Eurostat/OECD treatable mortality is an age-standardised rate per 100 000 over ages 0-74, so adding the same Delta / 100 000 at each age under 75 raises the standardised rate by exactly Delta. But treatable deaths cluster at 45-74. With Delta at -50 per 100 000 (halving a seed of 100), the flat form gains about +1.7 years (sum over x < 75 of Delta l(x) e(x)); the same Delta placed at 50-74 gains about +0.8. The retired authored term gave +0.69 (1.0 x ln 2). And a fall larger than a child's own death rate (about 1e-4 at ages 1-14, against Delta of -5e-4) is floored by `MinRate` at those ages, so the effect is asymmetric between rises and falls. Failure scenario: a health budget that halves treatable mortality moves life expectancy about twice as far as the health family's own sourced quantity justifies, and a deeper improvement saturates silently. Fix: spread Delta over ages 0-74 in proportion to the seed schedule (or to a sourced age profile of treatable deaths), normalised so the standardised rate still moves by Delta. Or declare the flat form [AUTHORED-DRAFT] with the factor printed by (b).

### L-1 (Low) - Census four-decimal rates write zero and doubled ratios into the USA's trend

np2023_a2 prints rates to 1e-4. The USA's 2100 anchor carries ratio 0.00 at ages 10-12 (log taken at 1e-9, so -20.7: between 2090 and 2100 those rates collapse toward zero), and steps of 0.5 or 0.33 appear from 2030 at ages 4, 12-20. The e0 effect is about +0.02 years, but the schedule is not physical, and a reader of `MortalityRatio` would take the zero for data. Fix: floor the ratio at half the rounding unit over the base (0.00005 / m_2024), or smooth over adjacent ages before the ratio.

### L-3 (Low) - diagnostic loops that run zero times pass silently

(d2) runs a window only while `end - start + 1 >= MinWindowYears`, and (d3) breaks when `open.CalendarYear < leFirst`. For the six starts in `WorldClock.StartDate` (the earliest is Italy, 21 Jul 2022) every loop runs. But a start moved before 2022, or a WEO horizon past CBO's 2055, would drop the check with no FAIL. Fix: count the checks each country makes and fail on zero.

### L-4 (Low, info) - the HLW persistence is a one-year share of an AR(2)

rho is g(4) of HLW's AR(2) from a standing gap (US 1.4043 / -0.4700 gives 0.6481; EA 1.6530 / -0.7102 gives 0.6089; I recomputed both by hand). The model then decays geometrically, so after two years it keeps rho^2 = 0.42, where the AR(2) keeps 0.355 (US). An annual output-gap persistence of 0.6-0.65 sits inside the usual 0.5-0.8 range. Using the real-rate term at zero is consistent, because the model closes the gap separately through the Taylor rule. Stated as a declared approximation, no change needed.

## Checks that hold

- **(1) Life table.** q = m / (1 + m/2) with a = 1/2 at every closed age, the infant year included (the infant a0 is near 0.1; the bias is about 0.4 x q0, around 0.001-0.002 years, and c absorbs it). L = l - d/2; the open 95+ is l / m95, correct for a constant hazard. The 95+ collapse from 95..99 and 100+ is the stationary m = l95 / T95. The prep tool's e0 on the seed schedules matches the publishers' (SE 83.824 vs 83.8, DE 81.165 vs 81.2, FR 83.013 vs 83, IT 83.705 vs 83.7, PL 78.509 vs 78.5, USA 79.916 vs Census 79.9). The USA's CDC 79.0 baseline is reached by c, about 1.1. Daily cost per country: two locked dictionary hits, one double[96] allocation, 96 `Math.Exp` and a 96-step loop - microseconds. Six a day, or 220 000 calls in a no-policy century, is negligible.
- **(2) R.** The anchors are 2022, 2024 and 2030..2100 by 10. The log-ratio is linear between anchors, held at 2022's before the first and at 2100's after the last; an exact anchor year reads that anchor (w = 1); 2024 is exactly 1. The USA's 2022 anchor holds Census's 2023 rates.
- **(3) Treatable units.** Per 100 000, ages 0-74, against the trended anchor (so the trend is not counted twice with R). See M-3 for the age profile.
- **(5) Save format 65.** `MortalityMultiplier` (EconomyState) and `IdentityPropensitySeed` / `IdentityAutonomousShareSeed` (Country) are public fields on `[Serializable]` classes that Newtonsoft writes whole; any version other than 65 is refused with the plain message (ruling A). The preview clone copies both Country fields explicitly, and the state comes through `MemberwiseClone`.
- **(6) Readers of LifeExpectancy.** The pension-age statutes read their published paths, not the model's figure. Approval does not read it. The Policy Web carries the node and its icon, with no formula edge. The statistics tile, StatHistory and the publication pipeline read the stored value. The cohort substrate reads `HealthFamily.DeathRateFor`, which is untouched (life expectancy stays inputs-only). The health family's life-expectancy term is retired cleanly, and `HealthFeedbackDiagnostic` now tests the treatable excess at zero at the seed.
- **(7) The diagnostic.** (a1), (a2), (b), (c1), (c2), (d1), (d2) and (d3) are real assertions that count failures and exit 1. (a2) recomputes with its own interpolation and life table; (c2) shocks one twin and holds the kept share to rho in both forms. With H-1 fixed, a DE/IT/PL failure in (d) is a real finding about the model.
- **(9) Reproducible.** The digests are checked against SHA256SUMS.txt before reading, and two runs give byte-identical output equal to the committed file.

## Verdict: NOT READY

H-1 is a data error in a generated SOURCED table, and it changes how the item's own acceptance results read. The fix is small: bound the scan, add asserts, re-generate, and re-read DE's (d) results. M-1 needs a tag and a ruling on kappa. M-2 and M-3 should be fixed or declared before the merge. The life-table arithmetic, the gap algebra (the daily form composes exactly to the turn form), the save step and the preview clone are correct.
