Every figure was checked against the extracted PDF text of the draft book [BK-D].

## Findings and what was done

1. **HIGH - the renormaliser took back most of each sourced loss (confirmed).** The shift was added to every named party and the whole vector divided by (1 + the sum of the shifts), so a governing party's own share of the rescale returned part of its loss: in the first diagnostic run M's -1.50 points arrived as -1.08, SD's -0.60 as -0.11, and L at the 2022 prior gained on a worse economy. **Fixed:** `EconomicVote.ApplyRecordShift` delivers each named party's shift exactly and rescales only the parties it does not name, which absorb the difference in proportion. The diagnostic now asserts, for M, KD, L and SD, that the predicted share moves by exactly its shift (to 1e-9).
2. **MEDIUM-HIGH - the support party's figure was called sourced; it is not, and it outweighed the cabinet partners (confirmed).** No page gives a party outside the cabinet an economic vote (GAP 4); the book's opposition-influence weight enters only a concentration measure predicting the prime minister's vote, and its effect is "quite small compared to ... the role that parties play in cabinet" (p. 271). SD had carried .0119 against KD .0035 and L .0019. **Fixed:** the rule is labelled `[AUTHORED-DRAFT]` in the code and on the calibration list, and capped at the smallest cabinet partner's loss - the spec's §5.5 "less than sitting in the cabinet, but not nothing". Asserted.
3. **MEDIUM - Figure 9.7's regressor is the portfolio share (confirmed from the raw PDF: the axis reads "Percent of Cabinet Portfolios Held by Party").** **Stated, not changed:** the code takes Gamson's law's estimate of that share - the partner's seats over the cabinet parties' seats, the sourced rule §634 allocates by - rather than the game's six portfolio slots, whose sixths are the game's quantisation. The comment says so; the literature's large-party underpayment is unsized (§634), so the real cabinet's 13/6/5 is not used for the start alone.
4. **MEDIUM - a partner under a 12.3 % share got a negative magnitude from the insignificant intercept (confirmed arithmetic; Italy's NM, Poland's NL).** **Fixed:** the partner line is floored at zero, stated; the diagnostic asserts no governing party in any of the six countries carries a negative magnitude.
5. **MEDIUM - the save format was not bumped (confirmed).** A v33 save made mid-campaign would replay its campaign without the record and count the election so, silently. **Fixed:** format 34; an older save is refused plainly, as every bump since 3. The play save re-staged at 34.
6. **LOW, fixed:** `RecordShift`'s summary had displaced `DrawCountsAtStart`'s; a zero shift still divided by a total not exactly one (now returns the shares untouched, asserted by reference); the stale "the electorate does not move with the simulation" claims in `GameController.cs` and `PartyCampaignCapital.cs` and "WIRED TO NOTHING" in `PerceivedPerformance.cs`; the Presidency branch now shares the cabinet branch's null guard; the header's date; the film harness's model election night now reads the record as play's no-campaign polling day does (`UiScreenshotDriver.CaptureElectionNightFromModel`).
7. **LOW, noted, not fixed (dormant):** a government formed by the game (`GovernmentRecord.FromView`) never sets `Kind`, so a formed US government would read as a cabinet - no US election is live; if a House election is wired, Table 9.1's legislative row is the matching one; a campaign opening on an unpublished economy reads index 50 and stays neutral to polling day, by the rule "judged once".

## Found after the review, by the film

8. **HIGH - in a campaign the record moved nothing (confirmed on film; INTRODUCED by the fix to finding 1, so no reviewer saw it).** The play save's campaign logged the record judged at its opening (index 43.4, M -0.40 points), yet the dry film's count came out at the previous family's shares to the decimal. The cause: the campaign's form handed every party an entry, the opposition a zero, so the exact-delivery rule found no unnamed party to absorb the loss and returned the shares as given. Only the no-campaign path had worked. The old renormaliser, for all its fault, had at least moved the campaign; the exact-delivery rule needs an unnamed party to absorb, and the campaign form named every one. **Fixed:** `SimulationManager.RecordShiftFor` gives a party the record does not name NaN; `EconomicVote.ApplyRecordShiftByIndex` names only the governing parties and delivers exactly; `CampaignRun.CurrentPreference` calls it. The diagnostic now asserts the campaign's form - M moved by exactly its shift, S up, the shares summing to one - and that a vector naming every party moves nothing, which is why the form carries NaN. The lesson: a fix to a reviewed defect is itself unreviewed code - the diagnostic exercised the fixed rule on one path (the prediction) and passed while the other path (the campaign) was inert; the film, read against the previous family's count, is what caught it.
