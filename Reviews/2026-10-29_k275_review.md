## Findings

### High

**H1. The new diagnostic is registered in no bar, so none of its five statements has ever run in the engine.** `IncomeTaxCreepCapDiagnostic.Run` appears nowhere in `Assets/Editor/CheckSuite.cs`: not in the Simulation table (`CheckSuite.cs:518` ff., where `DebtPathHoldDiagnostic` and the other `NoPolicyCentury` readers sit) and not in the cheap suite. A grep of the branch finds no caller at all. Its header still claims (`IncomeTaxCreepCapDiagnostic.cs:21-22`) that "in the simulation bar it costs no run of its own". This repeats the failure `CheckSuite.cs:194` records for `PartyLeadershipDiagnostic` ("it had run in no bar, so its ASCII-folded names failed unheard").

As a result, every piece of evidence for the branch comes from outside the engine, on .NET rather than Mono ("a prediction to the printed decimal at best", in creep_play.sh's own words). Three things in particular are unchecked in the engine: that statement (3) does not pass vacuously under Mono (that Poland really crosses its bound by year 40), that the AI books stay inside the bound, and that the seed reads exactly one.

Failure scenario: a later change lets the factor read the lever (the kink K275 forbids), or K271's lift drifts in an AI book. The bar stays green because the one instrument built to catch it never runs.

Fix: register `("IncomeTaxCreepCapDiagnostic", IncomeTaxCreepCapDiagnostic.Run)` in the Simulation table, after the shared-century readers and before `RatchetSlackCheck`, and run the simulation bar once before integration.

### Medium

**M1. The revenue is capped but the behavioural rate is not, and the mismatch is undeclared.** `TaxSchedule.EffectiveRate` (`TaxSchedule.cs:772-778`) still reads the uncapped yield: seed + (yield now − seed yield). Three readers take it:

- the participation term (`MacroSystem.cs:1041`);
- the Gini term (`MacroSystem.cs:1373`);
- a bill's hike in points (`ParliamentSystem.cs:220`). These are differences, so the creep largely cancels there.

In Poland's idle player book at year 100, the flat-equivalent rate is 12.37 + (5.6517 × 6.14 − 6.14) = **40.9 %**. That is above the statute's own top marginal rate of 36 %, while the revenue's take is held at 34.9 %. The participation term reads 0.116 × 100 × ln(59.1 / 87.63) = **−4.57 points**, close to its ±5 clamp. In other words, the player is charged the full labour-supply and inequality effect of creep the revenue no longer collects. Before K275 the gap ran the other way: the revenue took 69.9 % against a 40.9 % behavioural rate.

The K275 paragraph (`TaxSchedule.cs:61-71`) does not mention this. The ruling's text covers only "the yield", so a code change is not necessarily owed. What is owed is either a DECLARED line saying the behavioural rate keeps the statute's creep uncapped, and why, or a ruling line for Elias.

### Low

**L1. The cap can bind in an AI book, and when it does the AI ministry misprices its point.** There are two routes:

- The player governs a crept book and then hands it over: an election loss, the player taking the presidency (SP-7b), or `HoldsNoPolicyChange` after the creep. The doc (`TaxSchedule.cs:68-70`) declares this route.
- An older save whose AI books crept under the pre-K271 build. The lift starts again from 1 at the load, so a Polish AI book cut after about 25 years loads with its creep already past 2.91. This route is not declared; the K271 save note covers the lift, not the cap.

In either case F4-5's identity (seed = AER ÷ (AER(+1) − AER)) is off by the factor f. A point then raises f × base while the AI ministry prices it at base, overestimating it by 1/f.

**L2. The DECLARED bound's justification is only approximately what the code does.** At unchanged rates the capped take works out to top rate × AER_ref ÷ AER_seed, not to the top rate. So "the take at or under the statute's top rate" holds only while the pyramid keeps the income-indexed statute's yield at or under the seed's. That is measured at 0.972 for Poland and 0.977 for Germany at year 100, so it holds in these runs, but it is not true by construction. Statement (3) asserts it for Poland alone. The phrase "the binding regime is the top rate on the anchored base" (`TaxSchedule.cs:66-67`) should say "about".

**L3. Comment claims are out of date.**
- `TaxSchedule.cs:58-59` (the K271 paragraph) still ends "The player's creep stays the ratio form's ..., filed with K271's measurement". The K275 paragraph right after it now contradicts this; it should say K275 answers it.
- The class-level K275 text says an AI book's creep "stays one", but it is measured up to 1.0003 (Sweden, year 20, in the Germany world). `StatuteCreep`'s own summary already says "near one"; the class text should match.

**L4. The probe literals in `StatuteTopMarginalRate` carry no provenance mark.** `+ 1000.0` and `AxisCeiling(s) * 10.0` (`TaxSchedule.cs:748-750`) have no CONVENTION mark. They sit under `Data/`, outside `ConstantProvenanceCheck`'s Simulation scope, so nothing fails, but house style marks such literals.

**L5. Statement (2) checks only that the cap does not bind.** It asserts that `AiCreepBinds` is zero, not that the AI creep stays near one. A regression in K271's lift that let an AI book drift to a creep of 1.5 would stay green until the creep passed the 1.67 bound. `AiCreepMax` is already recorded, so an extra assertion `AiCreepMax < 1 + 1e-3` would cost nothing; the measured value is 1.0003.

**L6. For information only, not caused by K275.** In creep_play, Germany's top sub-row set to 0 at year 1 reads ×1.000025, a rise. This is GA-2-11's documented residue (the ramps read in place of § 32a's formula once any override is present). Statement (5) probes Poland only, so it is unaffected.

## The hunt list, answered

1. **The seed and the AI books.** The factor is exactly 1 at the seed: both sides read `(double)(float)AER(0, 1, 1)`, and the seed's wage is 100 and its price level 1 (`Country.cs:309-310`). Wherever the creep is inside the bound, `YieldRatio` returns the unmultiplied `ratio` (`cap < 1.0 ? ratio * cap : ratio`), so the result is the same to the bit. The all-AI frf CSVs are identical on two seeds.
   Can an AI book's thresholds lag its incomes? The lift is the product of the same float ratios the boundary references are updated with (`SimulationManager.cs:8463-8474`), so the only drift is float rounding. Poland's AI book reads inc 61.9900 and thr 61.9900 at year 100. The lift is serialized (the K271 field), so a load does not reset it.
   The only routes to a binding AI book are those in L1. The lift's timing and year boundaries are not routes.
2. **No kink.** The factor reads `RateSeedOf(line)`, the statute (a static table: `readonly struct Bracket`, no writers anywhere in Assets), the boundary scales, the lift and the pyramid. It reads no lever, no `BracketRates` and no shift. `ThresholdsIndexed` is a literal in the static table. `RateSeed` is written only at the seed (`Country.cs:323`, `WorldFactory.cs:1188`). Re-levying the line flips `IsImplemented` and keeps `RateSeed` (`ParliamentSystem.cs:748`). No player bill can move the bound. Rise over cut matches the form's to about 1e-15 at years 1, 40 and 100.
3. **The cache.** `Memo.Reference` is cleared in `Reset`. The memo's key (level, wage, lift, the overrides key and the pyramid's contents) covers everything Reference reads, and it reads more than Reference needs, which is harmless. `StatuteCreep` moves the memo through `YieldNowAtStatute` before it reads Reference.
   Memos are `[NonSerialized]`, and the preview clone (`SimulationManager.cs:7645 ff.) builds a fresh `Country` without them. It does copy the lift, the price and wage references, `IncomeTaxSeedAer` and `RateSeed`, so a preview reads the run's factor.
   `TopRates` is static, but it is a pure function of the static statute, and no threaded path reaches it (the two `Parallel.For` loops in Editor/ are vote fits and file hashing). There is no leak.
4. **The household burden.** Both sides of the gap are multiplied by the same factor (`TaxBases.cs:153`, `MacroSystem.cs:174`), so the gap is exactly zero at no policy. Once the cap binds, a player's move shows in the gap scaled by f, consistent with the revenue.
5. **Save format and determinism.** No persisted field is added and no format bump is owed. `NoPolicyCentury.RecordIncomeTax` probes clones of the line. Those probes do reset the country's live `ScheduleMemo` key (the clone's overrides key differs from the line's), but this is only cache churn: values are recomputed bit-identically, and no state outside the cache is written. `Year` is a class, so the writes are kept.
6. **The diagnostic.** Each assertion's `ok = false` guard is reachable. Statement (3)'s guard against a vacuous pass is correct (out of the engine Poland binds from year 25, by 4.19 against 2.91 at year 40). The tolerances (1e-9 relative, 1e-6 on the kink quotient) are a double product's width, so they hide no drift. But nothing runs it (H1), and statement (2) is weak (L5).
7. **The bound and the uncapped rate.** See M1 and L2.
8. **CRLF and provenance.** The blobs are LF, as at the base (`core.autocrlf true`), and the working files are CRLF. The new `.meta` matches its neighbours byte for byte in form, and its GUID is unique. For provenance see L4; for comment claims see H1 (the bar claim) and L3.

**Verdict: NOT READY.** The design is sound and the measurements reproduce, but H1 must be fixed first: register the diagnostic and run it once in the simulation bar. M1 needs a DECLARED line or a ruling.
