## (1) Definitions against [BD06]

I ran pdftotext on the stored PDF.

- **Appendix I (txt :1075-1093).** It defines "Balance is the difference between the central government's Total Revenue & Grants and Total Expenditure (i.e., the fiscal surplus)" and gives:
  - "BALCH_term = ½ * (BAL-1 + BAL-2) - ½ * ( BAL-3 + BAL-4)"
  - "* Where there are no data on BAL-3 and BAL-4 then: BALCH_term = BAL-1 - BAL-2"
  - "BALCH_ey = BAL0 - BAL-1"

  `FiscalVote.Judge` (FiscalVote.cs:139-145) matches all three exactly. Using the fallback when only one of BAL-3/BAL-4 is missing (three closed years) is declared reading 3. The source says "no data on BAL-3 and BAL-4", so that reading is an extension. It is acceptable.
- **Sign, end to end: correct.**
  1. The game's balance is `actualRevenue - totalSpending` (SimulationManager.cs:8348), so a surplus is positive.
  2. The FiscalTurnReport keeps that sign: `BudgetBalance = AccruedBudgetBalance + oneOffs` (:6079), and one-offs are signed as the balance (FromLedger: `-AppliedDelta`).
  3. A growing deficit therefore gives a negative BALCH, a negative Δz (β > 0) and a negative shift_gov.
  4. `ApplyRecordShift` adds the shift; a positive value is a gain, consistent with `RecordShiftOf = -magnitude*d`.

  The source confirms the positive-β convention on p.11 (txt :403-411): "a negative value of BALCH_term implies a loss of votes … the probability of reelection is increasing when the fiscal balance improves". Table 2's note 2 (txt :1629) calls both variables "the change in the ratio of the government deficit". That wording is the source's own inconsistency. The appendix and the text (balance, surplus positive) win, and the code follows them. See L1.
- **Units: verified.** The ratios are fractions, not percent, even though Appendix I says the variables are "presented as a percentage of GDP". The authors' own marginal effects pin the units (txt :564-568): "+1 pp surplus → +3-4.5 pp reelection probability; election year 7-9 pp". At the probit centre, φ(0)·11.339·0.01 = 4.52 pp and φ(0)·22.073·0.01 = 8.80 pp. On a percent scale the effects would be 100× larger and absurd.
- **Coefficients.** 11.339 [0.026], 22.073 [0.001], N 164, narrow sample, Developed; the expanded sample's 7.172 [0.108] and 19.390 [0.003], N 180. All match Table 2 (txt :1586-1625). Leaving growth out (-3.077 [0.615]) is correctly sourced.

## Findings

### M1: the fixed window charges a government formed in play for its predecessor's years, outside [BD06]'s estimating sample

- **Where:** FiscalVote.cs:31-32 (declared reading 4), :139-145.
- **Scenario:** a government formed by a no-confidence vote three months before the count gets BALCH_term from BAL-1..BAL-4 and BALCH_ey in full. [BD06]'s narrow sample, which is the source of β (col. 2), is restricted to "leaders who were in office for at least two fiscal years prior to the elections" (txt :335). The coefficient is being applied to cases it was never estimated on. It also conflicts with §752: the economic vote is judged over the government's own term, while the fiscal term uses a fixed window. COAL-1 reads both components.
- **Fix:** an Elias ruling. One option is to carry BALCH_term only when the government took office on or before the start of BAL-2's year, and BALCH_ey only when it held office through BAL-1, printing "not in office for the window" otherwise. The other is to keep reading 4 and list it in §4 (6) Risks as a sample extrapolation.

### M2: σ_v is the sample sd of a fat-tailed swing distribution; the point value leans high and the printed "95 % interval" is too narrow

- **Where:** Tools/aloop_sigma_fit.pl:101-110; IncumbentVoteSwing.cs:354-356; FiscalVoteFitCheck (its twin).
- **Evidence:** the swings' kurtosis is **6.5** (a normal is 3).
  - Four observations beyond 3 sd carry the spread: ESP 1982 UCD -28.2, CAN 1993 PC -26.7, TUR 2002 DSP -21.0, FRA 1993 PS -17.3. Without them σ = 5.12.
  - Robust scales give 4.45 (MAD) and 4.23 (IQR).
  - A bootstrap 95 % interval (4000 resamples, seed 880) is **5.00-6.78**, against the printed 5.41-6.48. The kurtosis-corrected asymptotic se gives σ²(κ-1)/(4n) ⇒ 5.02-6.78, which agrees with the bootstrap.
- **Why it matters:** the premise is that one probit unit equals σ_v points. Under a probit, the marginal effect near the threshold depends on the density there. For a fat-tailed swing distribution, the normal scale that matches that density is smaller than the sample sd, so shift_gov could be overstated by roughly 15-30 %.
  - Two forces pull the other way. If re-election carries noise beyond the swing (coalition formation, a threshold that is itself uncertain), σ_v·Δz is a lower bound.
  - Neither direction is ruled.
- **Fix:**
  - Print the interval as a bootstrap or kurtosis-corrected interval, or label the chi-square one "under normality".
  - Print the kurtosis and the robust scale beside σ_v.
  - Add the choice of spread estimator to §4 (6) Risks or the calibration list for Elias. Keep the point estimate as ruled (J1 Q4).

### M3: the term's range is ±19.7 pp of share, set by an AUTHORED-DRAFT bound

- **Where:** FiscalVote.cs:61 (`SupportBound = 0.10`).
- **Scenario:** cap = σ_v·(β_term + β_ey)·0.10 = 5.899·33.412·0.10 = **19.7 pp**. The a2/q13 deficit poles run deficits of roughly 30 % of GDP a year (debt 35 → 95 % in two years), so both variables will sit at the bound. That gives a probit index of -3.3, a region where P(reelect) ≈ 0 and far outside any developed-country observation.
- **Fix:** none required now; it is on the calibration list. Print the cap in pp on the RECORD line or in the diagnostic header so the night's figure is read against it. Consider a tighter interim bound for the developed sample. Table A3 prints country averages of BALCH_ey only within a few points.

### L findings

- **L1 (plan wording).** A_LOOP_PLAN.md §4 (2) quotes Table 2 note 2's "government deficit" wording as the definition. The build correctly follows Appendix I and p.11 (balance, surplus positive). Cite Appendix I plus p.11 in the plan, so that nobody later "fixes" the sign to match the note.
- **L2 (fit exclusions).** Every CDU-led German election (8) is dropped because ParlGov's cabinet table lists CDU+CSU (1727) while its election table lists CDU (808) and CSU (1180). The same id-change exclusion drops ITA 1994-2001, FIN 1972/75, GRC 1989/90 and FRA 1962-78. Those include party collapses, which bias σ in an unknown direction. Using the CDU+CSU sums would change σ by about -0.1. Fix: map 1727 → 808+1180 in the tool and its twin, or list the dropped ids by name in the output.
- **L3 (NaN).** `Held` passes NaN through, because Math.Min and Math.Max return NaN. A NaN `Balance` or `OpenFlow` would carry NaN into every governing party's record. Fix: treat a non-finite ratio as absent in `Ratio`/`OpenRatio`.
- **L4 (boundary-day count).** A count taken on a boundary day after AdvanceTurn sees OpenDays 0. It gets no BALCH_ey, and BAL-1 is the election year itself. The chance is about 1/365 per count. Print it, or treat the just-closed year as BAL0.
- **L5 (early-year BAL0).** BAL0 on day 1-30 of a fiscal year annualises a few days. In US lapse periods those days are not a uniform slice. Declared reading 2 covers this. A minimum-days rule, or blending with BAL-1, would steady the polls (5293 reads the record daily).
- **L6 (prereg risk).** The B&D variables are changes. A pole that sets its stance inside BAL-4 and holds it flat can print a term near 0, so "deficit term negative, surplus positive" may fail because of the model rather than a defect. Interest compounding probably makes the deficit poles negative. The surplus poles are the less certain pair. The pass's 0.01 pp recomputation tolerance is fine given the printed precision.
- **L7 (diagnostics' strength).**
  - (1b)/(1c) are strong: day-by-day mirroring against the debt ledger's days, and the closed year against the report and GdpAtClose.
  - The gaps:
    - FiscalVoteFitCheck is a twin of the perl reading, so it shares its blind spots (L2).
    - Nothing pins the fraction units against the paper. Add a check that φ(0)·β·0.01 reproduces 4.5 and 8.8 pp (txt :564-568).
    - Every planted path is negative, so the live sign of a policy deficit is proved only by the prereg run.
    - There is no load-then-run continuity check for OpenDays; it is sound by construction.
- **L8 (source mismatch).** [BD06]'s Balance is central government, while the game's is the country's whole budget. GDP at close is used instead of the year's GDP, a bias of about 2 % of the ratio at 4 % nominal growth. Both are negligible; worth a line in the Risks section.
- **L9 (σ_v reuse).** σ_v is fitted on the prime minister's party but applied to the whole government side (reading 7), and carried to the US president's party (reading 6). Both are declared. A coalition bloc's swing spread is not the prime minister's party's.
