## Findings

### H1. The balanced absorption's weights cannot depend on the shares: Sweden's W is uniform (1/7), so a campaign takes the same points from a 4.7 % party as from a 20.9 % one
`CampaignPersuasion.cs:219-268` (`BalancedAbsorption`). The claims at `:174` ("W is the share-weighted absorption scaled on both axes") and `:219` ("p gives up in proportion to its share").

The seed is s_p·o_pq, and the Sinkhorn scaling returns W_pq = a_p·s_p·o_pq·b_q. The row factor a_p absorbs s_p completely. So the fixed point is the balancing of the overlap pattern alone, and it is the same for any shares, apart from which parties count as held. With every party everywhere (Sweden), the pattern is the zero-diagonal all-ones matrix, and its balancing is 1/(n−1). Measured on Sweden's prior:
- W's column for S is 0.1429 for every other party, and so is W's column for L.
- S alone at +0.40: SD, M, V, C, KD, MP and L each lose **−0.057143**. The record's proportional absorption (`Apply`) took SD −0.121 and L −0.027.
- The live AI field (seed 777, the run's own effects), balanced against proportional: V **−0.139** against −0.077, MP **−0.140** against −0.057, C −0.128 against −0.066. The same holds on seeds 778 and 779.

So Δ_p = e_p − mean_{q≠p} e_q is an unweighted mean. K65 ruled the share-weighted mean, and K81 says the effects "stay" relative to the field. The diagnostic cannot see this. Its `BalancedByHand` (`CampaignPersuasionDiagnostic.cs:291`, `:344`) is the same scaling written again, so it recomputes the same uniform W.

The trade-off is real, and it is Elias's to rule. A column proportional to share (losses by size) cannot also have rows summing to one (equal effort zero) when the shares differ: that is §881's drift. K81's conditions only require W to be doubly stochastic with a zero diagonal. That is a whole polytope, and Sinkhorn on any diagonal scaling of the pattern picks its uniform member.

*Scenario:* a Swedish player campaigns hard. KD (5.42 %), MP (5.16 %) and L (4.68 %) sit within about a point of the 4 % Riksdag threshold. Each loses the same absolute points as the M (19.4 %), roughly twice what the proportional reading takes. That moves a small party toward the threshold by the model's choice, not the ruling's, and the seats follow.

*Fix:* put it to Elias in ask mode with the three measured columns: uniform (as built), proportional (`Apply`, the drift back), and a share-aware member of the polytope (for example the Euclidean, not KL, projection of the proportional matrix onto the constraints). Whatever he rules, correct the comments at `:174` and `:219`, and assert the ruled loss distribution in the diagnostic by an independent formula, not by the same iteration.

### M1. The cap's reading under an attack is DECLARED, not asked: an attack makes idle bystanders gain past their own sourced effect
`CampaignPersuasion.cs:180-183`; the live bound at `CampaignPersuasionDiagnostic.cs:287-299`.
K81 reads "no party's net effect exceeds its sourced effect in magnitude". The build reads it as gains up to the own effect plus a W-share of the others' losses. For gains alone that is exact, and the AI field never breaks the literal cap (seeds 777-779: no party moved past max(0, e)). The literal reading of "in magnitude" cannot hold for losses at all, since an idle party must lose for a lone campaigner to gain. But under an attack, S attacks SD (SD −1.00, S's own +0.20). S moves +0.343, and every idle party moves **+0.114** on a sourced effect of 0. The live-bound check is a tautology given W ≥ 0, so it certifies the reading, not the ruling. Nothing in `docs/ASKS_FOR_ELIAS.md` puts it.
*Scenario:* the player's AGAINST chip on SD lifts M, KD and L above their own campaigns, which K81's words forbid.
*Fix:* add it to H1's ask. Either the target's loss is absorbed by the field (as built, so the cap is "plus a share of losses"), or the attack's loss goes to the attacker, and the attacker's sourced effect is its own case plus what it took.
