# The blind design panel - §791, PS-6 US-11: the House seat methods and R-US18's decision rule (2026-10-06)

A design workflow (`us11-blind-rule-panel`, run `wf_a2f32abe-28a`, seven agents): three designers, each from one lens - the game's use, inference from few cycles, the precedents R-US14 and R-US15 and the record - wrote full specifications independently from the brief below; a judge scored and merged them; two critics attacked the merge with twenty-two attacks; a reviser wrote the final specification, accepting all twenty-two - four of them only in part, one leg of each of those four fixes rejected with its reason. Every agent carried the same instruction: never open, list or grep the scratch folder that held the session's rough estimates of the methods' results, and never compute any method's seat counts, from the catalog or by hand; the repository was read-only to them.

The session that ran the panel had made those estimates first (`COMPLETED.md` §791). It condensed this specification into `docs/specs/USA_STAGE_PLAN.md` - US-11's *Design* and R-US18's preregistered block - and corrected both where its review found them wrong; §791 and `Reviews/2026-10-06_s791_us11_rule_review.md` list each correction. **Below, verbatim: the panel's final specification, the reviser's disposition of each attack, and the brief** (its headings one level down, under the section headings added here). The three designs, the judge's scores and merge, and the attacks as the critics raised them are not reproduced; they stay in the workflow's run transcript, out of tree. Where the plan departs from the specification, the plan governs.

## The final specification

### Rationale

FROM THE GAME'S USE. The House must give three things every time:
- control, which matters most, since bills pass by party blocs;
- all 50 delegations, for R-US3 and the board;
- 435 seats.
(a) and (b) give all three. (c) gives only the first, so it stands as the national benchmark and is never recommended alone.

The game feeds the count one number, its poll. So every method moves by the poll's own change, in plain additive form, on shares read by candidate, with no re-solve. Every method is also anchored on the previous House, which in play is the game's own (R-US11 (a)). A method's level therefore never jumps at the first game election, and the measurement compares responses rather than proportionality's level.

Maps move in play: 67 districts in 2024 and 173 to 181 in 2026. So (a) carries a declared fallback. That choice would govern about a third of the House in 2026 and persist, and the record's redraws may not be the kind play meets. So the record can keep the default but never switch it silently.

FROM STATISTICS. Of the three decisive cycles, two measure a response (C1, C2) and one, at zero swing (C4), measures only structure. So a switch must:
- win both swing cycles and lose nothing in C4, on held-state seats rather than on three national numbers;
- not cost a delegation class.
The national error can raise a question only when both swing cycles agree. (c) can do so only when two forward swing cycles exist, which the record on disk does not hold.

Every declared convention ([HH-DIV]'s 2020, the item's own cycles, Minnesota's and Colorado's classes, the swing's basis) is re-run, and any that changes the outcome asks. So no one-seat convention decides silently, and the conventions go to Elias blind before the run. No noise band is invented. The rule is preregistered by digest, with an amendment path because Elias amends rulings.

FROM THE RULINGS AND THE STANDING RULES. R-US14 supplies the sum and the tie to (a); R-US15 supplies 'not above'. The plan's 'the measurement decides' lets a consistent (b) win. The grain ('nothing measured goes live unruled'; 'a premise that fails is STOPPED') and CLAUDE.md's one green bar per commit are reconciled by registering an ask rather than failing on it, with a guard on US-16. R-US11 (a) sets the base in play. R-US3 makes the delegation classes, and a tied delegation's lost vote, matter. R-US13 (a) carries the apportionment forward. R-US5 (a) makes the count a pure function.

BLINDNESS. No method was run on the record, no seat count of any method informed a choice, and the estimates folder was not opened. To verify the P4 attack, the national two-party vote shares on the deciding-count basis were summed from the catalog: vote shares only. While weighing the C4 attack on S3, I sketched an illustrative rounding with an assumed, unfitted rho. It decided nothing: that change rests on the identities and Delta = -0.08 pp alone.

### The rule

R-US18'S RULE, PREREGISTERED. Its text and constants sit in one fenced block of the plan's R-US18 entry, between begin and end markers, with the ruling lines outside it. A digest of that block is committed before the first run (P17). The instrument runs the rule and generates the verdict.
- D = {C1, C2, C4}: swing cycles C1 and C2; level cycle C4.
- (a) means (a) with F-old; (b) means (b) anchored.
- Every comparison is strict, on integer seats, with no noise band: neither precedent uses one, and any band would be an invented number.
- Equal is never better.

STEP 1: (a)'s fallback. Over D's redrawn state-cycles (CO, MN and PA 2018; NC 2020; AL, GA, LA, NY and NC 2024), compare sum W under F-old with sum W under F-prop.
- F-old is kept if its sum is not above F-prop's, ties included. F-old is (a)'s own form, so a second seat rule inside (a) must earn its place.
- If F-prop's sum is strictly lower, the rule STOPS (S5). It never switches automatically, for two reasons:
  - the fallback would govern 173 to 181 districts on 3 Nov 2026, and with no district shares it would persist in every state it counts;
  - the record's redraws, court plans and enacted acts alike, may not be the kind play meets.
- Printed per cycle and per state, with each map's instrument and K.
- S5 bears on the verdict only when (a) is the step-2 method or step 2 stops.

STEP 2: (a) against (b), on held-state seats misplaced (H).
- Code recommends (a) if sum_D H(a) <= sum_D H(b). This is R-US14's sum and its tie to (a), and R-US15's 'not above'.
- Code recommends (b) only if ALL of these hold:
  - H(b) < H(a) in C1 and in C2;
  - H(b) <= H(a) in C4;
  - sum_D G(b) < sum_D G(a) over the whole House;
  - K(b) >= K(a) in each cycle of D, since R-US3 reads classes.
  'The measurement decides', but a switch away from the plan's expectation must hold in every cycle that can measure it, must not be worse in the cycle that cannot, and must not cost a delegation class.
- Otherwise the rule STOPS (S1), naming each condition that failed.

STEP 3: control. If step 2 picked a method, the rule STOPS (S2) if that method's |N| is strictly larger than the other's in both C1 and C2. After S1, both directions are evaluated and listed under S1, not as a separate stop. The national grain may raise a question; it never makes a switch.

STEP 4: (c). The rule STOPS (S3) only if all of these hold:
- D has at least two forward swing cycles;
- in every one, (c)'s |N| is strictly below the step-2 method's (after S1, below both (a)'s and (b)'s);
- in every one, (c)'s |N| is strictly below the no-change benchmark's.
On the record on disk only C2 qualifies, so S3 cannot fire, and (c)'s C2 comparison is printed in the verdict line. (c) is never recommended alone.

STEP 5: the strawman guard. If (b') is fitted in every cycle of D, the rule STOPS (S6) if (b') beats the step-2 method by step 2's own switch conditions:
- H strictly lower in C1 and in C2;
- H not higher in C4;
- sum G strictly lower;
- K not lower in any cycle.
After S1, (b') is compared with both methods and the results are listed.

STEP 6: the declared readings. Steps 1-5 are re-run under each alternative reading, one at a time:
- (i) 2020 at [HH-DIV]: 212 of 434, with NY-22 undecided and scored by interval;
- (ii) the item's own cycles, D = {C2, C4}. Only the method and the fallback are compared: with one swing cycle, S2, S3 and S6 would each rest on a single cycle, which the rule forbids, so they are printed;
- (iii) Minnesota 2018 held;
- (iv) Colorado 2018 held;
- (v) both held;
- (vi) Delta and V on the deciding-count basis.
The rule STOPS (S4) if any reading changes the outcome (the step-2 verdict, the fallback, or which of S1-S3, S5 and S6 fire), naming the reading.

STEP 7: premises. The rule STOPS (S7) if a measured cycle has a winner outside REP and DEM (P1 fails), and prints that measurement.

TIE-BREAK: equality on step 2's sum goes to (a), and on step 1's to F-old. This follows 'Code expects (a) where maps held' and R-US14's tie to (a).

OUTCOME:
- 'code recommends (a)' (with F-old), or 'code recommends (b)', when step 2 picks it and no stop fires;
- otherwise the rule does not decide, and every stop is listed with its figures.

REACH, stated in the ask:
- The rule can reach (a) with F-old, (b), or an ask. It never selects F-prop, (b') or (c) by itself.
- C4 can only veto.
- A one-seat difference in a figure can move the outcome between (a) and S1, or between S1 and (b); no integer rule is free of an edge in the data.
- No declared one-seat convention can decide silently, because S4 re-runs each one.

THE VERDICT LINE is generated, never typed.
- Decided, for example: 'By R-US18's rule, code recommends (a): districts by uniform swing where the lines held, a redrawn state counted on its previous lines' pattern (F-old). Held-state seats misplaced x against y over 2016->2018, 2018->2020 and 2022->2024 (per cycle ...); whole House ...; delegation classes ...; national error ...; (c) printed: one forward swing cycle (2018->2020), closer/not closer - S3 needs two; the same under every declared reading.'
- Otherwise: 'R-US18's rule does not decide: <S1-S7 with the figures> - asked.'

R-US18 is asked with the full table in every case (US-11's done-when). The conventions go to Elias with this spec, before the first run (see stop_and_ask).

### Stops and asks

THE STOPS are printed after the whole table, each with its options. Every stop's options also include 'amend the rule as ruled'.
- S1, step 2 inconclusive: (b) has the lower held-state sum but fails a switch condition, which is named. Options: (a) as measured, or (b) as measured.
- S2, control: the step-2 method's national error is larger in both swing cycles. Options: the picked method, or the other.
- S3, (c) (dormant on the record on disk). Options:
  - the step-2 method as measured;
  - (c)'s total with that method's delegations reconciled (method_c);
  - another delegation rule for (c), not yet specified. Choosing this leaves R-US18 ASKED, because no method can be built from it.
- S4, a declared reading decides. The options are the readings as conventions: the declared reading, with its reason from the game's model, or the alternative. They are never phrased 'X with its verdict'; the table still shows both outcomes, as it must.
- S5, the fallback. Options:
  - F-old;
  - F-prop, persisting in each state it counts;
  - sourcing published results by new district for each enacted plan before US-16 and US-27.
- S6, (b') beats the step-2 method. Options: the step-2 method as measured, or (b) re-declared as (b')'s fitted curve (two parameters).
- S7, a premise fails (a winner outside the two parties). The premise is STOPPED with its measurement. Options:
  - score that seat as misplaced for every method alike;
  - score it as undecided by interval;
  - amend P1 (SP-6b's third party).

ASKED IS REGISTERED, NOT FAILED. This reconciles the grain ('asks the ruling it informs, with its table'; 'nothing measured goes live unruled') with CLAUDE.md's 'ONE GREEN BAR PER COMMIT, NO EXCEPTIONS'.
- THE RULING RECORD. The instrument holds one record, mirrored by a line in the plan's R-US18 entry outside the fence. Its states:
  - NONE;
  - ASKED, with the section number of the ask and the decisive digest;
  - RULED, with the ruling's name, the option, and the decisive digest it was ruled on.
- THE DECISIVE DIGEST is SHA-256 over every figure steps 1-7 read under every reading: W per redrawn state-cycle, and H, G, N and K for every deciding and guarding row in D. It excludes the printed-only parts (C3, the game range, (b0), the U-blind line, and C5 until it is measured).
- NONE: a decided verdict passes ('code recommends X'). A stop FAILS both bars until its ask is registered, so the ask cannot be skipped.
- ASKED: the bars pass while the decisive digest equals the registered one. A moved digest fails until the ask is re-registered with the new table, and Elias is told.
- RULED: the bars pass while the generated outcome equals the outcome the ruling was made on.
  - A moved digest with the same outcome fails as moved pins, until the session re-pins and updates the record's digest in that commit, naming the old digest, the new one and 'outcome unchanged'.
  - A changed outcome fails until the session re-asks. It is never re-pinned. Causes include a correction to the [PROVISIONAL] catalog after R-K9's re-read, and C5.
  - A ruling on a decided verdict binds exactly as one on a stop.
- A ruling must name an option that can be built: (a) with F-old or F-prop; (b); (b'); or (c) reconciled to (a) or (b). Otherwise the record stays ASKED.
- US-16's GUARD: its Congress-election diagnostic asserts R-US18 RULED, with an option that can be built and a current digest. While not, it fails.

THE RULE'S DIGEST is SHA-256 of the fenced block, LF-normalised.
- The instrument holds the list of digests: the preregistered one, then one per amendment, each naming Elias's ruling (AMENDED (Fn): old -> new).
- The current fence must match the last digest on the list. A change without a ruling fails.
- After an amendment the instrument re-runs, and the ruling is checked against the new outcome as above.

CONVENTIONS ASKED BLIND, BEFORE THE FIRST RUN. With this spec, Elias is sent the declared defaults:
- D with C1;
- 2020 by the Clerk;
- MN and CO by the Bureau's list;
- Delta on US-5's own lines;
- (b)'s seat rule: anchored proportional, or (b')'s curve.
A convention he rules before the run replaces its default under the digest, and its S4 reading becomes printed only. A convention not ruled keeps its default and its S4 reading.

C5 RE-ASK. When C5 is measured, the rule is re-run over D plus C5, and step 1 over C5's redrawn states. Either giving an outcome other than the ruled one re-asks R-US18, and the bars fail until the ask is registered.

DATA FAILURES ARE DEFECTS, NOT ASKS. They fail the instrument until fixed:
- the truth gate or the delegations of record;
- districts or seats not equal to the apportionment, or a total not equal to 435;
- a state-cycle with no held/redrawn class;
- V(E) not equal to US-5's catalog;
- a method's output outside its bounds, or not summing to 435;
- a winner outside REP and DEM in 2016-2024 (caught by the gate);
- an Alaska 2022 row without its final round, or an L row without its finalists;
- a row outside N and U with final_r = final_d = 0;
- NC 2018's M not equal to 12;
- WA-8 2018 or VT 2024 not contested on the deciding count;
- a zero-swing identity that fails, P3's included;
- M = 0;
- sum x^2 = 0 in a (c) window;
- a rule digest not on the instrument's list;
- a pin moved, a measured row not pinned, or a stale block;
- a failure of GeneratedCatalogCheck's re-read.

PRINTED, NEVER A STOP:
- C3, and step 1 over all redrawn state-cycles;
- (b0), (b U-blind) and the no-change benchmark;
- (c)'s other fits, and a rho_hat <= 0, marked unplayable;
- the stops' figures under reading (ii);
- the control and contingent-election calls;
- the tilt flag and the adequacy line;
- the tie-breaks that fired;
- the game-range curves and the fallback's footprint.

A decided verdict prints 'code recommends X', and the bars stay green.

### Method (a)

(a) DISTRICT UNIFORM SWING ON THE MAP IN FORCE; A REDRAWN STATE COUNTED ON ITS OLD LINES' PATTERN (F-OLD).

INPUTS - all generated by Tools/us_house_prep.pl, re-read by GeneratedCatalogCheck, never typed:
- house_districts.csv (UsHouseDistricts.cs, the other part of UsPresidentialReturns): year, state, district (0 = at large), votes_r, votes_d, own_r, own_d, cands_r, cands_d, final_r, final_d, winner (R/D/O/-), flags (A C F L M N S U). final_r and final_d ALREADY EXIST (the record's reading 5; the earlier draft called them new): the deciding count - votes_r/votes_d, but each Louisiana runoff row (L) at its two finalists' December votes alone, and Alaska 2022 at the FEC's last round (DEM 137,263, REP 112,471); 0 where no votes were printed. Maine (C) is printed at its last round. Alaska 2024 stays at first choices (its rounds BILLED) and is never a base in a decisive cycle. GeneratedCatalogCheck already holds that every winner leads its deciding count.
- house_maps.csv: seats and lines_changed by state and election, 2016-2026. The tool gains two generated columns, on which nothing decides: in_force (equal to lines_changed, except Missouri 2026 = 0, from the 120th tab's saved note that the 2026 plan 'cannot be used'); and instrument (the map's instrument as reading 8 names it: 'court-ordered plan' for AL 2024; 'act' for NC 2020, LA 2024, NY 2024 and NC 2024; 'cosmetic' for MN 2018; '-' where no saved page says, which covers CO and PA 2018, GA 2024 and every 2022 and 2026 state). The facts sheet's 'court-ordered' for PA 2018 is on no saved page.
- V(E) is US-5's own-line series, read from house_by_state.csv (votes_r and votes_d: each party's own ballot line), never recomputed: 50.5454, 45.5534, 48.4437, 51.3968 and 51.3174 % for 2016-2024. The instrument asserts that the districts' own_r and own_d sum to it, as GeneratedCatalogCheck does. Delta = V(T) - V(B): C1 -4.992, C2 +2.890, C3 +2.953, C4 -0.079 pp. Under S4's reading (vi), V_dc(E) = sum final_r / sum (final_r + final_d) over the 435 races replaces V in every method; its swings are -4.871, +2.799, +3.008 and -0.048 pp, computed and pinned by the instrument.

1 SHARE, for each base district, decided in this order:
 (i) N (no winner, no votes; NC-9 2018 only): no share.
 (ii) U (unopposed, printed without votes): d = 1 if its winner is REP, 0 if DEM.
 (iii) ONE-PARTY FINAL (exactly one of final_r and final_d is zero): d = 1 if final_d = 0, and d = 0 if final_r = 0. This covers a race with no other-party candidate, a same-party top-two general and a same-party runoff.
 (iv) CONTESTED: d = final_r / (final_r + final_d). A Louisiana open field with both parties on its one ballot is contested, its candidates summed by party.
 Asserted:
 - no row outside N and U has final_r = final_d = 0;
 - North Carolina 2018 has M = 12 shares;
 - WA-8 2018 (Rossi's 'GOP', a declared Republican label) and VT 2024 (the joint 'Republican, Libertarian' line) are contested on the deciding count.
 Nothing is imputed.

2 IDENTITY. For every base row with a share, d > 0.5 if and only if its winner is REP, and d < 0.5 if and only if DEM. GeneratedCatalogCheck guarantees it: every winner leads its deciding count, and there is no tie for first. A failure is therefore a reading defect and fails the instrument; nothing is substituted.

3 SWING. dhat = d + Delta, plain additive, never re-solved to V(T). Clamping to [0,1] is for printing only, because the clamp cannot change a call.

4 HELD OR REDRAWN, read from house_maps.csv (binary).
- A state is redrawn for B->T if lines_changed = 1 at any election after B up to T, or if seats(T) != seats(B). Otherwise it is held.
- A state with one seat at both elections is held.
- MN 2018 and CO 2018 are redrawn. Reading them as held is S4's readings (iii) to (v).

5 HELD STATE. Each district is called on its own: REP if dhat > 0.5, DEM if dhat < 0.5, and at exactly 0.5 its base winner's party. Rhat_s = the number of REP calls.
- A held state whose prior is an unlabelled set (in play, after F-old) is counted as the set, each element's tie going to its stored call.
- A held district with no base share (none in the record, impossible in play) is counted with its state by F-old's quantile form over the state's remaining shares.

6 REDRAWN STATE: F-OLD. A district number is a label and is never matched across a redraw (Georgia's 2024 6th is mostly its 2022 13th). The state's base shares form an unlabelled set of M swung values, sorted x(1) <= ... <= x(M). N = seats(T).
- M = N: Rhat_s = #{x(j) > 0.5} + #{x(j) = 0.5 whose base winner is REP}. This is the count (a) makes on the old lines, so a redraw that moves no seat changes nothing.
- M != N (NC 2018->2020, where M = 12 and N = 13; or any change of seats):
  - q(k) = Q((k - 0.5)/N) for k = 1..N, where Q interpolates linearly through the points ((j - 0.5)/M, x(j)) and is flat beyond the end points;
  - Rhat_s = #{q(k) > 0.5};
  - a q(k) of exactly 0.5 goes to the party that held more of the state's base seats, then to the national base majority.
  - Checked: with M = N, the quantile form returns the plain count.
- M = 0 cannot occur: only N rows lack shares, and no state's base is all N. This is asserted.
F-PROP is printed, is step 1's alternative, and is used in play only if Elias rules it at S5. It counts the state as (b0) does: U seats go to their party, and the rest are proportional on the counted vote, Rhat_s = U_R + P(c_T, uhat_s) in (b)'s notation.

7 TOTALS. Dhat_s = n_s(T) - Rhat_s; Rhat = sum Rhat_s; Dhat = 435 - Rhat.
Every call asserts:
- 50 states;
- each n_s(T) equal to the apportionment in force (the 2010 census to 2020, the 2020 census from 2022, carried forward by R-US13 (a));
- a sum of 435;
- each Rhat_s in [0, n_s].
A failed assertion throws. There is no random draw.

8 OUTPUT on every call: the national seats; all 50 delegations with their class (REP majority, DEM majority or tied); every held district's dhat and call; and the redrawn states with the fallback that counted them and their instrument.

IN PLAY (US-16 on the presidential day; US-27 every even year)
- V(T) = REP/(REP+DEM) of the game's poll on polling day, taken before E1's candidate factors (R-US15 (a), asked). B is the previous House election: the record's 2022 for 5 Nov 2024, then the game's own (US-26, under R-US11 (a)). V(B) is the V that count used.
- STORED per state, unclamped, in the save (a format step at US-16):
  - labelled district shares where the state was held;
  - the F-old set (the x(j) or q(k)) with each element's call where F-old counted; this is the state's next prior;
  - and always u_s (the record's u_s plus the summed swings), which (b) and F-prop read after the game's own election, since no district votes are drawn (P18).
  Because no clamp is stored, every share is its base plus the summed swings, so rolling forward is exact.
- A state counted by F-prop (only under a ruling) has no district shares and stays on F-prop at every later election. Its footprint is printed in the game range.
- The redrawn states come from house_maps.csv's in_force reading:
  - 5 Nov 2024: AL, GA, LA, NY and NC (67 districts);
  - 3 Nov 2026: the nine states other than Missouri (173 districts), Missouri held by its saved note (all ten and 181 districts if a later read restores the plan);
  - past the rows read, the last map read stays in force.
- The chamber is set by ParliamentSystem.SetSeatsFromElection with REP = Rhat and DEM = 435 - Rhat. The delegations are stored with the contest and saved, for R-US3 on 6 January and for the night's board.

NOT MODELLED (stated): third-party winners; runoffs, ranked-choice transfers and Georgia's majority rule in play (with two parties the plurality is a majority); specials; party switches; incumbency.

### Method (b)

(b) STATE DELEGATIONS BY A STATE SWING, ANCHORED ON THE BASE DELEGATION.

INPUTS: (a)'s rows aggregated by state; n_s(T); Delta as in (a). (b) reads no map, so a redraw changes nothing in it. Its anchor is the base delegation, whatever lines it was won on.

1 STATE QUANTITIES AT THE BASE B
- u_s = sum final_r / sum (final_r + final_d) over the state's base districts with votes. One-party rows add the votes they cast; U and N rows add nothing. These are the deciding counts (a) reads.
- U_R and U_D: the base's unopposed (U) seats by party.
- m_B: the base's decided seats (NC 2018: 12).
- R_B: REP's decided base seats, U seats included.
- c_B = m_B - U_R - U_D: the decided base seats with votes.
- c_T = n_T - U_R - U_D, asserted >= 0.
- A state with c_B = 0 takes u_s = R_B / m_B. No state in the record does.

2 SWING. uhat_s = u_s + Delta: plain additive, never re-solved, stored unclamped. The clamp to [0,1] sits inside the seat rule P, as a bound on seats.

3 DELEGATION
- P(c, x) = the nearest integer to c * min(1, max(0, x)). That is the largest remainder, Hamilton's and Webster's rule alike for two parties. An exact half goes to the party that held more of the state's base seats, then to the national base majority, by one rule in every use of P.
- n_T = 1: REP if and only if uhat_s > 0.5; at exactly 0.5, the base winner. This is identical to (a) in a held at-large state, and the instrument checks it.
- n_T >= 2: Rhat_s = min(n_T - U_D, max(U_R, R_B + P(c_T, uhat_s) - P(c_B, u_s))). That is the base delegation plus the proportional change in the state's counted seats.
  - U seats stay their party's (P6), as (a)'s 1 and 0 stay for any swing under 50 pp.
  - North Carolina's undecided base seat (2018) and C3's seat changes need no special case: c_T != c_B carries the extra or lost seats at the state's share.
- Dhat_s = n_T - Rhat_s, and Rhat = sum Rhat_s.
- IDENTITY, asserted: at Delta = 0 with c_T = c_B, Rhat_s = R_B for every state.
  - (a), (b) and (c) therefore all return their base at zero swing.
  - They differ only in their response: (a) district by district, (b) proportionally within the state, (c) by one national curve.

4 OUTPUT: as (a), with the same assertions, but without district calls.

PRINTED BENCHMARKS
- (b0), UNANCHORED PROPORTIONAL (the earlier draft's (b)), never deciding:
  - Rhat_s = U_R + P(c_T, uhat_s) for n_T >= 2, plurality at one seat.
  - At Delta = 0 against the base delegations, it gives the proportional level error, printed apart from any response.
  - It is also F-prop, step 1's alternative fallback.
- (b U-BLIND), never deciding: (b) with c_B = m_B and c_T = n_T, so U seats are spread at the counted share. It is a sensitivity line.
- (b'), THE STRAWMAN GUARD: it can stop the rule (S6), and is never recommended.
  - A two-parameter logit seats-votes curve: p(u) = 1/(1 + exp(-(alpha + k * ln(u/(1-u))))), with u clamped to [1e-9, 1-1e-9].
  - It is fitted each cycle on the base alone, with no T data, by binomial maximum likelihood over the base's states with n_B >= 2, c_B >= 1 and 0 < u_s < 1. Each state contributes R_B - U_R successes in c_B trials.
  - Newton from (alpha, k) = (0, 1), converged when the step's largest component is below 1e-12, at most 100 iterations. Non-convergence (separation included) or k <= 0 prints 'not fitted'.
  - Anchored as (b): Rhat_s = min(n_T - U_D, max(U_R, R_B + round(c_T * p(uhat_s)) - round(c_B * p(u_s)))), with halves as in P and plurality at n_T = 1.

IN PLAY: stored per state, unclamped: R_s (the count's delegation), u_s, U_R and U_D. They roll forward (US-26), U seats stay their party's, and a redraw changes nothing in (b).

### Method (c)

(c) THE ANCHORED BILOGIT: ONE FITTED PARAMETER.

FORM: logit Shat(T) = logit S(T-1) + rho * [logit V(T) - logit V(T-1)], where logit x = ln(x/(1-x)).
- Rhat = the nearest integer to 435 * Shat. An exact half goes to T-1's majority. Dhat = 435 - Rhat.
- V is (a)'s series (V_dc under S4's reading (vi)), clamped to [1e-9, 1-1e-9].
- The anchor reads the map's bias from T-1, the same starting point (a) and (b) take. The logit keeps any landslide inside 0..435.

SEAT BASIS: S = REP seats / decided seats:
- 2016: 241/435
- 2018: 199/434
- 2020: 213/435 (212/434 under S4's reading (i))
- 2022: 222/435
- 2024: 220/435
No fractional seat is used.

FIT: least squares through the origin: rho_hat = sum(x*y) / sum(x^2), where x = logit V(t) - logit V(t-1) and y = logit S(t) - logit S(t-1).

WINDOW, in the backtest direction (US-5: neither the predicted election nor any later one is an input). It holds every transition whose later election precedes T, except a transition in which more than half of the 435 seats were redrawn or re-apportioned.
- That count is read from house_maps.csv: the seats of states with lines_changed = 1 or a change of seats. The rule is declared before the run.
- It leaves out C3 (429 seats: the census redistricting resets the maps by statute, not in response to votes). It keeps C1 (33 seats), C2 (13) and C4 (67).
- C1: no window; prints '-'.
- C2: {C1}.
- C3 (printed): {C1, C2}.
- C4: {C1, C2}.
- In play, if ever ruled: {C1, C2, C4}. Rho is fixed from the record, and the anchor is the game's previous House.

PRINTED, NEVER DECIDING:
- every (x, y);
- the leave-one-out rho_hat and errors for every cycle, labelled 'fitted on later elections' where they are;
- the fit with every transition included (C3 too);
- the fit on HELD STATES ONLY, with x and y from the states held across each transition (their own-line votes in house_by_state.csv, their decided seats). It shows how far C1's Pennsylvania and C2's North Carolina redraws move rho, the reason that leaves C3 out applied to them.
A rho_hat <= 0 is printed and marked unplayable.

CHECK: at V(T) = V(T-1), Shat = S(T-1) exactly.

DELEGATIONS: none, so (c) is never recommended alone (R-US3). If Elias rules its total in, the step-2 method's delegations are reconciled to it:
- Dlt = Rhat_c - Rhat_m.
- Move |Dlt| seats toward the party Dlt favours, nearest to flipping first. Every distance is a vote share:
  - an (a) held district: |dhat - 0.5|, among the other party's calls;
  - an F-old state: |x - 0.5| (or |q - 0.5|), among the other party's set calls;
  - a (b) state: |uhat_s - (j + 0.5)/c_T|, where j = P(c_T, uhat_s), for a REP gain; or |uhat_s - (j - 0.5)/c_T| for a DEM gain. A state at its clamp bound is excluded.
- Ties go by postal code, then district.
- The result is asserted to sum to 435, to every n_s, and to every bound.

### The premises

**P1.** Two parties. Every share is REP's share of REP+DEM. In 2016-2024 the truth gate holds every winner to REP or DEM. In a newly read cycle (C5), a winner outside them fails this premise and the rule STOPS (S7) with that measurement. In play the plurality is a majority, so runoffs, ranked choice and Georgia's majority rule have nothing to decide.

*Reason.* The game's US roster holds two parties, and the record seated no one else in 2016-2024. A third-party run is SP-6b, under R-US4 (a). The grain says a premise that fails is STOPPED with the measurement that killed it, so it is not filed as a data defect.

**P2.** Candidate basis, on the deciding count. Each candidate's lines are summed (fusion). The candidate's party is the first R or D label in the record's closed label table (reading 3):
- 'GOP' (WA-8 2018) is Republican;
- Democratic-Farmer-Labor and Democratic-Nonpartisan League are Democratic;
- 'Republican/Tax Revolt' is Republican;
- Vermont 2024's joint 'Republican, Libertarian' line is Republican;
- write-ins are other.
The deciding count is the catalog's final_r and final_d (reading 5): Maine's last round; Louisiana's December finalists alone; for Alaska 2022, the FEC's last round; otherwise the general as printed.

*Reason.* Counting party lines calls seven winners wrong. A share must come from one ballot. A base that misreads its own winner, as Alaska 2022 does on first choices summed by party, carries that error into C4 and into the first election the game plays.

**P3.** Identity. At zero swing (a) returns every base winner, and (b), (b') and (c) return their base wherever seats are unchanged. GeneratedCatalogCheck already guarantees (a)'s identity (every winner leads its deciding count; no tie for first). Each identity is asserted, and a failure is a defect: nothing is substituted.

*Reason.* The identity makes each method's error a response error rather than a level error. Substituting a value would hide a misreading.

**P4.** The swing is the poll's: Delta = V(T) - V(B) on US-5's own-line series. In play, V is the game's poll before E1's factors. The deciding-count basis is a declared alternative reading (S4 (vi)).

*Reason.* The game feeds the count its poll. Under R-US15 (a) the poll is the parties' own-line House vote, so the backtest swings by that series. The offset between that series and the deciding-count basis the shares are read on is NOT constant:
- WA-8 2018's GOP line and Vermont 2024's joint line sit outside the own lines;
- Alaska 2022 and Louisiana's runoffs are read differently.
So the swings differ: own-line -4.99, +2.89, +2.95 and -0.079 pp, against deciding-count -4.87, +2.80, +3.01 and -0.048 pp. A district or a rounding inside that gap could move a strict integer comparison.

**P5.** Plain additive and never re-solved. In (a) the clamp is for printing only. In (b), (b0) and (b') the clamp is a bound inside the seat rule. In play, shares are stored unclamped.

*Reason.* A re-solve would push the basis offset into every unit and change close calls; §689's re-solve would do exactly that. The clamp cannot change a call in (a). Unclamped storage makes the roll forward exact.

**P6.** One-party and unopposed seats.
- In (a), one-party seats keep the record's share, 1 or 0, moving with the swing, and an unopposed seat takes 1 or 0 by its winner.
- In (b), (b0), (b') and F-prop, an unopposed seat stays its party's, and the state's response or proportion is taken on its counted seats.
Nothing is imputed.

*Reason.* No swing of record reaches an imputation's flip point, so the record cannot test one, and an imputed value would be [AUTHORED-DRAFT]. Holding unopposed seats in every method makes (a) and (b) treat Florida's no-opposition law alike, so their difference stays the rule from votes to seats.

**P7.** Undecided seats are scored by interval: NC-9 2018 always, and NY-22 2020 under reading (i). The 2020 truth is the Clerk's 213 of 435. [HH-DIV]'s 212 of 434 is S4's reading (i).

*Reason.* Every method is treated alike, scores are integers and no seat is guessed. The game counts all 435 seats by plurality and models no certification, so the Clerk's reading matches its model. If the one seat's reading would decide the ruling, the rule asks.

**P8.** Held or redrawn is read from house_maps.csv, which is binary: lines changed, or seats changed. A district number is never matched across a redraw. Minnesota and Colorado 2018 read as held are S4's readings (iii) to (v).

*Reason.* The catalog's own source decides, so no one judges a change's size with results in sight. The two readings the source leaves open (Minnesota's change 'cosmetic', Colorado's undescribed) are both run rather than chosen.

**P9.** F-old is (a)'s fallback, in quantile form when M != N. F-prop (unopposed seats held, the rest proportional) is printed. If F-prop misplaces strictly fewer seats over D's redrawn state-cycles, the rule STOPS (S5) rather than switch.

*Reason.* F-old is (a)'s own form, extending US-4's DECLARED stand-in without its matching of numbers, and a cosmetic redraw costs it nothing. Neither fallback can see a redraw's intent. The record's redraws are of mixed kinds:
- Alabama 2024 has a court-ordered plan;
- North Carolina, Louisiana and New York have enacted acts;
- PA 2018 and GA 2024 are undescribed on disk;
- play's 2026 redraws are unread.
The fallback would count 173 to 181 districts in 2026, and F-prop would persist in each. A choice that large, on evidence of uncertain kind, is asked rather than made by the rule.

**P10.** (b) is anchored: the base delegation plus the proportional change in the state's counted seats (largest remainder), with plurality at one seat. It has no parameter. Three variants are printed: (b0) (unanchored), (b U-blind), and (b') (an anchored fitted curve). Only (b') can stop the rule (S6).

*Reason.* Anchored, (b) returns its base at zero swing as (a) and (c) do. The three then differ only in their response, so the comparison tests what the plan's expectation is about: district information where maps held. An unanchored (b) would instead carry proportionality's level error in every cycle, a term the no-swing C4 would charge to it alone, so the rule could never return it. The plan reserves its one fitted parameter for (c). (b') checks that a weak rule does not pass for a weak method.

**P11.** (c) is the anchored bilogit on decided seats, fitted forward. A transition with more than half the House's seats redrawn or re-apportioned is left out of its window; the count comes from house_maps.csv. (c) can stop the rule only on two forward swing cycles.

*Reason.* Its one parameter is spent on responsiveness. The backtest direction follows US-5. A census redistricting resets the maps by statute, not in response to votes. The threshold is declared from the catalog before the run, and every other fit is printed. One forward swing cycle cannot carry a question, any more than it can carry a switch.

**P12.** Ties stay with the base.
- A district at exactly 0.5 goes to its base winner.
- A rounding half goes to the state's base majority, then to the national base majority.
- A half in (c) goes to T-1's majority.

*Reason.* No swing past the line means no change, and the count stays deterministic.

**P13.** The count is a pure function with no draw: 435 seats in 50 states at the apportionment in force, asserted on every call.

*Reason.* R-US5 (a), 2 U.S.C. 2a, US-16's done-when, and R-US3's need for every delegation.

**P14.** The base in play is the previous House election: the record's 2022, then the game's own.

*Reason.* R-US11 (a), RULED, as US-26 words it; facts §2.

**P15.** (a) against (b) is decided on held-state seats misplaced (H). A switch to (b) needs all of:
- strictly fewer in each swing cycle;
- no more in the level cycle;
- fewer over the whole House (G);
- no fewer delegation classes right (K) in any cycle.
The national error can only stop the rule.

*Reason.* Held states are where the plan's expectation applies and where the two methods differ in response. G covers the redrawn states, and K covers the classes R-US3 reads. Consistency keeps one cycle from carrying a switch. The national error gives two swing-cycle numbers, so it can raise a question but not make a switch.

**P16.** D = {C1, C2, C4}. C1 and C2 are the swing cycles and C4 the level cycle. C3 is printed. The item's own cycles {C2, C4} must agree with D on the method and the fallback (S4 (ii)).

*Reason.* C1 holds the record's largest swing and is a midterm, the kind of election a 2024-start game meets first. C4's Delta of -0.08 pp leaves every anchored method at its base, so C4 can test structure and veto, but cannot measure a response. Play meets no census redistricting before the 2030 apportionment is sourced. C1 departs from the item's list, so it may only turn a verdict into an ask.

**P17.** Preregistration with an amendment path. The rule's text sits in one fenced block of the plan's R-US18 entry, with the ruling lines outside it. Its digest is checked. It changes only by a ruling of Elias's that names the old and new digests.

*Reason.* This guards against forking paths: a red bar cannot be turned green by retuning the rule. Elias amends rulings (F8: R-US6 and R-US9), so an amendment must have a recorded path rather than read as a defect.

**P18.** No House vote breakdown is drawn; the board shows seats and delegations. The state shares (b) and F-prop need are stored as the record's share plus the summed swings.

*Reason.* §689's sum-to-national requirement applies to a displayed breakdown, and re-solving the shares would change the count (P5).

**P19.** Asked is registered, not failed.
- A stop fails both bars until its ask is registered with the decisive table's digest.
- A registered ask passes while that table holds.
- A ruling binds the outcome it was made on, decided or stopped.
- US-16's count is guarded on R-US18 being RULED.

*Reason.* The grain wants the instrument in the cheap bar, asking its ruling with its table. CLAUDE.md wants one green bar per commit, with no exceptions. Failing on an ask would keep the asking commit off main. Registration keeps 'nothing measured goes live unruled' through US-16's guard, and the ask cannot be skipped, because an unregistered stop fails.

**P20.** Every declared reading runs. The rule is re-run under each alternative the spec declares:
- [HH-DIV]'s 2020;
- the item's own cycles;
- Minnesota and Colorado held;
