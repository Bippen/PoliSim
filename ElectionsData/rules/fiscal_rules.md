## EU: Treaty reference values (apply to Sweden, Germany, France, Italy and Poland)

- **Rule:** the Commission assesses budgetary discipline against two reference values. These are a deficit of 3 % and a debt of 60 % of GDP at market prices (general government).
- **Instrument 1:** Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Art. 126(1)–(2) (ex Art. 104 TEC), consolidated version OJ C 202, 7.6.2016, p. 99 (CELEX 12016E126).
  - Quote (en): "1. Member States shall avoid excessive government deficits. 2. […] (a) whether the ratio of the planned or actual government deficit to gross domestic product exceeds a reference value, unless […] (b) whether the ratio of government debt to gross domestic product exceeds a reference value, unless the ratio is sufficiently diminishing and approaching the reference value at a satisfactory pace. The reference values are specified in the Protocol on the excessive deficit procedure annexed to the Treaties."

## Sweden: budget-balance target, debt anchor and expenditure ceiling

- **Rule:** the target for general-government net lending (*finansiellt sparande*) is decided by the Riksdag on the Government's proposal (Budgetlag 2 kap. 1 §). It is **1/3 % of GDP on average over a business cycle**. The Riksdag decided on **2025-11-26** to replace it with **balance (0 %) over a business cycle from 2027**. A **debt anchor of 35 % of GDP** (gross consolidated Maastricht debt) is a benchmark, not an operative target. The Riksdag also fixes a nominal **expenditure ceiling** for the third coming year.
- **Thresholds:**
  - Net lending target: **+1/3 % of GDP** over a cycle (until and including 2026). **0 % of GDP over a cycle from 2027** (balansmål).
  - Deviation benchmark (in Skr. 2025/26:76, not in statute): structural net lending deviating by **more than 0.5 % of potential GDP** from the target.
  - Debt anchor: **35 % of GDP**. If the ratio deviates by **more than 5 % of GDP** (up or down), the Government must explain in a skrivelse with the spring fiscal policy bill.
  - Expenditure ceiling (central government incl. the old-age pension system): **SEK 1 936 bn (2026), 2 009 bn (2027), 2 105 bn (2028)**.
  - Carve-out (parliamentary agreement of June 2025, as reported in bet. 2025/26:FiU1): a deviation from the target is allowed for new defence spending and support to Ukraine during **2026–2034**.
- **Instrument 1, statute:** *Budgetlag (2011:203)*, 2 kap. 1 §, 1 a §, 2 §, 4 § (ändrad t.o.m. SFS 2025:1430).
  - Quote (sv): "1 § Regeringen ska till riksdagen lämna förslag till mål för den offentliga förvaltningens finansiella sparande. In the budget bill it must propose a ceiling on central-government expenditure for the third coming budget year. If a decided ceiling risks being exceeded, it must act or propose measures to the Riksdag. The statute contains **no number**; the numbers are Riksdag decisions.

## Germany: Schuldenbremse (Grundgesetz)

- **Rule:** federal and Land budgets must in principle be balanced without borrowing. The federation complies if net borrowing does not exceed **0.35 % of nominal GDP**. Cyclical effects are treated symmetrically, and deviations are tracked on a control account to be reduced above 1.5 % of GDP. Since the **2025 amendment**, defence, civil-protection, intelligence, IT-security and aid-to-attacked-states spending **above 1 % of GDP** is deducted from the borrowing that counts, and the Länder as a whole may borrow up to **0.35 % of GDP**. An emergency clause allows exceeding the limits by a Bundestag majority with a repayment plan. A **EUR 500 bn special fund** for infrastructure and climate neutrality (12-year horizon) has its own credit authorisation, to which Art. 109(3) and 115(2) do not apply.
- **Thresholds:** Bund **0.35 %** of nominal GDP (structural net borrowing). Exemption for the listed security spending **above 1 %** of GDP. Control-account threshold **1.5 %** of GDP. Länder (aggregate) **0.35 %** of GDP. Special fund **EUR 500 bn**, of which EUR 100 bn goes to the Länder and EUR 100 bn to the Klima- und Transformationsfonds. No debt-ratio rule in the GG.
- **Instrument:** *Grundgesetz*, Art. 109 Abs. 3, Art. 115 Abs. 2, Art. 143h. Stand: "Zuletzt geändert durch Art. 1 G v. 22.3.2025 I Nr. 94" (Vollzitat: "… zuletzt durch Artikel 1 des Gesetzes vom 22. März 2025 (BGBl. 2025 I Nr. 94) geändert worden ist").
  - Quote (de, Art. 115 Abs. 2 Sätze 1–2, 4–5, 7): "Einnahmen und Ausgaben sind grundsätzlich ohne Einnahmen aus Krediten auszugleichen. ## France: no binding national numeric limit

- **Rule (stated plainly):** France has **no binding national numeric ceiling on its deficit or its debt** in the constitutional and organic texts. The Constitution sets only an *objective* of balanced public accounts. The organic law (LOLF) requires each loi de programmation des finances publiques (LPFP), an ordinary law, to fix the medium-term objective (OMT, in the sense of the TSCG) and the structural and headline balance trajectories. The only national numbers with legal consequence are **deviation thresholds** (≥ 0.5 % of GDP in one year, or ≥ 0.25 % of GDP per year on average over two consecutive years, from the LPFP's structural-balance path). These trigger an opinion by the Haut Conseil des finances publiques (HCFP) and an explain-and-correct duty for the Government. There is no ceiling and no sanction. The binding numeric limits that apply to France are the EU's (3 % / 60 %).
- **Thresholds:** none on levels. Deviation trigger **0.5 % of GDP (one year)** or **0.25 % of GDP per year (two-year average)** relative to the LPFP path.
- **Instrument 1:** *Constitution du 4 octobre 1958*, art. 34 (penultimate paragraphs).
  - Quote (fr): "Les orientations pluriannuelles des finances publiques sont définies par des lois de programmation. Elles s'inscrivent dans l'objectif d'équilibre des comptes des administrations publiques."
  - Gloss: multi-year public-finance orientations are set by programming laws, and they fall within the objective of balanced general-government accounts. This is an objective with no number.

## Italy: constitutional balanced budget (Art. 81) and legge 243/2012

- **Rule:** the State must ensure balance between revenue and expenditure, taking account of the adverse and favourable phases of the cycle. Borrowing is allowed only to account for cyclical effects or, with an absolute-majority authorisation of both Chambers, on exceptional events. By statute, "balance" **is** the EU medium-term objective (a structural balance), with an adjustment path where the EU allows. Where debt/GDP exceeds the EU reference value, budget targets must provide for reducing the excess.
- **Thresholds:** the structural balance must meet the **OMT** (a number set under EU rules, not in the Italian text) or the adjustment path towards it. For debt, the **EU reference value (60 %)**. There is no separate national debt number.
- **Instrument 1:** *Costituzione*, art. 81 (text from L. cost. 20 aprile 2012, n. 1, applicable from financial year 2014; "Testo in vigore dal: 8-5-2012").
  - Quote (it): "Lo Stato assicura l'equilibrio tra le entrate e le spese del proprio bilancio, tenendo conto delle fasi avverse e delle fasi favorevoli del ciclo economico. Il ricorso all'indebitamento è consentito solo al fine di considerare gli effetti del ciclo economico e, previa autorizzazione delle Camere adottata a maggioranza assoluta dei rispettivi componenti, al verificarsi di eventi eccezionali."
  - Gloss: the State ensures balance between the revenue and expenditure of its budget, taking account of the adverse and favourable phases of the business cycle. Borrowing is allowed only to account for cyclical effects and, after authorisation by both Chambers by an absolute majority of their members, when exceptional events occur.

## Poland: constitutional debt ceiling (3/5 of GDP), prudential thresholds, stabilising expenditure rule

- **Rule:** the State may not borrow or grant guarantees that would make *państwowy dług publiczny* (public debt on the national definition, announced under ufp art. 38) exceed **3/5 of annual GDP**. The public finance act adds automatic prudential procedures at **55 %** and **60 %**. The 50 % step (art. 86 ust. 1 pkt 1) is repealed. A **stabilising expenditure rule** (ufp art. 112aa) caps the expenditure of the central-government units, funds and BGK funds it lists by a formula. A downward **correction** applies when the latest Commission forecast shows a general-government deficit beyond **3 %** of GDP or general-government (EU-definition) debt above **60 %** of GDP.
- **Thresholds:** constitutional **60 % (3/5)** of GDP. Statutory **> 55 % and < 60 %**: a draft budget with no state-budget deficit, or a deficit that brings the State Treasury debt ratio down; a public-pay freeze; pension indexation limited to CPI; and others. **≥ 60 %**: as above plus a recovery programme to the Sejm within a month, local-government budgets with expenditure ≤ revenue, and no new guarantees. SRW correction trigger: EC-forecast deficit **< −3 %** of GDP or EU debt **> 60 %** of GDP.

## United States: dollar debt limit; no ratio rule; statutory PAYGO

- **Rule (stated plainly):** the United States has **no debt-to-GDP or deficit-to-GDP rule**. Federal debt is capped by a **statutory dollar limit** on the face amount of obligations (31 U.S.C. § 3101(b)), which Congress raises or suspends by statute. The current limit was set by **Pub. L. 119-21 (H.R. 1, approved 4 July 2025) § 72001, +USD 5 trillion**. As of the latest Treasury statement fetched (31 Aug 2026) the limit is **in force, not suspended**. Separately, the **Statutory Pay-As-You-Go Act of 2010** (2 U.S.C. §§ 931–939) enforces budget neutrality of new revenue and direct-spending legislation through scorecards and sequestration. It is a procedural rule with no level.
