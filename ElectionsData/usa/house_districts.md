# USA — the House of Representatives by district, 2016–2024, and the maps in force, 2016–2026 [SOURCED] [PROVISIONAL]

Class: SOURCED (PS-6 US-11, `COMPLETED.md` §790, 2026-10-06). `[PROVISIONAL]` until a second session re-verifies (R-K9) — for every parsed figure a re-run of the tool over the saved pages.

**What this is.** Every race of the five House general elections of record, 2016–2024 — 435 a year — and, for each election from 2016 to 2026, which states' district lines had changed since the previous House election: generated by `Tools/us_house_prep.pl` from pages saved byte for byte under `raw/` (each group's `fetch_log.txt` carries every URL as reached, the UTC time and the HTTP status, the refusals among them; `SHA256SUMS.txt` every file's digest). US-5 read the same Clerk publications' national recapitulation (`president_returns.md`, reading 13); this tool reads each state's own listing and recapitulation. The design the item measures on this data — the three seat methods and R-US18's rule — is DECLARED in `docs/specs/USA_STAGE_PLAN.md` (US-11).

- `house_districts.csv` — a row a race a year: the candidates' votes by party (a candidate's ballot lines summed; his party the first of his labels that is Republican or Democratic), the race's total as its row of the state's recapitulation prints it, the candidates' own single Republican and Democratic lines (US-5's basis), how many of each party the Clerk printed, the deciding count by party (reading 5), the winner, and the flags its header lists;
- `house_maps.csv` — a row a state an election, 2016–2026: the seats the apportionment in force gives it (the Census Bureau's Table 1) and whether its lines had changed since the previous House election, with the page that says so; since §799 also `in_force` - the lines changed, but where a saved page says the new plan was not used (Missouri 2026, the 120th tab's note) - which R-US18's held and redrawn read, and `instrument`, printed only: what made each change, as reading 8 names it, held to words on its saved page (Minnesota's 2016 and 2018 changes "cosmetic", North Carolina's two session laws, Alabama's court-ordered plan, Louisiana's and New York's acts; "-" where no saved page says);
- `house_years.csv` — a row an election: the Congress it elected and its party division by the House Historian (`[HH-DIV]`, `raw/executive/history_house_party_divisions.html`), and the seat its footnote leaves unassigned (no certificate before the opening day);
- `Assets/Scripts/Elections/Generated/UsHouseDistricts.cs` — the three CSVs as C# literals, each CSV's SHA-256, and every page the run read (`HouseRawSources`): the other part of the partial class `UsPresidentialReturns` (its first part, `UsPresidentialReturns.cs`, is `Tools/us_returns_prep.pl`'s). `GeneratedCatalogCheck` (the cheap bar) re-hashes the pages and re-reads the CSVs figure by figure, and holds: the races' years the record's, each maps year the 50 states with 435 seats; each election 435 races, each state numbered to its seats; no race's parts above its total; every winner leading its race's deciding count; the winners counted to the House Historian's division with its vacancy; and the districts summed to the 50 House rows of each election (US-5).

`ElectionsData/.gitattributes` keeps every House CSV out of line-ending conversion (`usa/house_*.csv -text`), as it keeps the raw pages. No game path reads the House part yet: `UnwiredSubsystemCheck` counts it reached through its class's name, which `ElectoralCollege.cs` holds — itself unreached until US-8 wires the presidency; part two's instrument and US-16 are its readers to come. (That check was corrected in this commit: a file declaring the same type, a partial type's other part, is not a mention of it.)

## What the tool proves

It writes nothing and dies on any mismatch, naming every mismatch it has found, unless all of these hold (the full list heads the tool):

- every page it reads is the bytes of its group's `SHA256SUMS.txt` line;
- each census's Table 1, read from its PDF (`us_returns_prep.pl` reads the same tables' workbooks), apportions 435 Representatives over 50 states, and the 2020 table's change column is the two tables' difference, state by state;
- `[HH-DIV]` has a row for each of the 115th–119th Congresses, each summing to 435 (less one where a footnote marks a vacancy); the two footnoted vacancies are the ones the tool declares — North Carolina's Ninth District for the 116th, New York's Twenty-Second for the 117th — and no other Congress carries a footnote;
- each Census page carries its sentence naming the states that changed lines for its election, the count word equal to the list's length, every state known and named once; for 2022, the exceptions to "all states" are exactly the states the 2020 census gives one seat; the geography page's state for the 117th is the 117th tab's; the 2026 page's note on Missouri is read and kept;
- in each Clerk volume: the page numbers rise a page at a time; every state is listed once FOR UNITED STATES REPRESENTATIVE; each state's districts are 1 to its seats (AT LARGE for one); no district twice; no entry without its figure, no figure without its entry; every recapitulation row's cells sum to its Total, every district has a row and every row a district; each district's listing sums to its row's Total once its footnote marks are read — a mark is one of the state's own footnote numbers glued to a figure, the reading must be the only one that closes the listing, and any figure not grouped by thousands must be one of them; no footnote number twice in a state; a mark on a district's number only on the race printed without votes, its footnote ordering a new election; an unopposed seat alone, its footnote a no-opposition law, its row empty and marked the same; a footnote glued to a figure only of the kinds read (a runoff, a ranked round, a replaced candidate), a runoff's or round's marking exactly two finalists; every party line in a fusion state a declared label with a candidate above it; no entry without a name outside a fusion state but the one declared, and that one read; every label naming a major party declared (reading 3); no tie for first; and **the second reading** — `pdftotext -table`, which sets each entry's label and figure on one line by where they stand on the page — giving, state by state, the same labels and figures in the same order as the first (reading 1);
- the winners give `[HH-DIV]` every year, but for the two vacancies: in 2018 the one race without a winner is North Carolina's 9th; in 2020 the Clerk's winner in New York's 22nd is the Republican, whom the division does not count;
- the districts sum, state by state, to `house_by_state.csv` (US-5's catalog, from the Clerk's national recapitulation): the own Republican and Democratic lines and the totals, each of the 250 state-years once;
- the FEC's per-candidate House sheet (2016–2022): every district's winner by party is the Clerk's, its W marks all one FEC id, but the one declared exception, held to what it says (reading 10); North Carolina's uncertified 9th is "**" on every general candidate; Alaska's 2022 general column is the Clerk's first choices, party by party, and its rounds are read; the ranked-choice races are the declared ones, each held to the FEC's footnote giving the general election's later rounds (2016–2022) or to its own page (2024), and no other race's footnote gives such rounds (reading 6);
- every race's deciding count names its winner (reading 5);
- no perl warning arises during the checks, and every output is ASCII. All four outputs are built and tested before the first is written.

## Readings

1. **The source — each state's own listing, held to its own recapitulation, read twice (DECLARED).** The Clerk of the House's statistics print, state by state, "FOR UNITED STATES REPRESENTATIVE": each district's entries — a candidate with his party labels, a party line, or a line that is no candidate — then their figures in the same order, and after the state's races its "Recapitulation of Votes Cast in <State>", a row a district. Every race is held to its row's Total, so a figure misread, an entry dropped or a page number taken for a figure fails the run. The figures are paired with their entries by order in `pdftotext -raw`'s text; `pdftotext -table` sets each entry's label and its figure on one line by where they stand on the page, and the tool requires the two readings to agree, state by state, entry for entry — a figure transposed within a race cannot pass. One publication every year, the same the US-5 series reads; the FEC's workbooks (2016–2022) are the cross-check of the winners, the ranked-choice counts and Alaska's 2022 rounds (reading 10), and 2024 rests on the Clerk alone (the FEC's 2024 volume is not served, `raw/returns/fetch_log.txt`). The text is xpdf 4.06's: `-raw`'s lines end CRLF, a page's first line follows its form feed, a page's number is its last line, and names carry Latin-1 bytes — no name goes into the catalog.
2. **Footnote marks glued to figures.** `-raw` sets a superscript mark before the figure it marks, so "277,671" is mark 2 and 77,671. Five races carry such marks: Louisiana's 3rd and 4th in 2016 (marks 2 and 3, the December runoff), Ohio's 8th in 2016 (mark 1, "This candidate replaces Democrat Corey Foister, who withdrew from the race"), Louisiana's 5th in 2020 (mark 1, the runoff) and Maine's 2nd in 2022 (mark 1, "from round 2" — printed "1146,142", grouped by no reading but the mark's). The tool reads a mark only where it is one of the state's own footnote numbers and the reading is the one subset of such figures that closes the listing on the row's Total, which carries no marks. North Carolina's 9th in 2018 carries its mark on its number ("1 9. See explanation below").
3. **A candidate, his lines and his party.** In New York and Connecticut (and South Carolina in 2016) a candidate stands on several ballot lines; the Clerk prints the first line with his name and each further line with its party alone. The tool adds each such line to the candidate above it, and only a label it declares counts as one — the twenty these volumes print ("Conservative", "Working Families", "Blue Lives Matter", "Common Sense Suffolk" and the rest; with a comma, "Conservative, Common Sense"); any other bare label stops the run. In six races the largest single ballot line is another candidate's than the winner's (the run lists them), so counting lines alone would elect the wrong one there. A candidate's party is the first of his labels that is Republican or Democratic, and every label naming a major party is declared: Minnesota's Democratic-Farmer-Labor and North Dakota's Democratic-Nonpartisan League are Democratic; New York's 2016 "Republican/Tax Revolt" is Republican, as US-5's series counts it; **Washington's 2018 "GOP" (Dino Rossi, the 8th district) is Republican** — the label the state's recapitulation heads a column of its own with, which the national recapitulation counts among "Other Parties", so his votes are `votes_r` but not `own_r`; the write-in candidates' "Write-in (Republican)" and "Write-in (Democratic)" are other, as US-5's columns count them. Vermont's 2024 Republican on the joint "Republican, Libertarian" line is likewise a Republican candidate whose line is not US-5's own Republican line (`own_r` 0 there, as `house_by_state.csv` has it). Outside the fusion states an entry with no name is a candidate the Clerk printed unnamed: one, Virginia's 2nd in 2020 ("Independent"), declared to the tool.
4. **Lines that are not candidates** — write-ins, scattering, blank, void, over and under votes, others, miscellaneous, Maine's continuing and exhausted ballots — count in the race's total, never in a candidate's.
5. **The winner of record, and the deciding count.** The winner is the plurality of the candidates' totals — not the order printed (the winner stands first in only some races) nor the recapitulation (a party column adds a same-party race's two candidates together, and Alaska's two Republicans in 2022 outpoll the Democrat who won). Where a footnote marks a runoff or a ranked-choice round, the finalists it marks are the pool: Louisiana's 3rd and 4th in 2016 and 5th in 2020, whose November figures for the eliminated stand beside the finalists' December ones (in the 5th in 2020 a November figure is the largest printed); Maine's 2nd in 2022. An unopposed seat whose name was not printed on the ballot goes to its one name, with no votes (11 races: Florida's, Oklahoma's and Louisiana's no-opposition laws, each cited by its footnote). North Carolina's 9th in 2018 has no winner (no votes printed; the state ordered a new election). Georgia's majority rule never binds in a House general of 2016–2024 (no runoff is printed). **The deciding count** (`final_r`, `final_d`) is each race's Republican and Democratic votes in the count that decided it: the candidates' figures, but in Louisiana's runoff districts the two finalists' December votes alone, and for Alaska 2022 the FEC's last ranked-choice round (below) — the tool requires it to name every race's winner. The result is `[HH-DIV]` in every year but its two vacancies — and in 2020 the Clerk prints New York's 22nd (Tenney, the Republican, 156,098 to Brindisi's 155,989), a seat the division leaves out (its footnote 6: no certificate before the opening day), so the districts count 213 Republicans where the division prints 212 of 434.
6. **Ranked choice, as the Clerk prints it (declared race by race, flags C and A, each held to a page).** Maine's 2nd district at its finalists' last round: 2018 with no note and only the two finalists — the FEC's 2018 footnote says the general "went into ranked-choice voting rounds" and gives "the final-round vote totals", Golden 142,440 and Poliquin 138,931, the Clerk's figures; 2022 by its own footnote ("from round 2"), the eliminated Independent's and the write-ins' first-round figures beside the finalists, and the FEC's footnote gives "Second round results" equal to the Clerk's; 2024 beside a "Continuing Ballots" line equal to the finalists' sum. Maine's 2nd in 2020 is not declared: the FEC's footnote there gives rounds for a primary only, and the Clerk prints the general's two candidates alone — a majority on the first count. Alaska (2022, 2024) at its first choices, with no note: for 2022 the FEC's footnote says "Ranked Choice Voting for General Election. Votes shown in General Column are the official 1st Choice ...", its general column gives the Clerk's figures party by party (the tool holds them), and its rounds beside them end in a third round of Democratic 137,263 to Republican 112,471 — the race's deciding count, since the Clerk's first choices put the two Republicans together ahead. **Alaska's 2024 declaration rests on no saved page**: its rounds are on none (BILLED), so its deciding count stays the first choices, which name the winner of record.
7. **Same-party races (flag S)** — the deciding count's first two of one party: California's and Washington's top-two generals and Louisiana's open primary, 12, 5, 9, 7 and 7 races in 2016–2024. The other party may have stood and lost beside them (Louisiana's 3rd in 2022). Races where one major party stood alone are not flagged: `cands_r` and `cands_d` say so (the Clerk's printed candidates — none in the race printed without votes).
8. **The maps — which states' lines changed for each election, by the Census Bureau's pages.** Its Redistricting Data Program's Congressional Districts tabs (a page a Congress, all revised in place — "Page Last Revised - August 28, 2026") name the states that "delineated new boundaries" for each election cycle, all others "no changes ... from the [previous] cycle": 2016 Florida, Minnesota, North Carolina, Virginia; 2018 Colorado, Minnesota, Pennsylvania (Minnesota's block splits, the 115th's and 116th's tabs say, "were cosmetic in nature" and moved no population — the tool takes the tab's list as it stands); 2020 North Carolina alone (the 117th tab: "the only state that had changes ... between the 116th and 117th Congresses" — not collected by the Bureau, so no 117th tabulation exists; the geography page names the same state, which the tool holds); 2022 every state but the six with one seat (the geography page, "All states established new congressional districts in 2022, with the exception of the six single-member states", held by the tool to the 2020 census's one-seat states; 13 states changed seat counts); 2024 Alabama, Georgia, Louisiana, New York, North Carolina; 2026 Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, Tennessee, Texas, Utah — with the page's note that "On September 3, 2026, the Missouri Supreme Court ruled that the state's congressional district plan redrawn for the 2026 elections cannot be used", a referendum to come on the November 2026 ballot, after the Bureau had collected the plan. That note is dated after the page's own revision stamp; the note itself misprints the plan's signing as "September 2005". The 118th's tab names no state whose lines changed (it names Colorado's split block, Arkansas's corrected boundaries and Connecticut's planning regions). A House district's number is a label, not a place: across a change of lines no House district is paired with its namesake (US-4's presidential districts of Maine and Nebraska are the declared exception, there). The instruments read for the changes this item's cycles meet:
   - **2020, North Carolina:** Session Law 2019-249 (House Bill 1029), "AN ACT TO REALIGN THE CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICTS", "ratified this the 15th day of November, 2019" (`wayback_ncleg_SL2019-249_20200430.pdf`; ncleg.gov refuses this machine);
   - **2024, Alabama:** the "2023 Court Ordered Congressional Plan", named on the Legislature's reapportionment page beside the "2023 Legislatively Enacted Congressional Plan (Act 2023-563 2nd Sp Sess)" (`alison_reapportionment_block_tract.html`; the page says no more of either); the Supreme Court denied the Secretary of State's application for a stay of the district court's order on 26 September 2023 (docket 23A231, N.D. Ala. 2:21-cv-1291 and 1530, `supremecourt_docket_23a231.html`). The order's own date is BILLED (PACER);
   - **2024, Georgia:** named by the Census tab only; the enacting act and its date are BILLED (the Legislature's API answers 401, the Secretary of State 403);
   - **2024, Louisiana:** Act No. 2 of the 2024 First Extraordinary Session (Senate Bill 8), "Signed by the Governor. Becomes Act No. 2." on 01/22 (`legis_la_SB8_2024_1ES.html`); on 15 May 2024 the Supreme Court stayed "the April 30, 2024 order of the United States District Court for the Western District of Louisiana, case No. 3:24-cv-00122" (Landry v. Callais, docket 23A1002, `supremecourt_docket_23a1002.html` - the docket does not say what the order held; that the Act's map was the one used in 2024 rests on the Census tab's 2024 list, not on a saved instrument);
   - **2024, New York:** A.9310-A, substituted by S.8653-A, "SIGNED CHAP.92" on 02/28/2024 (`nyassembly_A09310_2023.html`);
   - **2024, North Carolina:** Session Law 2023-145, named by the State Board of Elections' file list ("SL 2023-145 US_Congress - Shapefile.zip", `ncsbe_uscongress_shapefiles_list.xml`) — that it is the 2024 plan rests on its number and the Census tab's 2024 list, as the list says nothing of which election each plan served; its text and date are BILLED (ncleg.gov 403; the Internet Archive's capture answered 429).

   The 2016, 2018, 2022 and 2026 instruments were not read: the Census pages date each change by election, which is what the measurement uses. The Bureau's block equivalency files (which block lies in which district) were not saved — they count blocks, not people, so they could not carry a vote onto new lines.
9. **`[HH-DIV]`** is read from the executive group's copy (`raw/executive/history_house_party_divisions.html`; `raw/records/` holds the same page fetched a day apart — the bytes differ only in request-time tokens). Its rows give each Congress's seats, Democrats, Republicans and others; a footnote mark stands after the seats where there is one (5 for the 116th, 6 for the 117th); the 119th's row carries no link.
10. **The FEC's sheets (2016–2022).** The FEC marks its general-election winner "W" ("W*" a runoff's winner) on every ballot line of his — a fusion winner's minor lines among them — so the tool takes the winner's party as the Republican or Democratic line among his marked lines, and requires those lines to be one FEC id. Its columns are read by their headings ("GE WINNER INDICATOR", the state, district, party, FEC id, general-vote and footnote columns); unexpired-term rows are read apart. Every district agrees with the Clerk's winner by party but one, declared and held to what it says: Indiana's 2nd in 2022, where the FEC marks no winner on the full term and the Clerk's winner on the unexpired term. North Carolina's 9th in 2018 has no winner in either: the FEC marks each of its general candidates "**" (the tool holds that, and that there is no W). The footnote column carries the ranked-choice counts (reading 6).
11. **The apportionment** is Table 1's, read from the PDFs (2010 for 2016–2020, 2020 for 2022–2026): the seats per state each election, and for each state the districts the Clerk must list.
