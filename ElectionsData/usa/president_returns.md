# USA — the presidential returns by jurisdiction, 2012–2024, and the House vote by state, 2016–2024 [SOURCED] [PROVISIONAL]

Class: SOURCED (PS-6 US-3, `COMPLETED.md` §786; the US-3 sourcing pass and this session, 2026-10-05; the House vote and 2024's candidates, US-5, §788, 2026-10-06). `[PROVISIONAL]` until a second session re-verifies (R-K9) — for every parsed figure that is a re-run of the tool over the saved pages, for the READ rows a second reading of the certificates. **One reading is open for Elias** (reading 3: the electors who broke their pledge).

**What this is.** The four presidential elections of record by jurisdiction, the electors in force each year, and Maine's and Nebraska's congressional districts — generated by `Tools/us_returns_prep.pl` from pages saved byte for byte under `raw/` (each group's `fetch_log.txt` carries every URL as reached after redirects, the UTC time and the final HTTP status, and the failed attempts the record names; `SHA256SUMS.txt` every file's digest; the register below names each page's publisher, basis and use):

- `president_by_year.csv` — a row a year: the nominees as NARA's results page names them, the electoral votes cast for each, and the votes each nominee's own electors cast for other persons;
- `president_by_state.csv` — a row a jurisdiction a year, 51 a year: the electors in force, the FEC's popular vote (the two nominees, all others together, the total), the electoral votes as cast and to whom the others went;
- `president_by_district.csv` — a row a congressional district of Maine and Nebraska a year: the two nominees' votes and the basis (`canvass`, or `READ`);
- `president_by_candidate.csv` (US-5) — a row a candidate column of the FEC's 2024 workbook: its national vote, the nominees marked by ticket (reading 14);
- `house_by_state.csv` (US-5) — a row a state a year, 50 a year, 2016–2024: the House vote by the Clerk of the House's recapitulation, each race as the Clerk prints it, each party's own ballot line, the other columns together, the Clerk's total (reading 13);
- `Assets/Scripts/Elections/Generated/UsPresidentialReturns.cs` — the CSVs as C# literals, each CSV's SHA-256, and the SHA-256 of every page the run read or relied on (`RawSources`). `GeneratedCatalogCheck` (the cheap bar) re-hashes the pages `RawSources` lists and the READ transcription, re-reads the CSVs against the catalog figure by figure, and runs `ElectoralCollege.FromCatalog` through the statute's allocator over each year against the record's split; `SeatAllocationBacktest` (the elections-backtests launch) prints each year's college beside its winner-take-all counterfactual and asserts it. The register's other pages are held by their groups' `SHA256SUMS.txt` (`sha256sum -c`), as every raw page is.

One input is not a parse: `maine_districts_read_2012_2016.tsv`, Maine's districts in 2012 and 2016, READ by eye from the Governor's certificates (reading 1) — and held by the tool to those certificates' own text layer. `ElectionsData/.gitattributes` keeps the CSVs and the transcription out of line-ending conversion, as it keeps the raw pages: a checkout gives back the bytes the catalog's digests were taken over.

## What the tool proves

It writes nothing and dies on any mismatch, naming every mismatch it has found — a page off its digest, or an input it cannot read, stops it at that point, and the mismatches found before it are named with it — unless all of these hold (the full list heads the tool):

- every page it reads is the bytes of its group's `SHA256SUMS.txt` line;
- each census's Table 1 apportions 435 Representatives over 50 states; each year's electors total 538 and equal NARA's per jurisdiction (and, in 2024, the FEC workbook's own ELECTORAL VOTES column);
- each Table 2's state rows sum to its Total row and each state's parts to its total; each row of the 2024 workbook's candidate columns sums to its total; a Total-row cell the tool reads that a formula fills answers to a typed national figure (reading 7);
- each jurisdiction's electoral votes as cast are NARA's; every elector is accounted for; the votes for other persons are named alike, person and count, by NARA's notes and the FEC's;
- each nominee's surname in an FEC heading is NARA's nominee's (one declared exception, reading 6);
- Nebraska's districts, and Maine's in 2020 and 2024, sum exactly to the FEC's state figures for both nominees; every Maine 2020 and 2024 leader, by district and statewide, holds more than half of the workbook's total ballots cast (TBC), each TBC is the sum of its candidate, Others and Blank columns, and the districts' TBC less their Blank is the FEC's typed state total (reading 8);
- Maine's READ districts of 2012 and 2016 are the certificate's own text layer, figure for figure, district for district and page for page, each slate's at-large figure the FEC's state figure and the overseas count the certificate's footnote — every cell READ once — and they never exceed the state nor fall short of it by more than the overseas votes; a text layer without page breaks is named as the extractor's, not the transcription's;
- where NARA's notes name which ticket carried which district (every state that split: Maine 2016, 2020 and 2024, Nebraska 2020 and 2024), each district's figures give it to that ticket and every district against its state is named; where there is no note, no district went against its state;
- no jurisdiction or district is tied, and in none do all other candidates together reach the larger nominee (reading 8);
- the record's winners — every jurisdiction's electors to its plurality winner, Maine's and Nebraska's districts to theirs — give NARA's table exactly, jurisdiction by jurisdiction and in total;
- `state_ev_2024.csv` (the 2024 table typed before these pages were saved) holds each jurisdiction once, each row as the 2024 rows give it, under its own heading;
- (US-5) each candidate column of the 2024 workbook: its typed Total-row figure is its state rows' sum, and the columns' national figures sum to TOTAL VOTES;
- (US-5) each year's Clerk's House recapitulation is on one page, titled for that year's election, headed by nine columns ending Total — Republican and Democratic read by their names, since the order differs from year to year — every state row's columns summing to its own Total, every state once, every column of the states summing to the Total row;
- (US-5) each FEC House table (2016–2022) is titled for its year, carries GENERAL ELECTION over Democratic, Republican and Other in columns E–G, and its rows sum to its typed Total row; every state once; and the two publications agree exactly on at least 35 of the 50 states (reading 13);
- (US-5) 2024's House line (Republican, Democratic, the total) and the three presidential rows of `returns_2024.md` — typed before these pages were saved, and read back against them on 2026-10-05 — are the pages' figures;
- no perl warning arises during the checks, and no name bound for a C# literal holds a quote or backslash. All six outputs are built and tested before the first is written.

On 2026-10-05 it passed, and a copy of its inputs reproduces the repository's bytes. **`Tools/us_returns_mutations.sh`** (committed; run it after any change to the tool or its pages) proves the failure paths on such a copy in a temporary directory: each case must stop the run, write nothing and print the exact mismatch it means, so a mutation that lands elsewhere cannot count. ## Readings

1. **Maine 2012 and 2016 by district — READ, and held to the certificate's text layer.** The Secretary of State published no district table for either year (`raw/district/maine_sos_previous_election_results_2012.html`, `maine_sos_election_results_2016-2017.html`; the 2012 and 2016 workbooks carry no district column). The figures stand only on the Governor's certificates, saved from NARA, whose text layer is OCR. They were READ by eye from the page images — 2012 by three readers (the sourcing agent, its verifier, and this session from the page JPEGs inside the PDF), 2016 by two (its page images are mixed-raster layers) — and the tool holds every READ figure to the text layer (`pdftotext -raw`, xpdf 4.06, the version the run prints): each slate's headings First, Second, At-Large, At-Large and its four figures in that order, split digit groups joined; the at-large figures the FEC's state figure, so the right slate was read; the cited page the figure's page, counted from the extractor's page breaks. The two readings agree throughout. The overseas votes "not tallied according to a Congressional district" are in the at-large totals only — the certificates' footnote gives the count, which the tool takes from the text layer too — so the districts fall short of the state by no more than that. The 2012 certificate NARA serves, and the one these rows stand on, is the Governor's *amended* certificate, given 20 December 2012; the original was not saved and is not read.
2. **2016's split as the electors were appointed — DERIVED from NARA's own cells.** NARA prints 304 – 227 as cast. Its table carries an "Other" column under each nominee: Texas's two sit under Trump's, Hawaii's one and Washington's four under Clinton's. Each nominee's electors as appointed are the nominee's column plus the "Other" column beside it — two printed cells added, per jurisdiction — and the record's winners give exactly that, jurisdiction by jurisdiction. In 2012, 2020 and 2024 there are no "Other" columns and the split is NARA's printed one.
3. **2016's seven votes for other persons — named by recipient; three of the electors named by the record. OPEN for Elias.** *PROVISIONAL (Elias's ruling G5, §797): built as read - the three named, the four recorded as unnamed; reversible, no game value moves.* NARA's notes (Hawaii: Bernie Sanders 1; Texas: Ron Paul 1, John Kasich 1; Washington: Colin Powell 3, Faith Spotted Eagle 1) and the FEC's Table 2 notes name the same persons and counts; the tool holds the one to the other. The three Certificates of Vote (saved) list the same persons and say how the electors voted — Hawaii "by distinct ballots", Texas "by individual ballot", Washington "voted, by ballot" — so no certificate ties a vote to an elector. The Supreme Court's opinion in *Chiafalo v. All three pledged to support Hillary Clinton in the Electoral College", and "the three Electors voted for Colin Powell for President" (slip op. 6); it adds that "Only seven electors across the Nation cast faithless votes" (slip op. 6–7). No saved official record names who cast the other four — Washington's for Faith Spotted Eagle, Texas's two, Hawaii's one. 4. **The electors in force — DERIVED from the apportionment, held to two printed sources.** 3 U.S.C. 3 counts them as the Senators and Representatives "to which the several States are by law entitled at the time when the President and Vice President to be chosen come into office"; 2 U.S.C. 2a(b) entitles a census's apportionment "in the Eighty-third Congress and in each Congress thereafter" for the 1950 census — the Congress elected two years after it. So 2012–2020 take the 2010 census's Representatives and 2024 the 2020 census's, plus two Senators each; the District of Columbia three (the Twenty-third Amendment, `raw/executive/archives_amendments_11-27.html`). The tool holds the result to NARA's per-jurisdiction column every year and, in 2024, to the FEC workbook's ELECTORAL VOTES column.
5. **The 2024 candidate-to-party key — NARA's nominees, held to the workbook's own labels.** The plan expected the workbook to carry no party labels; its electoral-vote columns do ("ELECTORAL VOTE: TRUMP (R)", "ELECTORAL VOTE: HARRIS (D)"), its vote columns only surnames. The tool takes the surnames from NARA's nominees, finds both forms, and the run dies unless the workbook's labels name the same party.
6. **The FEC's misprint.** Federal Elections 2020's Table 2 heads Trump's two columns "Trunp (R)". Declared to the tool as the one exception, so a second misprint still fails.
7. **"All others" and the national totals.** 2012–2020: the FEC's own "All Others" column. 2024: every column but the two nominees' — Nevada's "None of These Candidates" and the scattered write-ins among them — since the workbook's total counts them, and the tool holds each row's columns to its total. 2012's Table 2 fills two of its Total row's cells by formula — "All Others" over its state rows, "Total Vote" over that row's own three vote cells — so neither is a national figure the FEC typed, and holding the state rows to them would prove only the reader: the tool holds 2012's state rows to Table 1 of the same workbook, which types both nominees' national votes and the national total, and refuses a formula in any Total-row cell it reads (2016's, 2020's and 2024's carry none).
8. **The plurality is the larger nominee — and Maine's ranked-choice count.** Each catalog row carries the two nominees' votes, the others pooled. In every jurisdiction and district the tool requires all other candidates together — for a district, the state's others together, which bound any one of them there — to fall short of the larger nominee, so comparing the two nominees is the plurality. Maine's presidential count is ranked-choice under the statute as saved (21-A §1(27-C)(D), §723-A(2) — current text, reading 11): a leader holding more than half of all ballots cast, blank and overvoted ballots counted, wins outright. For 2020 and 2024 the tool requires every leader, in each district and statewide, to hold more than half of the workbook's total ballots cast (TBC) — and holds each TBC to the sum of its candidate, Others and Blank columns, and the districts' TBC less Blank to the FEC's typed state total. **READING:** that TBC is the statute's denominator — there is no overvote column, and no saved page says whether "Blank" holds ballots overvoted at the first rank; on that reading the test is the statute's outright majority. Which ticket carried the state and each district is held independently by NARA's notes and table. Rounds are not modelled (`ElectoralCollege`'s scope). For 2012 and 2016 the READ rows carry no ballots-cast count, and none is needed: §723-A says in its subsection 6, whose tag names IB 2015, c. 3 alone, "This section applies to elections held on or after January 1, 2018", and the Secretary of State's own 2012 Electoral College page (reading 11) states the most-votes rule; the other wording in force then is not on disk (reading 11). The count's start is not dated from this: only the earliest bound is on disk.
9. **Nebraska's districts** are the first two-number "Total" line after each "Congressional District" heading in the canvass book's text (`pdftotext -raw`, xpdf 4.06; the match ignores case). The president's race comes first under each district's heading; the House races come later, under the same headings in some books and other headings in others, and the tool keeps the first reading for each district, so no later Total replaces the president's. The two numbers are read in the book's order, the Republican first; a swapped reading, or a House race's totals, cannot pass the exact sum against the FEC's state row. Where Nebraska split (2020, 2024), NARA's notes name which ticket carried which district, and the tool holds each district to its ticket — which binds the Second; NARA names the First and Third together, both the Republican's, so which of those two stands under which label rests on the book's headings alone. In 2012 and 2016 every district's label rests on the headings alone. The 2016 book is headed "REVISED OFFICIAL REPORT" and does not say what was revised; the 2012 PDF calls itself "Official Results of Nebraska General Election - November 6, 2012".
10. **Maine's 2020 and 2024 districts** are each district sheet's "CG n Total" row of the Secretary of State's workbook by district (printed both with and without the space — "CG 1 Total", "CG1 Total" — which the tool's match allows): the towns and the overseas (UOCAVA) votes within it. They sum exactly to the state, and NARA's notes name the district each ticket carried.
11. **The statutes' dates.** Maine's statute pages are the Revisor's current text ("Data for this page extracted on 10/20/2025"); each section's history is on its page: §802, the district method — PL 1985, c. 161 only, unamended; §801 — PL 1985, c. 161; PL 2001, c. 516; PL 2019, c. 539; PL 2023, c. 628; PL 2025, c. 397; §803 — PL 1985, c. 161; PL 1989, c. 166; PL 2021, c. 273; PL 2023, c. 628 (repealed and replaced); PL 2025, c. 397; §805 — PL 1985, c. 161; PL 1989, c. 166; PL 2019, c. 539; PL 2023, c. 628; PL 2025, c. 397. The 2019 and 2021 chapters came after some of the four elections and the 2023 and 2025 ones after most or all, so for the amended parts the wording in force at a given election is not on disk. Two cautions: a "PL" year is the Legislature's citation year — a chapter may be enacted, or take effect, the next year, and the pages print no effective dates; and a subsection's tag names only its latest act (§801(2) and §805(2), the ranked-choice count, carry PL 2023, c. 628, though PL 2019, c. 539 amended both sections, and §723-A, earlier), so the count's start must not be dated from the tags. A contemporaneous statement of the rule as applied in 2012 is the Secretary of State's own 2012 Electoral College page (an Internet Archive capture of 2015-08-09). Nebraska's §32-1038 cites Laws 1994, LB 76 as its only source, so its saved wording governed all four elections; §32-710's sources run to Laws 2015, LB575. Both are Internet Archive captures: the Legislature's own host could not be reached — it refused connections in August (`district_method_2024.md`) and gave no connection on 2026-10-05 (curl status 000).
12. **NARA's 2020 and 2024 pages.** The tool reads the copies saved on 2026-09-25 (`raw/executive/`). The sourcing pass fetched both again on 2026-10-05 (kept out of tree): the bytes differ only in Drupal's per-request theme token and CSS cache tokens — the tables and notes are identical.
13. **The House series — the Clerk's recapitulation, one publication every year (DECLARED for US-5, `docs/specs/USA_STAGE_PLAN.md`; put to Elias, since it departs from the item's text).** The Clerk of the House's election statistics (*of the Presidential and Congressional Election* in a presidential year, *of the Congressional Election* in a midterm) print, for each election, a "Recapitulation of Votes Cast for United States Representatives": a row a state (50 — the Delegates and the Resident Commissioner are printed apart), a column a ballot line, a Total row. The header's order is the year's own: 2018 and 2020 put Democratic first, 2016, 2022 and 2024 Republican, and 2024 Independent before Libertarian — so the tool reads the columns by their names. A party's vote is its own ballot line: a candidate on several lines counts under each line's column (New York's fusion lines among them). **How the Clerk prints a race** (corrected at US-11, §790, which read every district - this reading first said "each race's final count", true of neither Louisiana nor Alaska; `house_districts.md`): in Louisiana's runoff districts the two finalists' December runoff figures, each carrying its footnote mark, beside the November figures of the candidates eliminated in November, the state's row adding them together (2016: the 3rd and 4th districts, its footnotes 2 and 3 - footnote 1 is the Senate race's; 2020: the 5th; each footnote gives the finalists' November figures); Maine's finalists at their last ranked-choice round (2022's footnote: "from round 2", with the eliminated Independent's and the write-ins' first-round figures beside them; 2018's figures are the final round the FEC's own footnote gives; 2024's stand beside a "Continuing Ballots" line equal to their sum); Alaska's first choices, with no note (2022 and 2024; for 2022 the FEC's workbook gives the same figures as its general vote, its ranked-choice rounds beside them); and nothing for North Carolina's 9th district in 2018 ("ordered a new election in this Congressional District"). The "Other Parties" column ("See each State's recapitulation table to determine the parties included in this category") and the Total also hold the non-votes some states report — blank, over and under votes — and Maine's ranked-choice lines, which count some ballots again (2022's 2nd district carries an "Exhausted Ballot" line larger than the district's whole first-round vote; 2024's carries "Continuing Ballots"): a party's share of the Total is not its share of the votes cast for candidates; the two-party split is untouched by them. A candidate unopposed whose name was not printed receives no votes (both publications say so).
