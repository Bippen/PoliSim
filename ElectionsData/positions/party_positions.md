### Scale note
Quoted from the CHES 2024 codebook (`CHES.2024.Codebook.pdf`):
- **lrgen**: "please tick the box that best describes each party's OVERALL IDEOLOGY on a scale ranging from 0 (extreme left) to 10 (extreme right)."
- **lrecon**: "STANCE ON ECONOMIC ISSUES such as privatization, taxes, regulation, government spending, and the welfare state. Parties on the economic left want government to play an active role in the economy. Those on the economic right want a reduced role for government. 0 = extreme left … 10 = extreme right."
- **galtan**: "'LIBERTARIAN' OR 'POSTMATERIALIST' parties favor expanded personal freedoms, for example, abortion rights, same-sex marriage, and democratic participation. 'TRADITIONAL' OR 'AUTHORITARIAN' parties reject these ideas in favor of order, tradition, and stability … 0 = libertarian/postmaterialist … 10 = traditional/authoritarian."
- **eu_position**: "GENERAL POSITION ON EUROPEAN INTEGRATION that the party leadership took during 2024 — 1 = strongly opposed … 4 = neutral … 7 = strongly in favor." **Note: 1–7, not 0–10.**
- GPS-2019 (codebook): **V4** economic left-right, "0. Left (pro-state) … 10. Right (pro-market)"; **V6** "0. Very liberal 10. Very conservative" with wording nearly identical to CHES galtan.

### Caveats
- **USA**: CHES-USA exists as a project but has published no data ("Coming soon"). The Global Party Survey 2019 (Norris) is the named substitute — expert survey, CHES-comparable 0–10 scales (its codebook documents r = 0.85–0.94 correlations with CHES on these items) — but its vintage is Nov–Dec 2019, pre-2020; no general left-right or EU item (marked [UNCONFIRMED]/n-a). GPS also gives US immigration positions (V10, 0 = liberal, 10 = restrictive): Democrats 2.73, Republicans 8.93.
- **Party coverage gaps**: Italia Viva has no row in CHES 2024 (Azione covered separately); AVS covered only via its components SI and EV; Poland's KO is represented by PO; Germany's Linke appears as "DL". Positions were assessed for 2024 regardless of each country's election year.
- A CHES 1999–2024 trend file exists (`1999-2024_CHES_dataset_meansV2.csv`) if longitudinal values are wanted; the 2024 cross-section is the most recent round.
